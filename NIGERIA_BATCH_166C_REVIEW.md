# Research batch 166c — Kaduna: peoples

Researched 2026-10-07. Third of three Kaduna language and people batches. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **36 new peoples:** Atyap, Bajju, Agworok, Asholio, Takad, Atyecarak, Fantswam, Ham, Gwong, Anghan, Bakulu, Nikyob, Nindem, Ayu, Numana, Kanufi, Ningeshe, Idun, Atsam, Abin, Abisi, Akurmi, Amala, Anu, Avori, Koonu, Ngmgbang, Dungi, Agbiri, Aniragu, Arumaruma, Avono, Anaseni, Tumi, Kuzamani, Kaivi.
- **Names:** each record uses the own name Wikipedia gives; the Hausa name is kept as an exonym (e.g. Atyap/Kataf, Bajju/Kaje, Ham/Jaba, Gwong/Kagoma, Bakulu/Ikulu, Akurmi/Kurama).
- **Existing peoples linked:** Hausa, Fulani, Gbagyi, Adara, Koro (with the names Bhazar and Batinor), Mada, Amo, Ninzam (with the name Ninzo), Nyankpa (with Nyenkpa and Yeskwa, and now linked to its language), Berom, Irigwe, Zaar.
- **LGAs with peoples linked:** 17 of 23 (chikun, igabi, ikara, jaba, jema-a, kachia, kaduna-south, kagarko, kajuru, kaura, kauru, kubau, lere, makarfi, sanga, zangon-kataf, zaria).
- **LGA links** come from Wikipedia's LGA pages; *well documented* where the Atlas also places the language there, *reported* otherwise.

## The records

**Atyap.** The Atyap, called Kataf or Katab by the Hausa, are a people of southern Kaduna State, named among its peoples by the federal government's state profile and Wikipedia's list of the ethnic groups of Kaduna State. They speak the Tyap cluster of Central Plateau languages (Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages, 2020). Wikipedia names them among the peoples of Zangon Kataf, Kaura, Jema'a, Kauru and Kaduna South LGAs. Wikipedia describes the Atyap, Bajju, Agworok, Asholio, Takad, Atyecarak and Fantswam as one ethno-linguistic group (Nerzit or Nenzit); Zangon Kataf is a town in the chiefdom of the Atyap.

**Bajju.** The Bajju, called Kaje by the Hausa, are a people of southern Kaduna State, named among its peoples by the federal government's state profile and Wikipedia's list of the ethnic groups of Kaduna State. They speak Jju, a Central Plateau language (Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages, 2020). Wikipedia names them among the peoples of Zangon Kataf, Jema'a, Kachia and Kaduna South LGAs.

**Agworok.** The Agworok, called Kagoro by the Hausa, are a people of southern Kaduna State, named among its peoples by the federal government's state profile and Wikipedia's list of the ethnic groups of Kaduna State. ## Research gaps

- **Kaduna: Atuku.** The federal profile and Wikipedia name the Atuku (in Jema'a and the Atyap group); their language is not identified in a source read, so no record is made.
- **Kaduna: peoples of Sanga and Lere not yet recorded.** Wikipedia names the Nandu, Ningon, Tari, Ninte and Nungu of Sanga and the Azelle of Lere; their languages are not yet recorded for Kaduna, so no records are made.
- **Kaduna: northern LGAs.** No source read names the peoples of Birnin Gwari, Giwa, Kudan, Sabon Gari, Soba or Kaduna North; they are left for the LGA profiles.
- **Kaduna: migrant and other-state groups.** Wikipedia's list includes the Kanuri and Marghi (from Borno) and the Zaar (Bauchi); they are not linked at state level. Settler groups named on LGA pages (Igbo, Yoruba, Idoma, Igala, Nupe) are not linked.
- **Kaduna: Koro.** The Koro of Kagarko and Kachia (Bhazar, Batinor; Ashe and Tinɔr–Myamya speakers) are linked to the existing Koro record, which also covers the Koro of Niger State and the FCT; whether they should be separate records is for review.
