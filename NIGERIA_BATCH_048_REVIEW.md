## The 17 descriptions

**Barkin Ladi** (114 words). Barkin Ladi is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Barkin Ladi (according to the state government and Statoids). The state government places it about 54 km south of Jos. It had a population of 175,267 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 1,032 km², the state government as about 1,312.5 km². Wikipedia gives Barakin Ladi as another form of the name and names the Plateau State Polytechnic among its institutions. Riyom LGA was carved out of the old Barkin Ladi LGA in 1996. Blench's Atlas places the Berom, Aten, Fɨran, Mwaghavul and Pyam languages and the southern dialect of Izere here.

**Bassa** (133 words). Bassa is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Bassa (according to the state government and Statoids). The state government places it about 30 km north-west of Jos. It had a population of 186,859 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 1,743 km², the state government as about 1,776 km². Wikipedia describes it as the northern LGA of the state, bordering Kaduna and Bauchi states, with towns such as Miango and Jengre, and as the home of the Nigerian Army's 3 Division at the Maxwell Khobe Cantonment. INEC's office for the LGA is at Rukuba. Blench's Atlas places here many small Plateau and Kainji languages, among them Che (Rukuba), Rigwe (Irigwe), Iguta, Janji, Jere, Lemoro, Map, Cara and the highly endangered Zora.

**Bokkos** (97 words). Bokkos is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Bokkos (according to the state government and Statoids). The state government places it about 77 km south of Jos. It had a population of 178,454 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 1,682 km², the state government as about 3,053 km². Three stone causeways in the LGA, at Batura, Forof and Tading, are declared national monuments of Nigeria (Nos. 61–63), the only ones in Plateau State. Blench's Atlas places the Ron (Run) and Kulere languages here.

**Jos East** (91 words). Jos East is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Angware (according to the state government, Statoids and Wikipedia). The state government places it about 35 km east of Jos. It had a population of 85,602 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 1,020 km², the state government as about 2,540 km². INEC's office for the LGA is also at Angware. Wikipedia names the Afizere as its predominant people, and Blench's Atlas places five villages of the Tunzu language here.

**Jos North** (123 words). Jos North is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Jos (according to the state government, Statoids and Wikipedia). Jos is the state capital. It had a population of 429,300 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 291 km², the state government as about 650 km². By Statoids' figures it is the smallest LGA of the state by area, and at the 2006 census it was the most populous. Wikipedia describes it as the commercial centre of the state and places in it the Gbong Gwom Jos palace at Jishe, the University of Jos and its teaching hospital, and the headquarters of the ECWA and COCIN churches; it names the Anaguta among its peoples.

**Jos South** (105 words). Jos South is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Bukuru (according to the state government, Statoids and Wikipedia). The state government places it about 15 km south of Jos. It had a population of 306,716 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 510 km². INEC's office for the LGA is at Dadin Kowa. Wikipedia places the new Government House at Rayfield in the LGA and names among its institutions the National Institute for Policy and Strategic Studies at Kuru, Karl Kumm University, the Theological College of Northern Nigeria and the College of Health at Vom.

**Kanam** (96 words). Kanam is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Dengi (according to the state government, Statoids and Wikipedia). The state government places it about 191 km south-west of Jos. It had a population of 165,898 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 2,600 km², the state government as about 2,788.4 km². INEC's office for the LGA is also at Dengi. Wikipedia names Boghom and Jarawa as languages of the LGA, and Blench's Atlas places here Boghom, Ngas and the Jar-cluster languages Mbat and Doori.

**Kanke** (104 words). Kanke is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Kwal (according to the state government, Wikipedia and INEC). The state government places it about 152 km south-east of Jos. It had a population of 121,424 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 926 km², the state government as about 1,000 km². Statoids spells the headquarters Kwali. Wikipedia describes the population as mostly Ngas and records that the former head of state Yakubu Gowon is from the LGA. The Ngolong Ngas is described as the traditional ruler of the Ngas of Pankshin and Kanke LGAs.

**Langtang North** (119 words). Langtang North is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Langtang (according to the state government and Wikipedia). The state government places it about 194 km south-east of Jos. It had a population of 140,643 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 1,188 km², the state government as about 2,440 km². INEC's office for the LGA is in Langtang town. Statoids gives Mabudi, which the state government, Wikipedia and INEC place in Langtang South, so Statoids appears to swap the two Langtangs. The palace of the Ponzhi Tarok is in the LGA, and the Tarok festival Ilum Otarok was held at the Langtang North Mini Stadium in 2025.

**Langtang South** (91 words). Langtang South is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Mabudi (according to the state government, Wikipedia and INEC). The state government places it about 237 km south-east of Jos. It had a population of 106,305 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 838 km², the state government as about 1,250 km². Statoids gives Langtang as the headquarters, apparently swapping the two Langtangs. Wikipedia records a Chief Magistrate Court at Mabudi and area courts at Sabon Gida, Dadin Kowa and Magama.

**Mangu** (114 words). Mangu is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Mangu (according to the state government and Statoids). The state government places it about 77 km south-east of Jos. It had a population of 294,931 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 1,653 km², the state government as about 1,587.5 km². It is the paramount seat of the Mwaghavul, whose ruler, the Mishkaham Mwaghavul, has chaired the Mangu Traditional Council. Blench's Atlas places here more languages than in any other Plateau LGA, among them Mwaghavul, Fyer, Tambas, Sha, Shagawu, Mundat, Cakfem–Mushere, Miship, Bo-Rukul, Horom and members of the Pan and Vaghat clusters.

**Mikang** (78 words). Mikang is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Tunkus (according to the state government, Statoids and Wikipedia). The state government places it about 240 km south-east of Jos. It had a population of 97,411 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 739 km², the state government as about 630 km². INEC's office for the LGA is also at Tunkus. Wikipedia's Ethnologue table lists Montol, Tarok and Youm there.

**Pankshin** (96 words). Pankshin is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Pankshin (according to the state government and Statoids). The state government places it about 120 km south-east of Jos. It had a population of 191,685 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 1,524 km², the state government as about 1,334 km². Wikipedia describes Pankshin as a farming and trading centre with a weekly Monday market, and mentions Pusdung as the annual cultural festival of the Ngas. Blench's Atlas places the Ngas, Tal and Pe languages here.

**Qua'an Pan** (102 words). Qua'an Pan is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Baap (according to the state government, Statoids and Wikipedia). The state government places it about 298 km south-east of Jos. It had a population of 196,929 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 2,478 km², the state government as about 2,688 km². INEC's office for the LGA is at Doemak. The LGA is the seat of the Long Pan, ruler of the Pan Chiefdom, a first-class stool; Blench's Atlas places here the Tèŋ and Shindai members of the Pan (Kofyar) cluster.

**Riyom** (91 words). Riyom is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Riyom (according to the state government, Statoids and INEC). The state government places it about 51 km south-west of Jos. It had a population of 131,557 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 807 km². According to Wikipedia, Riyom was carved out of the old Barkin Ladi LGA on 1 October 1996, is predominantly Berom, borders Kaduna and Nasarawa states, and is known for the Riyom rock formation, an emblem of the state.

**Shendam** (106 words). Shendam is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Shendam (according to the state government and Statoids). The state government places it about 254 km south-east of Jos. It had a population of 208,017 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 2,477 km², the state government as about 2,437 km². Wikipedia describes the LGA as bordering Ibi (Taraba State) to the south, Qua'an Pan to the east, Pankshin to the north and Mikang to the west, with Goemai and other West Chadic languages spoken. Shendam is the seat of the Long Goemai, a first-class stool.

**Wase** (113 words). Wase is a local government area of Plateau State with its headquarters at Wase (according to the state government, Statoids and INEC). The state government places it about 216 km south-west of Jos. It had a population of 161,714 at the 2006 census (Statoids). Statoids gives its area as about 5,031 km², the state government as about 4,306 km². It is the largest LGA of the state by area. Wikipedia places the town about 200 km south-east of Jos, bordering Kanam to the north, Taraba State to the south and east and Langtang North to the west. Wase is the seat of the Wase Emirate, founded between 1817 and 1820.

## Research gaps

- **Plateau: Langtang headquarters in Statoids.** Statoids gives Langtang North -> Mabudi and Langtang South -> Langtang; Wikipedia and INEC (LGA council secretariat at Mabudi) give the reverse, which the archive follows.
- **Plateau: Jos North population.** Wikipedia prints '729,300 at the 2006 census'; Statoids gives 429,300. The census publication should be checked; Statoids' figure is recorded.
- **Plateau: creation dates of LGAs.** Only Riyom's (1 October 1996, from Barkin Ladi) was found. An official list is needed, including when Kanke, Mikang, Qua'an Pan and the Jos and Langtang divisions were created.
- **Plateau: LGA areas.** Statoids and the state government's 2022 booklet give different areas for most LGAs (e.g. Bokkos 1,682 and 3,053 km²; Jos East 1,020 and 2,540 km²); both are recorded. The booklet gives no area for Jos South or Riyom.
- **Plateau: INEC offices outside the headquarters.** INEC's office for Bassa is at Rukuba, for Qua'an Pan at Doemak and for Jos South at Dadin Kowa; the headquarters are Bassa, Ba'ap and Bukuru (state government, Statoids, Wikipedia).
