# Research batch 038b — Taraba: the peoples

Researched 2026-09-26. Created in review; published only after your approval.

**Important:** unlike Nasarawa, **no official list of Taraba's peoples** could be found: not on the state government's site (live or archived), not in its investment guide or plan, and not on the NIPC page. The people records therefore rest on Wikipedia's list of the state's main groups, checked against Blench's Atlas, and are graded *well documented* at most, not *verified*.

- **11 new people records:** Mumuye, Mambila, Kuteb, Chamba, Ichen, Jibu, Ndoola, Tigon, Jenjo, Karimjo, Yandang. Each is linked to Taraba and to its language(s).
- **59 people–LGA links** from Ethnologue's language table (via Wikipedia): *well documented* where the Atlas agrees, *reported* otherwise. All are presence only.
- **Existing records:** the Jukun now 'speak' Wapan and Jukun Takum (as well as Wannu). The Jukun, Tiv, Fulani and Hausa are linked to the LGAs where Ethnologue lists their languages, and the Fulani and Hausa to Taraba State.
- **33 new language–LGA links** from Ethnologue, mostly for **today's** LGAs the Atlas does not use (Ussa, Donga, Kurmi, Lau, Ibi, Gassol, Ardo Kola). An example: Kuteb in Ussa.
- The Chamba record links to **both** Chamba languages (Samba Daka and Samba Leko), which the Atlas says are unrelated.
- All records are short (noindex; sitemap unchanged).

## The people records

**Mumuye.** The Mumuye are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them mainly in the north of the state. Blench's Atlas records their language as a large cluster spoken in Jalingo, Zing and Yorro LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Ardo Kola, Bali, Jalingo, Karim Lamido, Yorro and Zing LGAs.

**Mambila.** The Mambila are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them mainly in the centre of the state. Other names include Mambilla. Blench's Atlas places their language on the Mambila Plateau of Sardauna LGA and in Cameroon, and gives Nɔr as their own name. Ethnologue lists their language in Sardauna LGA.

**Kuteb.** The Kuteb are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them mainly in the south of the state. Other names include Kutev, Kutep. Blench's Atlas places their language in Takum LGA (from which Ussa was later created) and in Cameroon. Ethnologue lists their language in Takum and Ussa LGAs.

**Chamba.** The Chamba are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them mainly in the south of the state. Other names include Samba. Blench's Atlas records two unrelated Chamba languages: Samba Daka (Chamba Daka, a Dakoid language) and Samba Leko (Chamba Leko, an Adamawa language). Wikipedia also names the Daka as a people of the centre of the state. Ethnologue lists their language in Bali, Donga, Gashaka and Takum LGAs.

**Ichen.** The Ichen are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them mainly in the south of the state. Other names include Icen, Itchen. Blench's Atlas calls their language Etkywan and places it in Takum and Sardauna LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Bali, Donga, Kurmi, Takum and Wukari LGAs.

**Jibu.** The Jibu are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them mainly in the centre of the state. Blench's Atlas places their language, a member of the Jukun cluster, in Gashaka LGA. Ethnologue lists their language in Bali and Gashaka LGAs.

**Ndoola.** The Ndoola are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them mainly in the centre of the state. Other names include Ndola, Ndoro. Blench's Atlas places their language in Sardauna and Gashaka LGAs and in one village in Cameroon. Ethnologue lists their language in Bali, Gashaka and Kurmi LGAs.

**Tigon.** The Tigon are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them mainly in the centre of the state. Other names include Tigong, Tigun. Blench's Atlas calls their language Mbembe Tigong and places it in Sardauna LGA, but mainly in Cameroon. Ethnologue lists their language in Kurmi LGA.

**Jenjo.** The Jenjo are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Dza, Jen. Blench's Atlas calls their language Dza (other names Jenjo, Jen) and places it along the Benue in Karim Lamido LGA and in Numan LGA, Adamawa State. Ethnologue lists their language in Ibi, Karim Lamido and Lau LGAs.

**Karimjo.** The Karimjo are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Karim. Blench's Atlas gives Karim as a name of the Como Karim language, spoken in Karim Lamido and Jalingo LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Karim Lamido LGA.

**Yandang.** The Yandang are one of the main peoples of Taraba State named by Wikipedia. Their language is not among the Taraba entries of Blench's Atlas, which places the Yendang language in Adamawa State. Ethnologue lists their language in Karim Lamido, Lau and Zing LGAs.

## Research gaps

- **No official list of Taraba's peoples.** The state government's site (live and archived), its investment guide and plan, and the NIPC page name none. A state or academic ethnographic source would raise the people records above 'well documented'.
- **Wurkum, Wurbo, Sho, Daka.** Named by Wikipedia; 'Wurkum' is an umbrella name the Atlas gives for Kulung, Piya and Kholok; 'Wurbo' is an Atlas cluster (Como Karim, Jiru, Shoo-Minda-Nye); Daka is a Chamba group. Not recorded as separate peoples.
- **The many small peoples of Karim Lamido, Lau and the Mambila Plateau.** About 60 language communities are recorded as languages (batch 038) but not as peoples.
- **Who is indigenous where.** All LGA links are presence only (settlement status unknown).
- **Wikipedia's Ethnologue table.** Visibly edited ('Jibu (Jukun Kona)', mixed links). Unmapped names: Jiba, Duguri, Kwa, Pero, Kambu, Kpati, Lufu, Acha, Tarok, Nyong, Wurkun-Anphandi.
- **Yandang.** Named by Wikipedia and listed in Karim Lamido, Lau and Zing by Ethnologue; the Atlas's Yendang entry is in Adamawa State. No language link.
