# Research batch 166b — Kaduna: Plateau and other languages

Researched 2026-10-07. Second of three Kaduna language and people batches. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## The records

**Ajiya.** Ajiya is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which gives no classification; Glottolog places it in the Plateau languages (Kuturmi–Ajiya group). The Atlas places it in Kachia LGA, as it was before the later splits. Other names include Ajuli, Idon, Idong, Idon-Doka-Makyali. Glottolog lists it as Idon (idon1238).

**Ajuwa–Ajegha.** Ajuwa–Ajegha is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which gives no classification; no Glottolog languoid was found for it. The Atlas places it in Kajuru LGA. Other names include Ajuwa.

**Anib.** Anib is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Ninzic). The Atlas places it in Jema'a LGA, in two villages about 5 km west of Gimi, on the Akwanga road towards Kafanchan. Other names include Kanufi, Aninib, Karshi. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 2,000 (2006 estimate). The Atlas notes two forms, Kanufi I (locally Ákpúrkpòd) and Kanufi II (Ákob). Glottolog lists it as Kanufi.

**Ayu.** Ayu is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Ninzic, tentatively). The Atlas places it in Jema'a LGA, as it was before Sanga was created. Other names include Aya. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 2,642 (1934).

**Cori.** Cori is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which gives no classification; Glottolog places it in the Plateau languages (Hyamic). The Atlas places it in Jema'a LGA. Other names include Chori, Kyoli. Glottolog lists it as Kyoli (cori1240).

**Ekhwa.** Ekhwa is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which gives no classification; Glottolog places it in the Plateau languages (Northern Plateau). The Atlas places it in Kachia LGA; INEC has a ward Ankwa in Kachia LGA. Other names include Iku-Gora-Ankwa, ékhwá, Ahua, Ehwa. Glottolog lists it as Iku-Gora-Ankwa (ikug1238).

**Gbǝtsu.** Gbǝtsu is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Ninzic: Mada cluster). The Atlas places it in Jema'a LGA, in about six villages east of the road north of Akwanga. Other names include Katanza. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 5,000 (2008 estimate).

**Gwara.** Gwara is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which gives no classification; Glottolog places it in the Plateau languages (Koroic). The Atlas places it in Kagarko and Jaba LGAs. Other names include iGwara, Gora.

**Gyong.** Gyong is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Western: Gyongic). The Atlas places it in Jema'a LGA; Wikipedia and INEC list Kagoma (Gwong) among the subdivisions and wards of Jema'a LGA. Other names include Kagoma, Agoma, Gong, Gwong. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 6,250 (1934). Glottolog lists it as Kagoma.

**Hyam.** Hyam is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Hyamic). The Atlas places the cluster in Kachia and Jema'a LGAs, as they were before the later splits. Its members include Kwyeny, Yaat, Saik, Dzar, Hyam of Nok. Other names include Ham, Hum, Jaba. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 43,000. The Atlas records Scripture portions from 1923 and an alphabet chart (1999), and notes that 'Kwak', listed elsewhere as a language, is only a Hyam town name.

**Ikryo.** Ikryo is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau. The Atlas places it in Kachia LGA. Other names include ìkryó, West Kuturmi. With Obiro it is called West Kuturmi; Glottolog has a single 'Kuturmi' languoid (kutu1262) in its Kuturmi–Ajiya group, so no code is set.

**Jju.** Jju is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Central). The Atlas places it in Kachia and Jema'a LGAs, as they were before the later splits. Other names include Kәjju, Bajju, Baju, Kaje, Kajji. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 26,600 (1949); possibly 200,000 (1984). The Atlas records an official orthography, a New Testament (1983) and a literacy programme.

**Kamantan.** Kamantan is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Western: Gyongic). The Atlas places it in Kachia LGA, as it was before Zangon Kataf was created. Other names include Angan. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 3,600 (1949); 10,000 (1972).

**Ikulu.** Ikulu is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Northwestern). The Atlas places it in Kachia LGA, as it was before Zangon Kataf was created. Other names include Ikolu, Ankulu, Bekulu. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 6,000 (1949). The Atlas's head name is Kulu.

**Nincut.** Nincut is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Beromic: Berom). The Atlas places it in eight villages about 7 km north of Fadan Karshe, without naming the LGA. Other names include Aboro. Speaker figures in the Atlas: about 5,000 (2003 estimate). The Atlas notes that it is threatened by a switch to Hausa, and that it is treated as a language separate from Berom.

**Ninkyop–Nindem.** Ninkyop–Nindem is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Ninzic). The Atlas places the cluster in Jema'a LGA; Wikipedia lists Kaninkon among the subdivisions of Jema'a LGA. Its members include Ninkyop, Nindem. Other names include Kaninkon, Kaningkwom, Ninkyob. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 2,291 (1934, Ninkyop). Glottolog lists it as Kaningdon-Nindem.

**Ningye.** Ningye is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Ninzic). The Atlas places it in five villages along the Fadan Karshe–Akwanga road, directly north of Gwantu: Kobin, Akwankwan, Wambe, Ningeshen Kurmi and Ningeshen Sarki. Other names include Ningeshe. Speaker figures in the Atlas: fewer than 5,000 (2003).

**Ninka.** Ninka is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Ninzic). The Atlas places it in Sanga LGA. Other names include Aninka. Speaker figures in the Atlas: fewer than 5,000. The Atlas also gives Sanga as a name for it; the name is used too of the separate Sanga language and, the Atlas notes, mistakenly of the Numbu–Gbantu–Nunku cluster, so it is not stored as an alias here. Glottolog lists it as Aninka (Ninzic).

**Nyankpa.** Nyankpa is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Koro). The Atlas places it in Jema'a LGA and in Nasarawa State. Other names include Yeskwa, Yasgua, Nnaŋkpa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 13,000 (1973). The Atlas names Tattara as the 'standard' form of Yeskwa.

**Obiro.** Obiro is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Northwestern group). The Atlas places it in Kachia LGA, at Antara village. Other names include ìbìrò, West Kuturmi. With Ikryo it is called West Kuturmi; no Glottolog code is set.

**Sambe.** Sambe is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Alumic). The Atlas places it in Kaduna State without naming the LGA. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 2 (2005). The Atlas describes it as moribund, with some rememberers in 2005 but probably extinct by 2016; its speakers have shifted to Ninzo.

**Shakara.** Shakara is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Ndunic). The Atlas places it in a line of villages 7 km due west of Mayir, on the Fadan Karshe–Wamba road. Other names include ìShákárá, Tari. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 3,000 (2003 estimate). Its speakers also use Hausa (Atlas).

**Shamang.** Shamang is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Hyamic). The Atlas places it in Kachia and Jema'a LGAs, as they were before the later splits. Other names include Samban, Samang.

**Shang.** Shang is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Hyamic). The Atlas places it in Kachia and Jema'a LGAs, in two settlements, Kushampa A and B; Kushampa A is on the road between Kurmin Jibrin and Kubacha. Other names include Kushampa.

**Tinɔr–Myamya.** Tinɔr–Myamya is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Koro). The Atlas places it in Kagarko LGA. Other names include Begbere-Ejar, Koro Agwe, Agwere, Koro Makama. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 35,000 including Ashe (1972). Its peoples have no common name for themselves and refer to their villages; they share the ethnonym Uzar (plural Bazar; the language Ìzar, the origin of 'Ejar') with the Ashe. Ashe, Hyam and Gbagyi are nearby languages often spoken by the Tinɔr.

**Tyap.** Tyap is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Central). The Atlas places the cluster in Kachia, Saminaka and Jema'a LGAs, as they were before the later splits; Gworok (Kagoro), Atakar, Sholio (around Manchok) and Kafancan in Jema'a. Its members include Gworok, Atakar, Sholio, Kacicere, Kafancan. Other names include Kataf, Katab, Atyap, Atyab. Speaker figures in the Atlas: more than 130,000 (1990, Tyap); 9,300 (1949, Gworok); 5,700 (1949, Sholio). The Atlas records a New Testament in Gworok and a Bible translation in progress in Tyap. Glottolog lists Tyap (tyap1238), with Kagoro, Sholio and Kafanchan as separate languoids.

**Zhire.** Zhire is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Hyamic). The Atlas places it in Kachia and Jema'a LGAs, as they were before the later splits. Other names include Kenyi.

## Reported links

- ajiya → kajuru: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC has a ward Idon, with a polling unit at Makyali, in Kajuru LGA (Idon and Makyali are names the Atlas gives for the language).
- ayu → sanga: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC lists the polling unit Fadan Ayu I in Bokana ward of Sanga LGA.
- hyam → jaba: Placement not stated by the Atlas: Wikipedia (Kaduna State) calls the Ham the people of Jaba, and INEC has a ward Nok in Jaba LGA (Hyam of Nok is a member of the cluster).
- kamantan → zangon-kataf: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC has a ward Kamantan in Zangon Kataf LGA, and Wikipedia lists Kamantan among the towns of Zangon Kataf, which was created from Kachia LGA in 1989.
- kulu → zangon-kataf: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC has a ward Kamuru Ikulu North in Zangon Kataf LGA.
- nincut → sanga: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC has a ward Fadan Karshi in Sanga LGA; the Atlas places Nincut about 7 km north of Fadan Karshe.
- ningye → sanga: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC lists polling units at Kobin and Wambe, two of the five villages the Atlas names, in Nandu ward of Sanga LGA.
- sambe → sanga: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC lists the polling unit Sambe in Ninzam North ward of Sanga LGA.
- shakara → sanga: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC lists the polling unit Mayir in Ayu ward of Sanga LGA; the Atlas places Shakara 7 km due west of Mayir.
- shamang → jaba: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC has a ward Sambam in Jaba LGA, with a polling unit at Ungwan Samban Gida (Samban is the Atlas's other name for the language).
- shang → kagarko: Placement not stated by the Atlas: The Atlas places Kushampa A between Kurmin Jibrin and Kubacha; INEC lists both as wards of Kagarko LGA.
- tyap → zangon-kataf: Placement not stated by the Atlas: Wikipedia gives Zangon Kataf's Tyap name and calls Zangon Kataf a town in the chiefdom of the Atyap; the LGA was created from Kachia in 1989.
- tyap → kaura: Placement not stated by the Atlas: Wikipedia gives Kaura's Tyap name (Watyap) and lists Takad (Attakar), Manchok and Kagoro among its towns; the Atlas places Atakar, Sholio (around Manchok) and Gworok (Kagoro) in the old Jema'a LGA.
- zhire → kagarko: Placement not stated by the Atlas: INEC lists the polling unit Kenyi in Aribi ward of Kagarko LGA (Kenyi is the Atlas's other name for the language).

## Research gaps

- **Kaduna: pre-1989 LGAs.** The Atlas uses Jema'a before Kaura and Sanga were created, Kachia before Zangon Kataf and Chikun before Kajuru. Links to today's Jema'a and Kachia follow the Atlas; links to Kaura, Sanga, Zangon Kataf, Jaba, Kagarko and Kajuru that rest on Wikipedia or INEC place names are marked reported.
- **Kaduna: Ajuwa–Ajegha.** Neither the Atlas nor Glottolog classifies Ajuwa–Ajegha; it has no family in the archive.
- **Kaduna: Ikryo and Obiro.** The Atlas calls both West Kuturmi; Glottolog has one 'Kuturmi' languoid. No code is set for either.
- **Kaduna: Izere and Bacama.** The Atlas places Izere speakers in Jema'a LGA as 'probably migrants only', and Bacama fishing camps north-east of Kaduna town; neither is linked to Kaduna.
- **Kaduna: moribund languages.** The Atlas records Sambe with 2 speakers in 2005 and probably extinct by 2016, and Nincut as threatened; their current status needs a recent source.
