Researched 2026-10-02. Phase 3, the first Gombe batch. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## Summary

- **10 new languages** from Blench's Atlas, all with Glottocodes. They go under the existing West Chadic, Adamawa and Jukunoid branches; Cen Tuum is an isolate.
- **12 existing languages get Gombe links:** Tangale, Dadiya, Dijim–Bwilim, Longuda, Loo, Tsobo, Wiyaa (Waja), Tera, Jara, Kutto, Bole and Ngamo.
- **Old state names:** Gombe was formed from Bauchi in 1996, and the Atlas still writes 'Bauchi State' for Kwami, Ako (Akko), Bajoga and Dukku. These are linked to today's Gombe LGAs, with the Atlas wording quoted.
  - Kutto (Bajoga → Funakaye) and Ngamo (Nafada district → Nafada LGA) are *reported*, because they rest on a rename.
- **Billiri–Tangale** comes from Wikipedia's languages-by-LGA table and is *reported*.
- **Cen Tuum** is moribund or extinct; a 2010 search found no speakers.
- **51 links** and **25 other names**.

## The new languages

**Bangjinge** (bang1348). Bangjinge is a language spoken in Shongom LGA of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa–Ubangi (Adamawa: Waja group). Other names include Bangunji, Bangunje, Bangwinji, Báŋjìŋè. It is spoken in about 25 villages; its dialects are Nabang and Kaloh, and the orthography is based on Nabang. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 8,000 (CAPRO 1995). A reading and writing book appeared in 2007, and the Gospel of Luke has been translated. Glottolog lists it as Bangwinji.

**Burak** (bura1271). Burak is a language spoken in Shongom LGA of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa–Ubangi (Adamawa: Bikwin group). Other names include Ɓuurak, 'Yele. It is spoken at Burak town and in about 25 villages; Tadam village speaks a highly distinctive form. The Atlas notes that 'Shongom', the LGA's name, is also used for the people. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 4,000 (1992 estimate). A reading and writing book appeared in 2008.

**Cen Tuum** (cent2045). Cen Tuum is a language spoken in Balanga LGA of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as a language isolate. Other names include Centúúm, Jalaa. It was spoken at Cham town by a small number of old people among the Dijim, all of whom are fluent in Dijim. The Atlas records it as moribund or extinct: a search in 2010 failed to find any speakers. Glottolog lists it as Jalaa (cent2045), the Dijim name for it.

**Goji** (kush1236). Goji is a language spoken in Shongom LGA of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole–Tangale group). Other names include Kushi, Kushe, Chong'e. It is spoken in about twenty villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 4,000 (SIL 1973) and 5,000 (1990). A reading and writing book appeared in 2006 and New Testament extracts in 2007. Glottolog lists it as Kushi.

**Jan Awei** (jana1236). Jan Awei is a language spoken in Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Central Jukunoid). The Atlas places it west of the Muri mountains, north of the Benue, without a precise location. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 12 (1997, with a question mark).

**Ma** (kamo1254). Ma is a language spoken in Kaltungo and Akko LGAs of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa–Ubangi (Adamawa: Waja group). Other names include Kamo, Kamu. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,000 (SIL). A reading and writing book appeared in 2006 and New Testament extracts in 2007. Glottolog lists it as Kamo.

**Pero** (pero1241). Pero is a language spoken in Shongom LGA of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Walo, Péerò, Filiya. It is spoken around Filiya, in three main villages, Gwandum, Gundale and Filiya, with dialects tied to each. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 6,664 (Meek 1925) and 20,000 (SIL 1973). Primers appeared in 1931, and scripture portions and other literature in 1936–40.

**Tula** (tula1252). Tula is a language spoken in Kaltungo LGA of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa–Ubangi (Adamawa: Waja group). Other names include Ture, Kitule. Tula is 30 km east of Billiri; the language is spoken in about fifty villages, and its dialects are Baule, Wangke (used for literacy) and Yiri. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 19,209 (1952), 12,204 (1961–2), 19,000 (SIL 1973), and perhaps 100,000 (estimate). Reading and writing books appeared in 1991 and 2001, and the Gospel of John in 1929.

**Yebu** (awak1250). Yebu is a language spoken in Kaltungo LGA of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa–Ubangi (Adamawa: Waja group). Other names include Yěbù, Awok. It is spoken 10 km north-east of Kaltungo. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,035 (1962). A reading and writing book appeared in 2007. Glottolog lists it as Awak.

**Kwaami** (kwaa1269). Kwaami is a language spoken in Kwami LGA of Gombe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole–Tangale group). Other names include Kwami, Kwom, Kwáámì, Komawa. Its dialects are Kafarati and Ɗolli. The Atlas gives 'Bauchi State, Kwami LGA'; Kwami has been in Gombe State since 1996. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 10,000 (1990).

## Research gaps

- **Gombe: Hausa and Fulfulde.** The Atlas names Gombe among the states where Hausa is a first language, but Hausa is not yet a language record; Fulfulde (with its Gombe dialect) is not placed by LGA in the Atlas. Wikipedia's table gives Fulani for six LGAs (Akko, Dukku, Funakaye, Kwami, Nafada, Yamaltu-Deba) — for the peoples batch.
- **Gombe: languages in Wikipedia's table not yet matched.** Dera, Dikaka, Dza, Kyak, Moo, Yuwar, Wurkun and Cham (Balanga, Kaltungo, Shongom) are listed by Wikipedia (Ethnologue 22) but not placed in Gombe by the Atlas entries read; they need matching to Atlas names before linking.
- **Gombe: Tera's 'Gombi LGA'.** The Tera cluster's location line reads 'Gombe State, Gombi LGA'; Gombi is an Adamawa LGA. Not used; the Nyimatli member's LGA list is used instead.
- **Gombe: Jan Awei.** Location unknown beyond 'west of the Muri mountains, north of the Benue'; 12 speakers (1997, uncertain).
