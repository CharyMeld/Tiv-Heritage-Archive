## The 23 descriptions

**Ado.** Ado is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Igumale (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,220 km² and a population of 178,882 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia states that Ado was created in 1989 and that it borders Cross River State to the south and Ebonyi State to the south-east. It is one of the nine LGAs of Benue South.

**Agatu.** Agatu is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Obagaji (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,015 km² and a population of 115,523 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia states that Agatu was created in 1996 from the Agatu district of the old Otukpo division, and describes most of its people as farmers.

**Apa.** Apa is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Ugbokpo (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 995 km² and a population of 96,765 at the 2006 census. According to Wikipedia, Apa was first created on 23 March 1981, abolished on 31 December 1983 and re-created in August 1991. It borders Agatu to the north, Gwer West to the east, Otukpo to the south and Omala LGA of Kogi State to the west.

**Buruku.** Buruku is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Buruku (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,246 km² and a population of 203,721 at the 2006 census.

**Gboko.** Gboko is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Gboko (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,835 km² and a population of 358,936 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia calls Gboko the most populous of Benue's LGAs at the 2006 census. It describes the town as the ancestral headquarters of the Tiv, with the Tor Tiv's palace at its heart, and as home to the former Benue Cement Company, now Dangote Cement. It names Tiv, Hausa and English as the most widely spoken languages.

**Guma.** Guma is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Gbajimba (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 2,882 km² and a population of 191,599 at the 2006 census.

**Gwer East.** Gwer East is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Aliade (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 2,294 km² and a population of 163,647 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia lists three districts in the LGA: Yonov, Njiriv and Ngyohov.

**Gwer West.** Gwer West is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Naka (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,094 km² and a population of 122,145 at the 2006 census. Besides Naka, Wikipedia names Orawe, Bunaka, Agagbe, Nagi, Aondoana, Kula, Jimba, Anguhar, Atukpu and Ajigba among its settlements, and Ikyande as a well-known market.

**Katsina-Ala.** Katsina-Ala is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Katsina-Ala (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 2,402 km² and a population of 224,718 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes Katsina-Ala as the starting point of the A344 highway and as the site of archaeological finds of the Nok culture, and lists markets held on different days of the week, including Tomanyiin, Tor Donga, Gbor Tongov, Abaji and Amaafu.

**Konshisha.** Konshisha is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Tse-Agberagba (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,673 km² and a population of 225,672 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes the area as mainly Tiv, with Christianity the most common religion.

**Kwande.** Kwande is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Adikpo (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 2,891 km² and a population of 248,697 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia places Vandeikya to its west, Ushongo to the north, Katsina-Ala to the north-west, Cross River State to the south, the Republic of Cameroon to the south-east and Takum LGA of Taraba State to the east. It describes the area as mountainous and cooler than the rest of the state, with fifteen council wards and sixteen traditional districts.

**Logo.** Logo is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Ugba (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,408 km² and a population of 169,063 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia states that Logo was established in December 1996, when it was separated from Katsina-Ala, and that its name comes from the Logo stream, which crosses the area from east to west.

**Makurdi.** Makurdi is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Makurdi (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 820 km² and a population of 297,398 at the 2006 census. Makurdi is the capital of Benue State. Wikipedia describes the town as divided by the River Benue into north and south banks, joined by two bridges: the railway bridge of 1932 and a dual-carriage bridge commissioned in 1978. Its neighbourhoods include Wadata, High Level, Wurukum, Old and New GRA, Ankpa Ward, Nyiman Layout and Achusa.

**Obi.** Obi is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Obarike-Ito (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 423 km² and a population of 98,855 at the 2006 census.

**Ogbadibo.** Ogbadibo is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Otukpa (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 598 km² and a population of 128,707 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia names three districts: Orokam, Owukpa and Otukpa.

**Ohimini.** Ohimini is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Idekpa-Okpikwu (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 632 km² and a population of 71,482 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia states that Ohimini was created out of Otukpo LGA, and that its main district, Onyagede, borders Kogi State.

**Oju.** Oju is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Oju (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,283 km² and a population of 170,236 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia places Obi and Gwer East to its north, Konshisha and Yala (Cross River State) to the east, Izzi (Ebonyi State) to the south and Ado to the west.

**Okpokwu.** Okpokwu is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Okpoga (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 731 km² and a population of 176,647 at the 2006 census. Benue State Polytechnic is at Ugbokolo in this LGA, according to Wikipedia.

**Oturkpo.** Oturkpo is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Otukpo (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,269 km² and a population of 261,666 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes Otukpo as the headquarters of the Idoma nation and the seat of the Och'Idoma. The LGA's name is written Oturkpo in the Constitution and Otukpo in most other sources.

**Tarka.** Tarka is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Wannune (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 371 km² and a population of 79,494 at the 2006 census.

**Ukum.** Ukum is a local government area of Benue State. Statoids gives it an area of about 1,514 km² and a population of 216,930 at the 2006 census. Its headquarters is given as Zaki Biam by Statoids and as Sankera by Wikipedia (Sankera is also the name of the Tiv intermediate area that includes Ukum); the question is left open. Wikipedia describes Zaki Biam as the largest town of the LGA and the home of the Zaki Biam yam market, which it calls the largest yam market in the world.

**Ushongo.** Ushongo is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Lessel (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,228 km² and a population of 188,341 at the 2006 census.

**Vandeikya.** Vandeikya is a local government area of Benue State with its headquarters at Vandeikya (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 931 km² and a population of 230,120 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia states that Vandeikya LGA was carved out of Gboko in 1976, that its indigenous community is the Kunav, who speak Tiv, and that the area has deposits of barite, kaolin and iron ore. Most people are farmers, and small industries include rice milling.

## Research gaps

- **Ukum headquarters: Zaki Biam or Sankera.** Statoids gives Zaki Biam; Wikipedia gives Sankera (also the Tiv intermediate area's name). Needs the Benue State Government's list of LGA headquarters.
- **Creation dates of most Benue LGAs.** Only Ado (1989), Agatu (1996), Apa (1981; re-created 1991), Logo (1996) and Vandeikya (1976) are dated, from Wikipedia. The rest need the state or federal record of LGA creation.
- **Gboko LGA '11 May 1970'.** Wikipedia gives this date, before Nigeria's 1976 local government reform; not used. Needs checking.
- **Katsina-Ala Nok finds.** Wikipedia mentions archaeological finds of the Nok culture at Katsina-Ala; the site and finds need an archaeological source.
- **Official headquarters list.** Headquarters rest on Statoids and Wikipedia (Tier 3). An official Benue State list would raise them.
- **Basa-Makurdi classification.** No Glottolog entry was found under this name (Wikipedia calls it Basa-Benue). Needs a linguistic source.
