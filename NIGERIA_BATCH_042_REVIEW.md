## The 16 descriptions

**Ardo Kola** (41 words). Ardo Kola is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Sunkani (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). Statoids gives it an area of about 2,262 km² and a population of 86,921 at the 2006 census.

**Bali** (56 words). Bali is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Bali (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 9,146 km² and a population of 208,935 at the 2006 census. It is the largest LGA of the state by area. Wikipedia names the Federal Polytechnic, Bali among its institutions.

**Donga** (86 words). Donga is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Donga (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). Statoids gives it an area of about 3,121 km² and a population of 134,111 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes Donga as a town on the Donga River, created as an LGA in 1991, home mainly to the Chamba with Tiv, Ichen, Hausa and Fulani communities. Donga is the seat of the Gara Donga, the Chamba ruler, and the home of the Purma festival.

**Gashaka** (99 words). Gashaka is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Serti (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 8,393 km² and a population of 87,781 at the 2006 census. INEC's office for the LGA is in Gashaka town. Serti is the seat of the Lamdo Gashaka, and Wikipedia names Fulfulde as the main language of Gashaka and Serti. The Gashaka sector of Gashaka-Gumti National Park is reached through Serti (Nigeria National Park Service); with 8,393 km² the LGA is the second-largest of the state by area after Bali.

**Gassol** (99 words). Gassol is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Mutum Biyu (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). Statoids gives it an area of about 5,548 km² and a population of 244,749 at the 2006 census. At the 2006 census it was the most populous LGA of the state. Wikipedia describes the Benue River as its northern border, with the Taraba River flowing north through the LGA to their confluence, and names the Fulani, Wurkun, Tiv and Jukun as its main peoples, with the Dan-Anacha yam market and Jukun Wanu fishing communities at Tella.

**Ibi** (114 words). Ibi is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Ibi (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 2,672 km² and a population of 84,054 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia places the town on the south bank of the Benue, notes that the Taraba and Donga rivers flow into the Benue within the LGA, and describes it as home to Jukun Wanu and Wurbo fishing communities. The Niger Company opened a trading station at Ibi by 1899, and from 1900 the British made it the administrative headquarters of western Muri. Lake Nwonyo, five kilometres north of the town, is the site of the Nwonyo Fishing Festival.

**Jalingo** (81 words). Jalingo is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Jalingo (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 191 km² and a population of 139,845 at the 2006 census. Jalingo town is the capital of Taraba State and the seat of the Muri Emirate; with 191 km² it is the smallest LGA of the state by area. The National Museum, Jalingo, and the state office of INEC (Wuro Sembe Road) are in the town.

**Karim Lamido** (67 words). Karim Lamido is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Karim Lamido (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 6,620 km² and a population of 195,844 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes the Benue River as its southern border and names among its peoples the Karimjo, Jenjo, Bambuka, Munga Lelau, Munga Dosso, Kodei, Dadiya, Bandawa, Wurkun and Fulani.

**Kurmi** (78 words). Kurmi is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Baissa (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). Statoids gives it an area of about 4,353 km² and a population of 91,531 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes the LGA as bordering Cameroon to the south and Sardauna, Gashaka, Bali, Donga and Ussa LGAs, as a producer of cash and food crops and of timber, and notes the abandoned state-owned Baissa Timber Development Corporation.

**Lau** (64 words). Lau is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Lau (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,660 km² and a population of 96,590 at the 2006 census. According to Wikipedia the name means 'mud' in the local Lau language; the LGA borders Ardo Kola, Jalingo, Yorro and Zing LGAs and Numan in Adamawa State.

**Sardauna** (62 words). Sardauna is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Gembu (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 4,603 km² and a population of 224,437 at the 2006 census. Formerly called Mambilla, the LGA is, in Wikipedia's words, synonymous with the Mambilla Plateau. Gembu is the seat of the Chief of Mambilla.

**Takum** (103 words). Takum is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Takum (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 2,503 km² and a population of 135,349 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia records that it was created out of Wukari LGA in June 1976 and that it borders Cameroon to the south, Ussa to the west and Donga to the north. Among its peoples it names the Kuteb, Ichen, Jukun (Kpanzon), Tiv, Chamba and Hausa. Takum is the seat of the Ukwe Takum, a stool whose succession is disputed, and the home of the Kuteb Kuchicheb festival.

**Ussa** (85 words). Ussa is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Lissam (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,495 km² and a population of 92,017 at the 2006 census. INEC's office for the LGA is at Lissam. Wikipedia records that Ussa was created in 1996, after an earlier attempt failed in 1983, that it borders Cameroon to the south with the Donga River as its northern boundary, and that the Kuteb are its main people.

**Wukari** (97 words). Wukari is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Wukari (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 4,308 km² and a population of 241,546 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes the Donga River flowing through the LGA and the Benue forming its boundary with Nasarawa State to the north-west. Wukari is the seat of the Aku Uka, the ruler of the Jukun Wukari Federation; Puje, the Jukun sacred site, gives its name to one of the LGA's wards. Federal University Wukari and Kwararafa University are in the town.

**Yorro** (68 words). Yorro is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Pantisawa (according to Wikipedia and INEC). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,275 km² and a population of 89,410 at the 2006 census. Statoids gives the headquarters as Yorro, but Wikipedia names Kpantisawa and INEC places its office near the Local Government Secretariat in Pantisawa. Wikipedia describes the LGA as predominantly Mumuye.

**Zing** (75 words). Zing is a local government area of Taraba State with its headquarters at Zing (according to Statoids). Statoids gives it an area of about 1,030 km² and a population of 127,363 at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes Zing as predominantly Mumuye, made up of twelve clans. It is the seat of the Kpanti Zing; the Yakoko ward of the LGA shares its name with the Yakoko Stone Burial Ground, a proposed national monument.

## Research gaps

- **Statoids 'Disputed Areas' of Taraba.** Statoids lists a Taraba row 'Disputed Areas' with 20,253 people at the 2006 census and no area or headquarters. Which areas these are (and with which state or LGA they are disputed) needs the census publication.
- **Creation dates of Taraba LGAs.** Only three dates were found, all from Wikipedia: Takum (June 1976, from Wukari), Donga (1991) and Ussa (1996, after a failed attempt in 1983). An official list is needed.
- **Headquarters resting on one source.** Bali, Ibi, Jalingo, Karim Lamido, Lau, Takum, Wukari and Zing rest on Statoids alone (INEC shows only that its office is in the town). An official Taraba State list would settle them.
- **Yorro headquarters spelling.** INEC writes Pantisawa (also its ward names), Wikipedia Kpantisawa; Statoids gives Yorro as the headquarters. Pantisawa is used, with Kpantisawa as a variant.
