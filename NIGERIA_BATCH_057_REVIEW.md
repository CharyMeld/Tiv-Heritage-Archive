# Research batch 057 — Adamawa: the languages

Researched 2026-09-30. Phase 3, the first Adamawa batch. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## Summary

- **50 languages** and **1 new branch**, Biu–Mandara (Glottolog `bium1280`), all from Blench's Atlas.
  - All 491 Atlas entries were searched for Adamawa State and its LGA names; 69 matched and every one was read by hand.
  - Removed as false matches: Gwara, Labɨr, Numbu–Gbantu, Koenoem, Kororofa and Kuteb.
- **49 Glottocodes.** Each was matched by name or by one of the Atlas's other names, and its map point checked. Only Mukta has none.
- **134 links**, to the state and to LGAs, and **136 other names**, typed by the Atlas's key.
- **11 existing languages get Adamawa links:** Dadiya, Dong, Dza, Joole, Tha, Lamja-Deŋsa-Tola, Mumuye, Samba Daka, Samba Leko, Yendang and Laka.
- **The Fulani** people are now linked as speaking the new Fulfulde record.
- **Boundaries:** Adamawa and Taraba were one state until 1991.
  - Several entries write 'Taraba State' for Ganye, Mayo Belwa or Fufore, which are Adamawa LGAs. These are linked, quoting the Atlas.
  - 'Mubi' and 'Yola' are now divided LGAs, so those entries are linked to the state only.
- **One conflict:** Kpasam is placed in 'Numan LGA … south of Jalingo'. It is linked at *reported* level.

## The languages

**Baa** (kwaa1262). Baa is a language spoken in Numan LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Kwa group). Other names include nyaa Báà, Kwa. It is spoken at Gyakan and Kwa towns, after Munga. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,000 (SIL 1973).

**Bwatye** (baca1246). Bwatye is a language spoken in Numan and Guyuk LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group, Bata cluster). Other names include Gboare, Bwatiye, Kwaa–Ɓwaare. It is also spoken north-east of Kaduna town in Kaduna State, and Bacama fishermen migrate long distances down the Benue, with camps as far as the confluence. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 11,250 (1952); 20,000 (1963). Its speakers are known as Bachama (Bacama). Its dialects are Mulyen (Mwulyin), Dong, Opalo and Wa-Duku. An orthography appeared in 1987, and Mark in 1915. Glottolog lists it as Bacama.

**Bata** (bata1314). Bata is a language spoken in Numan, Song and Fufore LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group, Bata cluster). Other names include Batta, Gbwata. The Atlas also names Mubi LGA, since divided into Mubi North and Mubi South. It is also spoken in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 26,400 (1952), with an estimated 2,000 in Cameroon; 39,000 in total (Welmers 1971). Its dialects include Koboci, Wadi, Zumu (Jimo), Malabu, Bata of Ribaw, Bata of Demsa, Bata of Garoua and Jirai.

**Boga** (boga1251). Boga is a language spoken in Gombi LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Tera group, Eastern cluster). Other names include Boka. It is spoken in five villages.

**Ɓena** (bena1260). Ɓena is a language spoken in Song and Guyuk LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Yungur group). Other names include Ebina, Binna, Gbinna, Ebəna, Purra, Yungur. Purra is a general term for the northern Ɓena; the Atlas does not recommend the name Lala for them. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 44,300 (1963), probably including Lala and Roba; fewer than 100,000 (1990 estimate). The Ɓena are divided into seventeen clans, each said to have its own speech form, although these are too close to be called dialects. Ɓəna is also used as their own name by the Lala, Roba and Voro. Glottolog lists it as Bena (Nigeria).

**Ɓile** (bile1244). Ɓile is a language spoken in Numan LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Bantu (Jarawan). Other names include Bille, Bili, Bilanci, Kun–Ɓíilé. It is spoken 25 km south of Numan, east of the Wukari road, in 36 villages reported to be wholly Ɓile-speaking and 16 more where some Ɓile is spoken. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 30,000 (CAPRO 1992). Kun–Ɓíilé is said to be mutually intelligible with Mbula. Hausa, Fulfulde and English are widely used as second languages; Ɓile is still widely used, although young people often switch between it and Hausa. There are occasional television and radio broadcasts from Yola.

**Daba** (nucl1683). Daba is a language spoken in Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Central Chadic (West Central group: Daba group). It is spoken in a single village between Mubi and Bahuli, in Mubi LGA (since divided into Mubi North and Mubi South); most of its speakers live in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of fewer than 1,000 in Nigeria. Scripture portions have appeared since 1984, and a New Testament translation is under way.

**Dera** (dera1248). Dera is a language spoken in Shelleng LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Bo Dera, Kanakuru. The Atlas writes 'Shellen LGA'. It is also spoken in Shani LGA of Borno State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 11,300 (Westermann & Bryan). Its dialects are Shani, Shellen and Gasi. Mark and Scripture portions appeared in 1937. Glottolog lists it as Dera (Nigeria).

**Dijim–Bwilim** (diji1241). Dijim–Bwilim is a language spoken in Lamurde LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Waja group). Other names include Cham, Cam, Kindiyo, Mwana, Mwona, Fitilai. It is also spoken in Balanga LGA of Gombe State, in about twenty villages in all. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 7,545 (1968): Cham 3,257 and Bwilim 4,282. It has two members, Dijim and Bwilim. Its orthography is based on Dijim; a reading and writing book appeared in 2006, and the Gospel of Luke is ready for printing.

**Fali of Mubi** (fali1285). Fali of Mubi is a language spoken in Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group). Other names include Fali of Muchella. It is spoken in Mubi LGA (since divided into Mubi North and Mubi South), in four principal villages: Vimtim, north of Mubi, and Bahuli, Muchella and Bagira, north-east of Mubi. The Atlas cites speaker figures of more than 20,000 (1990 estimate). It is a cluster of four members named after these towns: Vin, Huli, Madzarin and Ɓween. Glottolog lists it as Fali, with Fali of Mubi, Fali of Mucella and Fali of Vimtim as dialects.

**Fulfulde** (fula1264). Fulfulde is a language spoken in Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Atlantic (Northern branch, Senegal group). Other names include Fillanci, Filatanci, Fula. The Atlas describes it as scattered throughout Nigeria and spoken in other countries of West-Central Africa, and names three main dialects in Nigeria: Central (Kano–Katsina–Bauchi–Borno), East (Adamawa) and West (Sokoto). Its Adamawa entries name Fulfulde as a second language of the Ɓile, and as the language that has replaced Holma. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,000,000 (1952). Its speakers are the Fulɓe (singular Pullo), known in Nigeria as Fulani. It has an official orthography and a newspaper; the New Testament appeared in 1964 and 1968 and the complete Bible in 1983 (in Cameroon). Glottolog treats Fula as a family (fula1264), within which it lists Adamawa Fulfulde.

**Gaa** (gaaa1245). Gaa is a language spoken in Ganye LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Northern Bantoid: Dakoid). Other names include Tiba, Təbaya. It is spoken on the Tiba Plateau. The Atlas cites speaker figures of fewer than 5,000 (Blench 1987).

**Ga'anda** (gaan1243). Ga'anda is a language spoken in Gombi LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Tera group). Other names include Mokar. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 7,600 (1952); 10,000 (SIL 1973). It is a cluster of three members: Ga'anda (six villages), Kaɓən or Gabin (twelve villages) and Fərtata (five villages). The Atlas explains the name Mokar as that of the place where 'the rolling pot stopped'.

**Gudu** (gudu1250). Gudu is a language spoken in Song LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group). Other names include Gutu, Gudo. It is spoken 120 km west of Song, in about five villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,200 (1971).

**Guɗe** (gude1246). Guɗe is a language spoken in Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group). Other names include Gude, Goudé, Mubi, Cheke, Tcheke, Mapuda. It is spoken in Mubi LGA (since divided into Mubi North and Mubi South), in Askira–Uba LGA of Borno State, and in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 28,000 (1952), with an estimated 20,000 in Cameroon. Three primers appeared in 1974 and folk tales in 1973, and a literacy programme is under way; Mark appeared in 1974, and Bible translation is in progress.

**Gvoko** (gvok1239). Gvoko is a language spoken in Michika LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Mandara/Mafa/Sukur major group, Mandara group). Other names include Gəvoko, Ngoshe Ndaghang, Ngweshe Ndhang, Nggweshe. It is also spoken in Gwoza LGA of Borno State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,500 (1963); 4,300 (SIL 1973); more than 20,000 (1990 estimate).

**Hdi** (hdii1240). Hdi is a language spoken in Michika LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Mandara/Mafa/Sukur major group, Mandara group). Other names include Hidé, Hide, Xide, Xədi, Gra, Tur. It is also spoken in Gwoza LGA of Borno State and in Cameroon.

**Holma** (holm1250). Holma is a language spoken in Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group). Other names include Da Holmaci. It was spoken north of Sorau, on the Cameroon border; the Atlas gives no LGA. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 4 (Blench 1987). The language has almost vanished and been replaced by Fulfulde, and the Atlas records it as probably extinct (1987).

**Huba** (huba1236). Huba is a language spoken in Hong, Maiha and Gombi LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bura–Higi major group, Bura group). Other names include Həba, Chobba, Kilba. The Atlas also names Mubi LGA, since divided into Mubi North and Mubi South. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 32,000 (1952); 100,000 (UBS 1980). Luwa is a dialect. A literacy programme and Bible translation are under way; Mark appeared in 1976.

**Hwana** (hwan1240). Hwana is a language spoken in Gombi LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Tera group). Other names include Hona, Hwona. It is spoken at Guyuk and thirty other villages, which the Atlas places in Gombi LGA. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 6,604 (1952); 20,000 (SIL 1973); more than 20,000 (Blench 1987 estimate).

**Kaan** (kaan1247). Kaan is a language spoken in Guyuk LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Yungur group). Other names include Libo.

**Kamwe** (kamw1239). Kamwe is a language spoken in Michika LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bura–Higi major group, Higi group). Other names include Vəcəmwe. It extends into Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 64,000 (1952); 180,000 (SIL 1973), with an estimated 23,000 in Cameroon. Its speakers are also called Higi, Hiji or Kapsiki. Its dialects are Nkafa, Dakwa (Bazza), Səna, Wula, Futu, Tili Pte and, in Cameroon, Kapsiki (Ptsəkɛ). Folk tales and a reading book appeared in 1970 and the New Testament in 1975.

**Kirya-Konzəl** (kiry1234). Kirya-Konzəl is a language spoken in Michika LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bura–Higi major group, Higi group). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 7,000 for Kirya (13 villages) and 9,000 for Konzəl (15 villages) (2007 estimates). It has two members, Kirya and Konzəl, whose speakers are also called Fali (Fali of Kiriya and Fali of Mijilu). A dictionary (2007) and a grammar (2009) have been published. Glottolog lists it as Kirya-Konzel.

**Kofa** (kofa1236). Kofa is a language spoken in Song LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Bura group). Other names include Kota. It is spoken north of the Belel road. The Atlas notes that its linguistic status is not certain, but that it is locally said to be a separate language.

**Koma** (koma1268). Koma is a language spoken in Ganye and Fufore LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Vere group). Other names include Kuma. It is spoken in the Alantika Mountains, and also in Cameroon, where most of its speakers live. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,000 (SIL 1982). Koma is a Fulfulde cover term for a cluster of closely related languages, Gomme (also Damti or Koma Kampana), Gomnome (also Koma Kadam, Mbeya or Gimbe) and Ndera (also Vomni or Doome), which the Cameroon language atlas (ALCAM) treats as separate languages; the Atlas notes that the correspondence between the Nigerian and Cameroonian names is uncertain. Glottolog lists Koma Alantika as a family.

**Kpasam** (kpas1242). Kpasam is a language spoken in Numan LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group, Yendang subgroup). Other names include Passam, Kpasham, Nyisam. The Atlas places it in a single village 'south of Jalingo', in Numan LGA of Adamawa State. Jalingo is in Taraba State, and Glottolog's map point for Kpasam also lies in Taraba, so its present state needs confirmation. Glottolog groups it with Bali as Bali-Kpasam.

**Kugama-Gengle** (kuga1239). Kugama-Gengle is a language spoken in Fufore LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group, Yendang subgroup). Other names include Kugamma, Gengle, Wegam, Wegele. The Atlas describes the number of speakers as small. Glottolog lists it as Gengle-Kugama.

**Kumba** (kumb1238). Kumba is a language spoken in Mayo Belwa LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group, Yendang subgroup). Other names include Sate, Yofo.

**Lala** (lala1261). Lala is a language spoken in Guyuk, Song and Gombi LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Yungur group). Other names include Lalla, Gworam. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 30,000 (SIL); 44,300 together with Ɓəna (1963). It is a cluster of Yang, Roba (also called Gworam) and Ebode. The Atlas notes that 'Lala' is also used as a cover term for Ɓena, Roba and other groups in Guyuk, Gombi and Song LGAs, not all of which are clearly defined. Glottolog lists it as Lala-Roba.

**Lamang** (lama1288). Lamang is a language spoken in Michika LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Mandara/Mafa/Sukur major group, Mandara group). Other names include Laamang, Waha. It is spoken mainly in Gwoza LGA of Borno State; its Central (Ghumbagha) and South (Ghudavan) members extend into Michika LGA, and Lamang South into Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 40,000 (1963); 15,000 (1970). It is a cluster of Zaladva (Lamang North), Ghumbagha (Lamang Central) and Ghudavan (Lamang South). Mark was drafted in 1991, and Bible translation is in progress.

**Longuda** (long1389). Longuda is a language spoken in Guyuk LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Longuda group). Other names include Languda, Nunguda, Nungura, Nunguraba, nyà núngúrá. It is also spoken in Balanga LGA of Gombe State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 13,700 (1952, Numan Division); 32,000 (SIL 1973). Its dialects are Nya Guyuwa (Guyuk plains), Nya Ceriya (Banjiram), Nya Tariya (Kola), Nya Dele (Jessu) and Nya Gwanda (Nyuar). Mark appeared in 1954 and 1975, a primer and folk tales in 1975, and the New Testament in 1979.

**Margi** (marg1265). Margi is a language spoken in Madagali and Michika LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bura–Higi major group, Bura group). Other names include Marghi, Margyi, Màrgí. The Atlas also names Mubi LGA, since divided into Mubi North and Mubi South. It is also spoken in Askira–Uba and Damboa LGAs of Borno State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 135,000 (1955) and 200,000 (UBS 1987), both for Margi, Margi South and Putai together. Its Central dialects include Margi babal ('Margi of the Plain', around Lasa) and Margi Dzərŋu (around Gulak), with Gwàrà, Mə̀lgwí and Wúrgà. A primer appeared in 1941, Scripture portions from 1940 and the New Testament in 1984. Glottolog lists it as Marghi Central.

**Margi South** (marg1266). Margi South is a language spoken in Michika LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bura–Higi major group, Bura group). The Atlas also names Mubi LGA, since divided into Mubi North and Mubi South. It is also spoken in Askira–Uba LGA of Borno State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 135,000 (1955), for Margi, Margi South and Putai together. Its dialects are Wamdiu and Hildi. The Atlas counts it as a separate language, more closely related to Huba. Glottolog lists it as Marghi South.

**Mboi** (mboi1246). Mboi is a language spoken in Song LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Yungur group). Other names include Mboire, Mboyi. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,200 (SIL 1973). It is a cluster of Gana (north-west of Song, at Livo village; 1,800 in 1971), Banga (west of Loko) and Haanda (west of Loko; 1,370 in 1971).

**Mbula** (mbul1260). Mbula is a language spoken in Numan, Shelleng, Song and Demsa LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Bantu (Jarawan). Other names include Ɓwà Ɓwàzà, Bare, Bere. The Atlas places the cluster in Numan, Shelleng and Song LGAs, and its Bwazza member in Demsa, Numan, Shelleng and Song LGAs, in twenty-six villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 7,900 (1952); 25,000 (Barrett 1972); 23,447 (1977); the Atlas is not sure whether these cover Mbula alone or Mbula and Bwazza. It is a cluster of Mbula, Tambo and Bwazza. There are radio broadcasts in Mbula and radio and television broadcasts in Tambo; Bwazza has a reading and writing book (2007), and the Gospel of Luke is ready for printing. Glottolog lists it as Mbula-Bwazza.

**Mom Jango** (momj1237). Mom Jango is a language spoken in Fufore LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Vere group). Other names include Vere, Were, Verre, Kobo. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 20,000 in total, including Momi, with 4,000 in Cameroon (SIL 1982). The Atlas's name Vere covers both Mom Jango and Momi.

**Momi** (vere1252). Momi is a language spoken in Fufore LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Vere group). Other names include Ziri, Vere, Were, Verre, Kobo. The Atlas also names Yola LGA, since divided into Yola North and Yola South. It is also spoken in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 20,000 in total, including Mom Jango, with 4,000 in Cameroon (SIL 1982). A dictionary was published in 2016. Glottolog lists it as Vere Kaadam, with Momi Yadim and Momi Bati among its dialects.

**Mukta** (no Glottocode). Mukta is a language spoken in Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Central Chadic (Kamwe cluster). The Atlas places it at Mukta village in Adamawa State, without naming an LGA. It forms a dialect cluster with Hya in Cameroon. No separate Glottolog entry was found.

**Ngwaba** (ngwa1251). Ngwaba is a language spoken in Gombi LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group). It is spoken at Fachi and Gudumiya. The Atlas cites speaker figures of fewer than 1,000. Its speakers are also called Gombi or Goba.

**Nyong** (nyon1241). Nyong is a language spoken in Mayo Belwa LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Leko group). Other names include Nyɔŋ, Nyɔŋ Nyanga, Mumbake, Mubako. It is spoken west of Mayo Belwa town, at Bingkola and five other villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 10,000 (SIL).

**Nzanyi** (nzan1240). Nzanyi is a language spoken in Maiha LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group). Other names include Njanyi, Njai, Njei, Zany, Nzangi, Zani. It is also spoken in Cameroon, west of Dourbeye near the Nigerian border (Mayo-Oulo subdivision). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 14,000 in Nigeria (1952); 9,000 in Cameroon. Its dialects are Paka, Rogede, Nggwoli, Hoode, Maiha, Magara, Dede and Mutidi, and Lovi in Cameroon.

**Pere** (peer1241). Pere is a language spoken in Fufore LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Leko group). Other names include Perema, Wom. It is spoken in ten villages around Yadim. The Atlas cites speaker figures of fewer than 4,000. The Atlas gives Kutin as the same language, formerly spoken in Ganye LGA and now only in Cameroon. Glottolog lists it as Peere.

**Sakun** (suku1272). Sakun is a language spoken in Madagali LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Mandara/Mafa/Sukur major group, Sukur group). Other names include Gemasakun, Sugur, Adikummu Sukur. It is spoken in seven villages. The Atlas writes 'Madgali LGA'. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 5,000 (1952); 10,000 (SIL 1973). Glottolog lists it as Sukur.

**Teme** (teme1252). Teme is a language spoken in Mayo Belwa and Fufore LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group, Yendang subgroup). Other names include Temme.

**Tsobo** (tsoo1241). Tsobo is a language spoken in Numan LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Waja group). Other names include Cibbo, Tsóbó, Lotsu–Piri, Pire, Fire, Kitta. It is also spoken in Kaltungo LGA of Gombe State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,000 (1952). Its dialects are Bərbou, Guzubo and Swabou. Glottolog lists it as Tso.

**Vemgo–Mabas** (vemg1240). Vemgo–Mabas is a language spoken in Michika LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Mandara/Mafa/Sukur major group, Mandara group). It has two members: Vemgo, also spoken in Gwoza LGA of Borno State and in Cameroon, and Mabas, a single village on the Nigeria–Cameroon frontier 10 km south-east of Madagali.

**Voro** (voro1240). Voro is a language spoken in Song and Guyuk LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Yungur group). Other names include Vɔrɔ, Ebəna, Ebina, Woro, Yungur. It is spoken south of the Dumne road, at Waltande and associated hamlets.

**Waka** (waka1275). Waka is a language spoken in Fufore and Mayo Belwa LGAs of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group, Yendang subgroup).

**Yoti** (yott1234). Yoti is a language spoken in Numan LGA of Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group, Yendang subgroup). Glottolog lists it as Yotti.

**Zizilivəkan** (zizi1238). Zizilivəkan is a language spoken in Adamawa State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (Biu–Mandara A: Bata group). Other names include Zilivə, Fali of Jilbu. It is spoken at Jilbu town in Mubi LGA (since divided into Mubi North and Mubi South), and in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 'a few hundred' in Cameroon.

## Research gaps

- **Adamawa: Mubi and Yola.** The Atlas names Mubi LGA (Bata, Daba, Fali of Mubi, Guɗe, Huba, Margi, Margi South, Zizilivəkan) and Yola LGA (Momi, Laka), since divided into Mubi North/South and Yola North/South. These are linked to the state only; the present LGAs need a current source.
- **Adamawa: LGAs with no language link.** Girei, Jada, Toungo, Mubi North, Mubi South, Yola North and Yola South have no Atlas language placed in them by name; Demsa only through the Bwazza member of Mbula. Chamba (Samba) and Fulfulde areas south of Yola need a current source.
- **Adamawa: Kpasam.** The Atlas places Kpasam in 'Adamawa State, Numan LGA, 1 village only, South of Jalingo'; Jalingo is in Taraba, and Glottolog's point for Kpasam also lies in Taraba. Linked at 'reported' until a source confirms the state.
- **Adamawa: Bali.** The Atlas writes 'Taraba State, Numan LGA, at Bali, a single village south of Jalingo'. Numan is in Adamawa but Bali is in Taraba; not linked to Adamawa.
- **Adamawa: Tera cluster.** The Atlas's head line says 'Gombe State, Gombi LGA, Kwami district'. Gombi is an Adamawa LGA, but Kwami is in Gombe State and no member is placed in Adamawa; not linked.
- **Adamawa: Thər.** An Atlas index line places Thər in Gombi LGA, north of Ga'anda, tentatively Chadic (Tera group), 'said not to be the same as Ga'anda'. It has no entry of its own; not recorded.
- **Adamawa: Jar cluster.** The Atlas's head line says 'Plateau, Bauchi and Adamawa States', but none of its members is placed in Adamawa; not linked.
- **Adamawa: languages now in Cameroon.** The Atlas records Kotopo and Kutin (Pere) as formerly spoken in Ganye LGA and now only in Cameroon (Kotopo since the creation of the Gashaka reserve in 1974). Not linked.
- **Adamawa: Holma and Mukta.** Holma (north of Sorau; probably extinct by 1987) and Mukta (Mukta village) are placed in Adamawa State without an LGA.
- **Adamawa: Hausa.** Named in the Atlas as a second language (Ɓile) but not yet a language record. Mukta has no Glottocode.
