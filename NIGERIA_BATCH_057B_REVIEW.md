# Research batch 057b — Adamawa: the peoples

Researched 2026-09-30. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## Sources

- **Adamawa State Planning Commission (official).** Its state page, as archived on 29 April 2023, has two useful tables:
  - **43 'popular festivals'**, each with its ethnic group and LGA. Today's page keeps only the first 10.
  - **Tourist sites**: the Elephant House of the Lunguda (Guyuk), the Koma Hills (Jada) and the Sukur Kingdom (Madagali).
- **Wikipedia, 'Adamawa State'**: the peoples it names, and a table of languages by LGA.
  - The table cites the Planning Commission, but I could not find it in any archived copy of that page, so it counts as Wikipedia only.
- **Blench's Atlas** (batch 057) for the languages and the peoples' own names.

## What it adds

- **29 new people records:** Bachama, Bata, Mbula, Bwazza, Lunguda, Kamwe, Kilba, Marghi, Verre, Tsobo, Gudu, Baa, Yungur, Ga'anda, Hwana, Gude, Fali, Nzanyi, Dera, Kaan, Sukur, Ɓile, Laka, Koma, Pere, Mboi, Lala, Ngwaba, Waka. Each is linked to Adamawa and to its language.
- **56 people–LGA links, covering all 21 LGAs:** 34 *well documented* and 22 *reported*.
  - A link is *well documented* when it is named in the state's table, or when Wikipedia names it and the Atlas agrees.
  - All links are presence only.
- **Existing records get Adamawa links:** Fulani, Chamba, Mumuye and Yandang (to state and LGAs), and Jibu (state only, *reported*).
- **Not recorded, listed as gaps:** Mafa, Waga, Bura and Waja, whose languages the Atlas does not place in Adamawa; Bali, which is in Taraba; and the 'Bagale', whom no source identifies.
- All the new records are short, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The people records

**Bachama.** The Bachama are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Bwatiye, Ɓwaare, Bacama. Blench's Atlas calls their language Bwatye, a Chadic language of the Bata cluster, spoken in Numan and Guyuk LGAs; Bachama fishermen migrate long distances down the Benue. The state's festival table also lists three Bwatiye festivals in 'Yola' (Njuwa at Rugange, and the Ngpalakiyie/Sharo circumcision festival at Njoboli, shared with the Fulani). The sources place them in Demsa, Fufore, Numan and Lamurde LGAs.

**Bata.** The Bata are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Gbwata. Blench's Atlas places their language, the Bata member of the Bata cluster (Chadic), in Numan, Song, Fufore and Mubi LGAs and in Cameroon. The sources place them in Demsa, Fufore and Girei LGAs.

**Mbula.** The Mbula are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Blench's Atlas places the Mbula cluster (Jarawan Bantu) in Numan, Shelleng and Song LGAs; its Tambo member has radio and television broadcasts. Wikipedia names the Mbula-Bwazza among the peoples of central Adamawa. The sources place them in Demsa and Girei LGAs.

**Bwazza.** The Bwazza are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Bare, Ɓwázà. Blench's Atlas lists Bwazza as a member of the Mbula cluster (Jarawan Bantu), spoken in twenty-six villages of Demsa, Numan, Shelleng and Song LGAs. The state's festival table calls the people of Bwazza 'Bare'. The sources place them in Demsa LGA.

**Lunguda.** The Lunguda are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Nungurab, Longuda, Núngúráyábá, Nùngùrábà, Lóngúrábá. Blench's Atlas places their language, Longuda (Adamawa family), in Guyuk LGA and in Balanga LGA of Gombe State. The state lists the Elephant House in Guyuk LGA as 'ancient architecture of great significance to the Lunguda people'. The sources place them in Guyuk LGA.

**Kamwe.** The Kamwe are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Higi, Higgi, Hiji, Kapsiki. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Chadic language of the Higi group, in Michika LGA and across the border in Cameroon. Wikipedia places the Kamwe in the north and centre of the state. The sources place them in Michika LGA.

**Kilba.** The Kilba are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Huba. Blench's Atlas calls their language Huba (other name Kilba), a Chadic language spoken in Hong, Maiha, Mubi and Gombi LGAs. The sources place them in Hong LGA.

**Marghi.** The Marghi are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Margi, Màrgí. Blench's Atlas places Margi in Madagali, Michika and (old) Mubi LGAs and in Borno State, and Margi South in Michika and Mubi LGAs. The state lists the old palace at Hamayaji, Madagali LGA, as housing the history of a Marghi king. The sources place them in Madagali and Hong LGAs.

**Verre.** The Verre are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Vere. Blench's Atlas uses the name Vere for two related languages of the Vere group: Mom Jango (Fufore LGA) and Momi (Fufore and old Yola LGAs, and Cameroon). The sources place them in Fufore and Yola South LGAs.

**Tsobo.** The Tsobo are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include nyi Tsó, Cibbo. Blench's Atlas places their language, of the Waja group (Adamawa family), in Numan LGA and in Kaltungo LGA of Gombe State. The sources place them in Lamurde LGA.

**Gudu.** The Gudu are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Chadic language of the Bata group, in Song LGA, in about five villages 120 km west of Song.

**Baa.** The Baa are a people of Adamawa State, named by Wikipedia among the state's ethnic groups. Other names include Kwa, Kwah, Báà. Blench's Atlas places their language, of the Kwa group (Adamawa family), at Gyakan and Kwa towns in Numan LGA. Wikipedia names the 'Kwah [Bah]' among the peoples of the south. The sources place them in Lamurde LGA.

**Yungur.** The Yungur are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Ɓəna, Yungirba, Ɓena. Blench's Atlas calls their language Ɓena (other names Yungur, Yangur), of the Yungur group (Adamawa family), in Song and Guyuk LGAs; the Ɓena are divided into seventeen clans. The sources place them in Song and Girei LGAs.

**Ga'anda.** The Ga'anda are a people of Adamawa State. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Chadic cluster of Ga'anda, Kaɓən (Gabin) and Fərtata, in Gombi LGA. The sources place them in Gombi LGA.

**Hwana.** The Hwana are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Hona. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Chadic language of the Tera group, in Gombi LGA, at Guyuk and thirty other villages. The sources place them in Gombi LGA.

**Gude.** The Gude are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Guɗe. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Chadic language of the Bata group, in (old) Mubi LGA, in Askira–Uba LGA of Borno State and in Cameroon. The sources place them in Mubi South LGA.

**Fali.** The Fali are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Muchala, Vimtim, Yimtim. Blench's Atlas places the Fali of Mubi cluster in (old) Mubi LGA, in four principal towns: Vimtim, Bahuli, Muchella and Bagira. The Atlas also calls the speakers of Kirya-Konzəl (Michika LGA) Fali; they are not linked here. The state's festival table lists the Danbi festival at Mijulu for the Fali of 'Mubi'. The sources place them in Mubi North LGA.

**Nzanyi.** The Nzanyi are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Nzangɨ. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Chadic language of the Bata group, in Maiha LGA and in Cameroon. The sources place them in Maiha LGA.

**Dera.** The Dera are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Kanakuru. Blench's Atlas places their language, a West Chadic language of the Bole group, in Shelleng LGA and in Shani LGA of Borno State. The sources place them in Shelleng LGA.

**Kaan.** The Kaan are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Libo, Libbo. Blench's Atlas places their language, of the Yungur group (Adamawa family), in Guyuk LGA. The state's festival table lists the Lamushi initiation festival of the Libbo at Libbo, Shelleng LGA, and Wikipedia's table lists Kaan in Numan LGA. The three sources name three different LGAs, and the Libbo are identified with the Kaan by name only. The sources place them in Shelleng and Numan LGAs.

**Sukur.** The Sukur are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Gə̀mà Sákún. Blench's Atlas calls their language Sakun (other name Sugur), a Chadic language spoken in seven villages of Madagali LGA. The state lists the Sukur Kingdom, at Sukur village, as a UNESCO heritage site. The sources place them in Madagali LGA.

**Ɓile.** The Ɓile are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Bille, ɓa Ɓíilé. Blench's Atlas places their language (Jarawan Bantu) 25 km south of Numan, east of the Wukari road, in 36 villages that are wholly Ɓile-speaking and 16 more where some Ɓile is spoken, in Numan LGA. The sources place them in Demsa LGA.

**Laka.** The Laka are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Lakka. Blench's Atlas places their language, of the Mbum group (Adamawa family), at Lau in Karim Lamido LGA (Taraba), in Yola LGA, and mainly in Cameroon. The sources place them in Yola North LGA.

**Koma.** The Koma are a people of Adamawa State. Blench's Atlas places the Koma cluster (Adamawa family) in the Alantika Mountains, in Ganye and Fufore LGAs, and in Cameroon. The state lists the Koma Hills, at Koma village in Jada LGA, as 'home to historic Koma people'. The sources place them in Jada LGA.

**Pere.** The Pere are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Peere, Pereba. Blench's Atlas places their language, of the Leko group (Adamawa family), in ten villages around Yadim in Fufore LGA, and notes that Kutin, the same language, was formerly spoken in Ganye LGA. The sources place them in Ganye LGA.

**Mboi.** The Mboi are a people of Adamawa State. Blench's Atlas places their language, a cluster of Gana, Banga and Haanda (Yungur group), in Song LGA. The sources place them in Song LGA.

**Lala.** The Lala are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Lala-Roba. Blench's Atlas places the Lala cluster (Yang, Roba and Ebode; Yungur group) in Guyuk, Song and Gombi LGAs, and notes that 'Lala' is also used as a cover term for several groups there. The sources place them in Gombi LGA.

**Ngwaba.** The Ngwaba are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Goba, Gombi. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Chadic language of the Bata group, at Fachi and Gudumiya in Gombi LGA. The sources place them in Gombi LGA.

**Waka.** The Waka are a people of Adamawa State. Other names include Wakka. Blench's Atlas places their language, of the Yendang subgroup (Adamawa family), in Fufore and Mayo Belwa LGAs. The sources place them in Demsa and Mayo Belwa LGAs.

## Research gaps

- **Adamawa: Wikipedia's language table.** Wikipedia's table of languages by LGA cites the Adamawa State Planning Commission's state page, but the archived snapshots of that page (2022-2024) do not contain it. It is attributed to Wikipedia only.
- **Adamawa: Mafa, Waga, Bura, Waja.** Named by Wikipedia (Mafa in Madagali and Mubi South; Waga in the north; Bura-Pabir in Gombi; Waja in Numan), but the Atlas places none of these languages in Adamawa (Waga is a dialect name within Lamang Central). Not recorded.
- **Adamawa: Bali.** Wikipedia names the Bali among the peoples of central Adamawa and lists Bali in Demsa; the Atlas places the Bali language at Bali, south of Jalingo (Taraba). Not linked.
- **Adamawa: Bagale.** The state's festival table lists the Ngrah Lakiye festival of the 'Bagale' in Fufore LGA; no source identifies this group. Not recorded.
- **Adamawa: Yola festivals.** Five Fulani festivals and three Bwatiye (Bachama) festivals are listed for 'Yola', which may mean Yola North or Yola South; they support state links only.
- **Adamawa: Kaan and the Libbo.** The Atlas (Guyuk), the state's festival table (Libbo, Shelleng) and Wikipedia (Numan) place the Kaan in three different LGAs; the Libbo are identified with them by name only. All links 'reported'.
- **Adamawa: smaller language communities.** About twenty Atlas languages in Adamawa have no people record because no source names the people: Dadiya, Dijim–Bwilim, Kumba, Teme, Kugama-Gengle, Yoti, Kpasam, Nyong, Gaa, Kofa, Holma, Mukta, Daba, Zizilivəkan, Boga, Tha, Joole and Dza, and the Michika languages Lamang, Hdi, Gvoko, Vemgo–Mabas and Kirya-Konzəl.
- **Adamawa: who is indigenous where.** All LGA links are presence only (settlement status unknown).
