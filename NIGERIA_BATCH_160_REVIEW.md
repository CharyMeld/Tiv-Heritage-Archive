# Research batch 160 — Niger: the languages

Researched 2026-10-07. Phase 3, the first Niger batch (peoples follow in 160b). Created in review; published only after your approval.

## Summary

- **33 new language records** from Blench's Atlas, plus **Gbagyi Nkwa** (a variety of Gbagyi) and a new **Songhai** branch (for Zarma).
  - **Kainji (most of them):** the Kamuku, Basa and Kambari clusters; Cicipu, Fungwa, Gurmana and Hùngwəryə; the Baushi cluster of Rafi (Hɨpɨna, Mɨn, Ndəkə, Rubu, Samburu, Wãyã); the Lake languages of Borgu (Reshe, Rop, Tsupamini, Shen); Rin (Pongu), Hun–Saare (Duka) and cLela (Dakarkari)
  - **Nupoid:** Asu, Dibo (Gana-Gana), Gupa–Abawa and Kami
  - **Plateau:** Jijili and Kadara
  - **Mande:** Boko and Kyenga
  - **Songhai:** Zarma
- **Existing records** gain Niger LGA links: Nupe, Gbagyi, Gbari, Kakanda, Gwandara and Busa.
- **Language links reach 14 of Niger's 25 LGAs.** The others (Bida, Bosso, Edati, Gurara, Katcha, Mashegu, Mokwa, Munya, Tafa, Wushishi and others) are not named in the Atlas, often because they were created later.
- **Endangered:** Basa-Gumna and Basa-Kontagora are probably extinct (1987), and Sambuga is possibly extinct (2008).
- **Not set:** where Glottolog has no matching entry, or only a combined one, no Glottolog code is set. Glottolog's 'Samburu' is a Kenyan language, so the Rafi Samburu record has no code.

## The new records

**Asu.** Asu is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Nupoid (Nupe group). The Atlas places it in several villages of Mariga LGA, south of Kontagora on the Mokwa road. Other names include Abewa, Ebe. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 5,000 (Blench 1987).

**Basa-Gumna.** Basa-Gumna is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kamuku–Basa group). The Atlas places it in Chanchaga LGA. Other names include Basa Kuta, Basa-Kaduna, Gwadara-Basa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: only two known semi-speakers in 1987. The Atlas describes it as probably extinct: the population known as Basawa speaks only Hausa.

**Basa-Kontagora.** Basa-Kontagora is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kamuku–Basa group). The Atlas places it in Mariga LGA, north-east of Kontagora. Speaker figures in the Atlas: fewer than ten speakers in 1987. The Atlas describes it as probably extinct, and lists it with Basa-Gumna in a single cluster.

**Basa-Gurmana.** Basa-Gurmana is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kamuku–Basa group). The Atlas places it at Kafin Gurmana, on the border of Rafi and Chanchaga LGAs. Other names include Kɔrɔmba. Speaker figures in the Atlas: more than 2,000 (1987).

**Boko.** Boko is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Mande (Southeast: Busa cluster). The Atlas places it in Borgu LGA and in the Nikki–Kande area of Benin Republic. Other names include Boo. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 120,000 in all (2004 est.). The Atlas notes literacy programmes in Boko in Benin Republic.

**Kamuku (Cinda-Regi-Rogo-Kuki).** Kamuku (Cinda-Regi-Rogo-Kuki) is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kamuku group). The Atlas places the cluster in Chanchaga, Rafi and Mariga LGAs, with Cinda and Regi also in Birnin Gwari LGA of Kaduna State. Its members include Cinda, Regi, Rogo, Kuki, Kwagere. Other names include Kamuku. The Atlas notes that the Kamuku, a cover name for this cluster, Sagamuk and Hùngwəryə, numbered 17,800 in 1952 (HDG); its member Kwacika, in Kaduna State, had only one old speaker in the 1980s.

**Cicipu.** Cicipu is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kambari cluster). The Atlas places it in Sakaba LGA of Kebbi State, in Mariga and Rafi LGAs of Niger State, and in Birnin Gwari LGA of Kaduna State. Other names include Acipa, Achipa, Achipawa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 3,600 (1949). Its own name is Cicipu, and the Atlas lists seven dialects, among them Kumbashi and Tikula.

**Dibo.** Dibo is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Nupoid. The Atlas places it in Lapai LGA, in the Federal Capital Territory and in Nassarawa LGA of Nasarawa State. Other names include Ganagana, Ganagawa, Zitako, Shitako. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 18,200 (1931); more than 100,000 (1990 estimate). The Atlas notes that an unknown number of Dibo living among the Gbari no longer speak the language.

**Fungwa.** Fungwa is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kamuku–Basa group). The Atlas places it in Rafi LGA, at Gulbe, Gabi Tukurbe, Urenciki, Renge and Utana. Other names include Tufungwa, Ura, Ula. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 900 (1949).

**Gupa–Abawa.** Gupa–Abawa is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Nupoid (Nupe). The Atlas places it in Lapai LGA, around the villages of Gupa and Edzu. Its members include Gupa, Abawa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: more than 10,000 Gupa and 5,000 Abawa (1989 estimate).

**Gurmana.** Gurmana is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Eastern group). The Atlas places it at Gurmana town and nearby hamlets in Shiroro LGA. Speaker figures in the Atlas: more than 3,000 (1989 estimate).

**Hɨpɨna.** Hɨpɨna is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (West: Baushi cluster). The Atlas places it at Supana town in Rafi LGA. Other names include Supana, Tihɨpɨna.

**Hun–Saare.** Hun–Saare is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Northern group). The Atlas places it in Sakaba LGA of Kebbi State and Rijau LGA of Niger State, with its Eastern dialect, tHun, around Rijau. Other names include Duka, Dukanci, Ethun. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 19,700 (1949); 30,000 (1980). It is also called Duka. Primers appeared in 1976, and the New Testament was nearly complete in 2003.

**Hùngwəryə.** Hùngwəryə is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kamuku–Basa group). The Atlas places it in Rafi LGA (and the former Kusheriki LGA), around the towns of Kagara and Maikujeri. Other names include Ngwoi, Ingwe, Hungworo. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 1,000 (1949); 5,000 (2007 estimate). The Atlas records an alphabet booklet (2004) and good language maintenance in 2007, with Hausa as the main second language.

**Jijili.** Jijili is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Southern: Jili group). The Atlas places it in Chanchaga and Suleja LGAs, north of the road from Minna to Suleja around Kafin Koro. Other names include Tanjijili, Koro Funtu. Speaker figures in the Atlas: about 8,000 in some eight settlements (1999).

**Kadara.** Kadara is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Northern group). The Atlas places the cluster mainly in Kachia and Kajuru LGAs of Kaduna State, with Eda also in Paikoro LGA of Niger State. Its members include Eda, Edra, Enezhe. Other names include Adara. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 22,000 (1949); 40,000 (1972). The Atlas notes a South dialect of Kadara named after Minna, and reading materials published in 2006.

**Kambari I.** Kambari I is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kambari group). The Atlas places the cluster in Magama, Mariga and Borgu LGAs of Niger State and Zuru and Yauri LGAs of Kebbi State, with its Baangi member at Ukata town in Kontagora LGA. Its members include Agaɗi, Avaɗi, Baangi, Tsishingini. Speaker figures in the Atlas: with Kambari II, 67,000 (1952) and 100,000 (1973). Glottolog treats its members as separate languages (for example Kakihum, Tsuvadi and Salka-Tsishingini); broadcasts in Salka from Radio Kontagora have halted.

**Kambari II.** Kambari II is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kambari group). The Atlas places the cluster in Magama, Rijau and Borgu LGAs of Niger State and Zuru and Yauri LGAs of Kebbi State; it also names Borgu LGA under Kwara State, before Borgu moved to Niger. Its members include Agaushi, Akimba, Cishingini, Nwanci. Other names include Agwara. Speaker figures in the Atlas: with Kambari I, 67,000 (1952) and 100,000 (1973). Its Cishingini member is also called Nwanci or Agwara, after the town.

**Kami.** Kami is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Nupoid (Nupe). The Atlas places it at Ebo town and eleven villages in Lapai LGA. Speaker figures in the Atlas: more than 5,000 (1989 estimate).

**Kyenga.** Kyenga is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Mande (Southeast Mande). The Atlas places it in Borgu LGA, north of Illo, and in Benin and Niger Republics. Other names include Kyangganya, Kenga, Tyenga. Speaker figures in the Atlas: five villages on the Nigerian side; 7,591 (1925); 10,000 including Shanga (1973).

**cLela.** cLela is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Northwestern). The Atlas places it mainly in Zuru, Sakaba and Wasagu LGAs of Kebbi State, around Zuru town, and in Rijau LGA of Niger State. Other names include Lelna, Dakarkari, Lalawa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 47,000 (1949); 69,000 (1971). Its dialects are Zuru and Ribah; a reader appeared in 1934 and scripture portions from 1931.

**Mɨn.** Mɨn is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (West: Baushi cluster). The Atlas places it in Rafi LGA, in twenty-seven villages under eight chiefships; Kukoki is the largest town. Other names include Tiimɨn, Bauchi Guda, Kukoki.

**Ndəkə.** Ndəkə is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (West: Baushi cluster). The Atlas places it at Madaka town in Rafi LGA, and notes that Shena may be a dialect. Other names include Madaka, Tundəkə.

**Reshe.** Reshe is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Lake group). The Atlas places it in Yauri LGA of Kebbi State and Borgu LGA of Niger State. Other names include Tsureshe, Gungawa, Gunganci. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 15,000 (1931); 30,000 (1973). It has three dialects; seven readers were published before 1967, and a Bible translation is in progress.

**Rin.** Rin is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Shiroro group). The Atlas places it in Rafi LGA, near Tegina. Other names include Pongu, Pangu, Tàrĩ. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 3,675 (1949); more than 20,000 (1988). The Atlas notes that, despite the own name, the community prefers forms of the name Pangu for publications; a literacy programme has run since 2004.

**Rop.** Rop is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Lake group). The Atlas places it in Borgu LGA of Niger State and Yauri LGA of Kebbi State, in at least six villages on the east shore of Lake Kainji and two on the west. Other names include Lopa, Lupa, Kirikjir. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 960 (1950); 5,000 (1992 estimate). Glottolog groups it with Tsupamini under Lopa (lopa1238).

**Rubu.** Rubu is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (West: Baushi cluster). The Atlas places it at Rubu town in Rafi LGA. Glottolog groups it with Wãyã (Wayam-Rubu).

**Samburu (Rafi).** Samburu (Rafi) is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (West: Baushi cluster). The Atlas places it at Samburu town in Rafi LGA and gives no further data. It is not the Samburu language of Kenya.

**Shama–Sambuga.** Shama–Sambuga is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kamuku–Basa group). The Atlas places it in Rafi LGA: Shama at Ushama (Kawo) town, 15 km north-west of Kagara, and Sambuga at Sambuga town, 10 km north-west of Kagara. Its members include Shama, Sambuga. Other names include Tushama. The Atlas marks Sambuga as possibly extinct (2008).

**Shen.** Shen is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Kainji Lake group). The Atlas places it in Borgu LGA. Other names include Laru, Laro, Laruwa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 1,000 (1992 estimate).

**Tsupamini.** Tsupamini is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Western Kainji: Lake group). The Atlas places it in Borgu LGA of Niger State and Yauri LGA of Kebbi State, in villages on both shores of Lake Kainji. Other names include Lopa, Lopanic. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 5,000 with Rop (1992 estimate). Glottolog groups it with Rop under Lopa (lopa1238).

**Wãyã.** Wãyã is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (West: Baushi cluster). The Atlas places it at Wayam town, in Rafi and Shiroro LGAs. Other names include Wayam, Tũwãyã. Glottolog groups it with Rubu (Wayam-Rubu, waya1265).

**Zarma.** Zarma is a language of Niger State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Nilo-Saharan (Songhai). The Atlas places it in Argungu, Birnin Kebbi and Bunza LGAs of Kebbi State, in villages of Niger State between Mokwa and Kontagora, and in Benin, Burkina Faso and Niger Republics. Other names include Djerma, Zabarma, Zabermawa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 50,000 in Nigeria (1973); 1,495,000 in Niger Republic (1986). The New Testament appeared in 1954 and the complete Bible in 1990.

**Gbagyi Nkwa.** Gbagyi Nkwa is a variety of Gbagyi spoken in Rafi LGA of Niger State, which Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020) lists as a separate entry, with more than 50,000 speakers (1989 estimate); its speakers call it Gbagyi. Glottolog lists it as Gbagyi Nkwa (gbag1257).

**Songhai.** Songhai is a group of languages of the middle Niger valley, centred on Mali and Niger Republic, which Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020) classifies as Nilo-Saharan. In Nigeria it is represented by Zarma, spoken in Kebbi State and in villages of Niger State between Mokwa and Kontagora. Glottolog lists Songhay (song1307) as a family.

## Research gaps

- **Niger: pre-1991 and pre-1996 LGAs.** The Atlas uses older LGAs: Kusheriki (no longer an LGA), Chanchaga before Bosso was split off, Suleja before Tafa and Shiroro before Munya. Only today's LGAs of the same name are linked; the presence of these languages in Bosso, Tafa, Munya and other later LGAs needs a current source.
- **Niger: Zarma's LGAs.** The Atlas places Zarma in 'villages between Mokwa and Kontagora' without naming the LGAs; it is linked at state level only.
- **Niger: glottocodes.** Mɨn, Rubu, Wãyã, Samburu (Rafi), Basa-Gurmana, Rop, Tsupamini and the two Kambari clusters have no single matching Glottolog languoid; none is set.
- **Niger: extinct or vanishing languages.** The Atlas marks Basa-Gumna and Basa-Kontagora as probably extinct, and Sambuga (Shama–Sambuga) as possibly extinct; their current status needs a recent source.
- **Niger: peoples.** The peoples of Niger State (the federal profile names Nupe, Gbagyi, Hausa, Kadara, Koro, Kakanda, Gana-Gana, Dibo, Kambari, Kamuku, Pangu, Dukawa and others) are left for batch 160b.
