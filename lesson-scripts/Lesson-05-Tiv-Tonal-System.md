## INTRO

> "Welcome to Lesson 5 — the final lesson in this foundational series. We are covering the Tiv Tonal System.
>
> Tiv is a tonal language. This means that the pitch at which you say a vowel — whether your voice goes high, stays mid, or falls low — determines the meaning of the word. The same consonants and vowels, said with different tones, produce completely different words.
>
> This is not optional. Tone is part of the word. Getting the tone wrong does not produce a 'foreign accent' version of a word — it produces a different word entirely.
>
> Tiv has three tones: High, Low, and Mid. There is also the distinction of long vowels. Let us learn all four tonal markers used in the Tiv writing system."

---

## SECTION 1 — Why Tone Matters in Tiv

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: Tone contrast examples]**

> "Before we look at the markers, here is a real example of how tone changes meaning.
>
> The word 'tòr' — written with a low tone grave accent — means KING.
> The word 'tór' — written with a high tone acute accent — means PESTLE.
> The word 'tŏr' — written with a breve, indicating mid tone — means a species of mouse.
>
> Same letters. Three completely different meanings. The only difference is the tone mark on the vowel."

**[AUDIO CUE: tòr — king | tór — pestle | tŏr — mouse]**

> "This is why tone is not decoration — it is meaning."

## SECTION 2 — The Four Tonal Markers

---

### MARKER 1 — á (High Tone)

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "á" | IPA: /á/ | Acute accent — voice rises or stays high]**

> "The HIGH TONE is marked by an acute accent — the mark that slopes upward from left to right, like this: á
>
> When you see this mark on a vowel, your voice rises or holds at a high pitch. Think of it like a question ending in English — the voice goes up and stays there for the duration of that syllable."

### MARKER 2 — à (Low Tone)

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "à" | IPA: /à/ | Grave accent — voice falls or stays low]**

> "The LOW TONE is marked by a grave accent — the mark that slopes downward from left to right, like this: à
>
> When you see this mark, your voice falls or holds at a low pitch. Think of a statement that ends with finality — the voice drops and settles."

### MARKER 3 — a (Mid Tone)

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "a" | IPA: /a/ | No accent mark — voice stays at middle level]**

> "The MID TONE carries no accent mark. When you see a vowel with no tone marking at all, the voice stays at a natural, relaxed middle pitch — neither high nor low.
>
> This is important: the absence of a mark IS the mark for mid tone. A plain vowel is not unmarked — it is actively mid tone."

**[AUDIO CUE: Pronunciation of mid tone "a"]**
> "Repeat: a — mid"

**[AUDIO CUE: Pronunciation of "wan"]**
> "Example — wan: meaning WORD or STORY. The same root as wán and wàn — but with mid tone it means a narrative or a spoken word."
> "Repeat: wan — word / story"

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: Full three-way contrast — wán / wàn / wan]**

> "Now hear all three forms together:"
**[AUDIO CUE: wán — to call | wàn — to be lost | wan — word / story]**

> "Three words. Same letters. Three tones. Three meanings."

**[AUDIO CUE: tŏr — a species of mouse]**
> "Additional: tŏr — a species of mouse (mid tone on the vowel)"

---

### MARKER 4 — aa (Long Vowel)

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "aa" | IPA: /aː/ | Double vowel — held for longer duration, changes meaning]**

> "The LONG VOWEL is written by doubling the vowel letter — aa, ee, ii, oo, uu.
>
> This is not simply holding the vowel twice as long for stylistic emphasis. In Tiv, doubling the vowel is a phonemic feature — it changes the meaning of the word. A long vowel and a short vowel are different sounds that produce different words.
>
> Long vowels can also carry tone marks — so you may see áá for high-long, àà for low-long, and so on."

**[AUDIO CUE: Pronunciation of long "aa" vowel]**
> "Repeat: aa — long A"

**[AUDIO CUE: Pronunciation of "vaa"]**
> "Example — vaa: meaning TO CRY or TO WEEP. The double A is essential — 'va' with a single A would be a different word."
> "Repeat: vaa — to cry / to weep"

## SECTION 4 — Tonal System Summary

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: Full tonal markers table]**

## SECTION 5 — Practical Application

> "As you read and speak Tiv, follow this approach:
>
> Step 1 — Identify the vowels in the word.
> Step 2 — Check each vowel for an accent mark.
> Step 3 — Acute accent = high. Grave accent = low. No mark = mid. Double vowel = long.
> Step 4 — Produce the word with those tones in sequence.
>
> At first this will feel slow. With practice it becomes automatic. Native Tiv speakers process this instantly — the tone is inseparable from the word in their mental lexicon.
>
> Your goal is to reach that same level of integration."
