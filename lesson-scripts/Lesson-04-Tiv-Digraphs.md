## INTRO

> "Welcome back. This is Lesson 4 — The Tiv Digraphs.
>
> A digraph is two letters written together that represent a single sound. In Tiv, digraphs are not shortcuts or abbreviations — they are their own letters with their own sounds. You must treat each digraph as one unit.
>
> The Tiv language has 13 digraphs. Some will feel familiar — like CH and SH. Others — like GB and KP — are sounds that do not exist in English at all. These are labial-velar sounds common in West African languages, and they are one of the most fascinating features of the Tiv phonological system.
>
> Let us go through all 13."

---

## SECTION 1 — What is a Digraph?

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: Digraph definition + example]**

> "A digraph = two letters, one sound.
>
> For example, in English, 'sh' in the word 'ship' is a digraph — S and H together make one sound. In Tiv, the same principle applies but with more variety and some uniquely African sounds."

> "Important: when you see a digraph in Tiv writing, do not pronounce the letters separately. GB is not 'G' then 'B' — it is one blended sound produced simultaneously."

## SECTION 2 — The 13 Digraphs in Detail

---

### GB gb — /ɡ͡b/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "GB gb" | IPA: /ɡ͡b/ | Labial-velar voiced stop]**

> "GB is a labial-velar stop — meaning the back of your tongue touches your velum (back of the mouth) while your lips close at the same moment. Both actions happen simultaneously as one sound.
>
> This sound does not exist in English. The closest way to approach it is to try to say 'g' and 'b' at the exact same time — not one after the other.
>
> Listen to the GB sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Gba.webm]**
> "The word is gba — meaning TO FALL. Focus on the GB sound at the start."
> "Repeat: GB... gba — to fall"

---

### KP kp — /k͡p/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "KP kp" | IPA: /k͡p/ | Labial-velar voiceless stop]**

> "KP is the voiceless counterpart of GB. The same simultaneous back-of-mouth and lip closure — but without voice. Think of it as K and P happening at once.
>
> Listen to the KP sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Kper.webm]**

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "SH sh" | IPA: /ʃ/ | Sounds like: 'sh' in shoe]**

### NY ny — /ɲ/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "NY ny" | IPA: /ɲ/ | Palatal nasal — like 'ny' in canyon]**

> "NY is a palatal nasal. The closest English example is the 'ny' sound in the word 'canyon' or the 'ñ' in Spanish 'mañana.' The tongue touches the hard palate behind your teeth while air flows through your nose.
>
> Listen to the NY sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Nyam.webm]**
> "The word is nyam — meaning MEAT. Focus on the NY sound at the start."
> "Repeat: NY... nyam — meat"

---

### GH gh — /ɣ/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "GH gh" | IPA: /ɣ/ | End-of-word sound — like 'gh' in dough]**

> "GH in Tiv is used at the end of words and produces a soft, nearly silent fricative sound — similar to the 'gh' in the English words 'dough' or 'borough.' The back of the tongue approaches the velum but does not fully close.
>
> In many natural speech patterns, this sound is very soft and blends into the vowel before it.
>
> Listen for the GH at the end of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Sugh.webm]**
> "The word is sugh — meaning TO THANK. Listen carefully to how the GH softens the ending."
> "Repeat: sugh — to thank"

---

### GW gw — /ɡw/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "GW gw" | IPA: /ɡw/ | G and W blended — like 'gw' in Gwen]**

> "GW is a labialised G — the hard G sound made while the lips are simultaneously rounded as if saying W. Think of the name 'Gwen' — the GW at the start is exactly this sound.
>
> Listen to the GW sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Gwa.webm]**
> "The word is gwa — meaning FAME or REPUTATION. Focus on the GW sound at the start."
> "Repeat: GW... gwa — fame"

### KW kw — /kw/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "KW kw" | IPA: /kw/ | Sounds like: 'qu' in queen]**

> "KW sounds like the 'qu' in 'queen' or the 'kw' in 'awkward.' It is a familiar English sound produced by rounding the lips into W while saying K.
>
> Listen to the KW sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Kwase.webm]**

### TS ts — /ts/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "TS ts" | IPA: /ts/ | Sounds like: 'ts' in cats or fits]**

> "TS sounds like the 'ts' at the end of the English words 'cats' or 'fits.' It is a single crisp sound — not two separate sounds.
>
> Listen to the TS sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Tsar.webm]**
> "The word is tsar — meaning BRIDGE. Focus on the crisp TS at the start."

### MB mb — /ᵐb/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "MB mb" | IPA: /ᵐb/ | Prenasalized B — hum through nose before the B]**

> "MB is a prenasalized B. Before the B sound, there is a nasal hum — like a short 'm' that launches the 'b.' You do not say M then B as two separate sounds. The nasal onset is very brief and blends immediately into the B.
>
> This type of prenasalized consonant is common in many African languages."

**[AUDIO FILE: Mba.webm]**
> "Repeat: MB"

**[AUDIO FILE: Mban.webm]**
> "Example — mban: meaning THEM."
> "Repeat: mban — them"

### ND nd — /ⁿd/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "ND nd" | IPA: /ⁿd/ | Prenasalized D — nasal onset before the D]**

> "ND is a prenasalized D. The same principle as MB — a brief nasal hum that immediately gives way to the D sound. It is one smooth articulation, not N + D pronounced separately.
>
> Listen to the ND sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Nder.webm]**
> "The word is nder — meaning WAKE UP. Notice the nasal onset before the D."
> "Repeat: ND... nder — wake up"

---

### NG ng — /ŋ/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "NG ng" | IPA: /ŋ/ | Nasal — like 'ng' in sing or ring]**

> "NG is the velar nasal — the same sound as the 'ng' at the end of English words like 'sing' or 'ring.' The back of the tongue touches the velum while air flows through the nose.
>
> In Tiv, NG can appear at the beginning of a word — this takes some practice for English speakers since English only uses this sound at the end of words.
>
> Listen to the NG sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Nger.webm]**
> "The word is nger — meaning WRITE. Focus on the NG sound at the very start."
> "Repeat: NG... nger — write"

---

### BW bw — /bw/

**[SHOW ON SCREEN: "BW bw" | IPA: /bw/ | B with rounded lips — B and W simultaneously]**

> "BW is the B sound made with the lips simultaneously rounded as if beginning a W. The lip rounding happens at the same moment as the B — not after it.
>
> Listen to the BW sound at the beginning of this word."

**[AUDIO FILE: Bwagi.webm]**
> "The word is bwagi — meaning TO PICK or a DIGGING TOOL. Focus on the BW at the start."
> "Repeat: BW... bwagi — pick / digging tool"
