# Research batch 044b — Plateau: the peoples

Researched 2026-09-30. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## Sources

**As for Taraba, no official list of Plateau's peoples was found.**
- The state government's People page names no ethnic groups.
- The Internet Archive was offline, so archived state and NIPC pages could not be checked.
- The records therefore rest on **Wikipedia's list of the state's indigenous groups, checked against Blench's Atlas**. They are graded *well documented* at most.

## What it adds

- **26 new people records:** Afizere, Amo, Anaguta, Aten, Bache, Berom, Boghom, Buji, Fyer, Goemai, Irigwe, Jipal, Kofyar, Miship, Montol, Mupun, Mushere, Mwaghavul, Ngas, Piapung, Pyem, Ron, Tarok, Youm, Bijim, Kadung. Each is linked to Plateau and to its language.
- **51 people–LGA links**, from Ethnologue's table via Wikipedia, which covers **all 17 current LGAs**.
  - *Well documented* where the Atlas agrees, *reported* otherwise.
  - All are presence only.
- **34 new language–LGA links.** These are mostly for current LGAs the Atlas does not name (Jos North and East, Kanke, Mikang, Riyom and the two Langtangs).
- **Existing records:**
  - **Kulere**, from the Nasarawa batch, gets Plateau links for Bokkos.
  - **Jukun** gets Plateau links for Wase.
  - The settler groups Wikipedia names that already have records (**Hausa, Idoma, Igbo, Yoruba**) are linked to Plateau as *migrant communities*.
- **Not recorded, listed as gaps:**
  - **Talet:** Wikipedia's link leads nowhere.
  - **Jarawa:** the name is ambiguous.
  - **Atyap:** a Kaduna people.
- All the new records are short, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The people records

**Afizere.** The Afizere are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Fizere, Afusare, Afizarek, Jarawan Dutse. Blench's Atlas calls their language Izere, a Plateau language spoken in Jos South and Barkin Ladi LGAs and in Toro LGA of Bauchi State. Ethnologue lists their language in Jos East and Jos North LGAs.

**Amo.** The Amo are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Amap. Blench's Atlas calls their language Map (Glottolog: Amo), a Kainji language of Bassa LGA and Saminaka LGA, Kaduna State. Ethnologue lists their language in Bassa LGA.

**Anaguta.** The Anaguta are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Blench's Atlas calls their language Iguta (also Naraguta), a Kainji language of Bassa LGA. Ethnologue lists their language in Bassa and Jos North LGAs.

**Aten.** The Aten are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Nìtèn. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Beromic language, in Barkin Ladi LGA and in Jema'a LGA of Kaduna State; Ethnologue's name for it is Iten. Ethnologue lists their language in Riyom LGA.

**Bache.** The Bache are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Rukuba. Blench's Atlas calls their language Che (also Kuche, Rukuba), a Plateau language of Bassa LGA, and gives Bache as the people's own name. Ethnologue lists their language in Bassa LGA.

**Berom.** The Berom are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Birom, Wòrom. Blench's Atlas places their language, the largest of the Beromic group, in Barkin Ladi and (old) Jos LGAs and in Jema'a LGA of Kaduna State. Ethnologue lists their language in Barkin Ladi, Jos North, Jos South and Riyom LGAs.

**Boghom.** The Boghom are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Burumawa, Bogghom. Blench's Atlas places their language, a West Chadic language, in Kanam LGA. Ethnologue lists their language in Kanam, Shendam and Wase LGAs.

**Buji.** The Buji are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Boze, anaBoze. Blench's Atlas calls them Boze, speakers of a member of the Jere cluster (Kainji) on both sides of the Jos–Zaria road north of Jos, in Bassa LGA; Glottolog treats Buji as a dialect of Jere. Ethnologue lists their language in Bassa LGA.

**Fyer.** The Fyer are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Fier. Blench's Atlas places their language, a West Chadic language of the Ron group, in Mangu LGA. Ethnologue lists their language in Pankshin LGA.

**Goemai.** The Goemai are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Blench's Atlas places their language, a West Chadic language, in Shendam LGA (and in Awe and Lafia LGAs, Nasarawa State). Ethnologue lists their language in Shendam LGA.

**Irigwe.** The Irigwe are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include yíɾìgʷȅ. Blench's Atlas calls their language Rigwe (also Irigwe, Miango), a Plateau language of Bassa LGA and Kauru LGA, Kaduna State. Ethnologue lists their language in Bassa LGA.

**Jipal.** The Jipal are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Blench's Atlas lists Jipal, in Mangu LGA, as a member of the Pan cluster (the Kofyar language); Glottolog treats it as a dialect of Pan. Ethnologue lists their language in Mangu and Pankshin LGAs.

**Kofyar.** The Kofyar are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia describes the Kofyar as comprising the Doemak, Kwalla and Mernyang. Blench's Atlas gives Kofyar as the people's own name for the speakers of the Pan cluster, spoken in Shendam, Mangu and Qua'an Pan LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Qua'an Pan LGA.

**Miship.** The Miship are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Blench's Atlas places their language, a West Chadic language, in Mangu and Shendam LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Pankshin and Shendam LGAs.

**Montol.** The Montol are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Blench's Atlas calls their language Tel (other names Montol, Baltap), a West Chadic language of Shendam LGA. Ethnologue lists their language in Mikang and Shendam LGAs.

**Mupun.** The Mupun are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Blench's Atlas lists Mupun as a member of the Mwaghavul cluster; Glottolog treats it as a dialect of Mwaghavul. Ethnologue lists their language in Kanke and Pankshin LGAs.

**Mushere.** The Mushere are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Blench's Atlas places their language, a member of the Cakfem–Mushere pair (West Chadic), in about thirteen villages of Mangu LGA. Ethnologue lists their language in Bokkos LGA.

**Mwaghavul.** The Mwaghavul are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Sura. Blench's Atlas places their language, a West Chadic language, in Barkin Ladi and Mangu LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Mangu and Pankshin LGAs.

**Ngas.** The Ngas are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Kerang, Angas. Blench's Atlas places their language, a West Chadic language with Hill and Plain dialects, in Pankshin, Kanam and (old) Langtang LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Kanam, Kanke, Pankshin and Qua'an Pan LGAs.

**Piapung.** The Piapung are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Pyapung. Blench's Atlas calls their language Pyapung, a West Chadic language of Shendam LGA.

**Pyem.** The Pyem are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Fyem. Blench's Atlas calls their language Pyam, a Plateau language of (old) Jos, Barkin Ladi and Mangu LGAs, and describes it as endangered. Ethnologue lists their language in Mangu LGA.

**Ron.** The Ron are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Challa. Wikipedia lists them together with the Kulere as 'Ron-Kulere'; Blench's Atlas treats Run (Ron) and Kulere as separate West Chadic languages, and places Run in Bokkos and Mangu LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Barkin Ladi and Bokkos LGAs.

**Tarok.** The Tarok are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Blench's Atlas places their language, a Tarokoid language, in (old) Langtang and Wase LGAs. Ethnologue lists their language in Kanam, Langtang North, Langtang South, Mikang, Kanke, Shendam and Wase LGAs.

**Youm.** The Youm are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Other names include Gerkawa. Wikipedia's Youm (linked there to 'Ywam language') are taken to be the speakers of the Atlas's Ywom (also Yiwom), a West Chadic language of Shendam and (old) Langtang LGAs; the match rests on the name. Mikang LGA, where Ethnologue lists Youm, was created from Langtang. Ethnologue lists their language in Mikang LGA.

**Bijim.** The Bijim are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia links their language to 'Kwanka language'. Blench's Atlas places Bijim in Tafawa Balewa LGA of Bauchi State, as a member of the Vaghat–Ya–Bijim–Legeri cluster (Tarokoid), whose Plateau members are in Mangu LGA. Ethnologue lists their language in Mangu and Pankshin LGAs.

**Kadung.** The Kadung are one of the indigenous peoples of Plateau State named by Wikipedia. Wikipedia links their language to 'Kwanka language'. Blench's Atlas gives Kadun and Kwanka as other names of Kwang (Vaghat), a member of the Vaghat–Ya–Bijim–Legeri cluster (Tarokoid) in Mangu LGA. Ethnologue lists their language in Mangu and Pankshin LGAs.

## Research gaps

- **No official list of Plateau's peoples.** The state government's People page names none, and the Internet Archive was offline, so archived state and NIPC pages could not be checked. Wikipedia says the state has 'over forty ethno-linguistic groups'.
- **Talet, Jarawa, Atyap.** Named by Wikipedia. Talet's link leads nowhere (not assumed to be Tal); 'Jarawa' is used in the Atlas both for the Izere and for the Jar cluster; the Atyap are a Kaduna people with no Plateau entry in the Atlas. Not recorded.
- **The many smaller Plateau peoples.** About 20 language communities recorded in batch 044 (Cara, Che's neighbours, Janji, Lemoro, Zora, Tunzu, Horom, Bo-Rukul, Tal, Tambas, Pe and others) have no people record because Wikipedia does not name them.
- **Who is indigenous where.** All LGA links are presence only (settlement status unknown).
- **Wikipedia's Ethnologue table.** Unmapped names: Ibaas, Panawa, Duhwa, Manguna (Shagau), Jhar and Jar, Saya, Dass, Jorto, Takad (Tyap), Ganang, and 'Jukun' as a language.
- **Youm and Ywom.** The Youm people record rests on a name match between Wikipedia's 'Youm' and the Atlas's 'Ywom'; a source that equates them is wanted.
