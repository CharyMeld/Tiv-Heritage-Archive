# Tiv Foods Arrangement for Archive Seeding

## 1. Ruam Kumen
**Category:** Staple Food  
**English Name:** Pounded Yam  
**Description:** The most popular Tiv staple food, usually served with a variety of native soups. It is widely eaten during both everyday meals and special occasions.  
**Key Ingredients:** Yam  
**Commonly Served With:** Traditional soups and sauces  
**Cultural Significance:** Considered one of the most important staple foods among the Tiv people.

## 2. Kyegh Sha Shwa
**Category:** Cultural Dish  
**English Name:** Chicken with Sesame Seed Sauce / Chicken and Beniseed Dish  
**Description:** A highly valued Tiv cultural dish made with chicken cooked in sesame seeds (beni-seed). It is one of the best-known Tiv dishes and is strongly associated with cultural identity and celebration.  
**Key Ingredients:** Chicken, sesame seeds (beni-seed), spices  
**Commonly Served During:** Festivals, celebrations, special gatherings  
**Cultural Significance:** So culturally important that it is associated with an annual festival.

## 3. Akpukpa
**Category:** Staple / Delicacy  
**English Name:** Bambara Nut or Bean Pudding  
**Description:** A Tiv delicacy prepared from bambara nuts or beans, often formed into a pudding or cake. It is a traditional local food enjoyed with soups or sauces.  
**Key Ingredients:** Bambara nuts or beans  
**Commonly Served With:** Local soups or sauces  
**Cultural Significance:** Recognized as a traditional Tiv delicacy and a staple protein source in Tiv cuisine.

## 4. Peto / Akuto
**Category:** Pottage  
**English Name:** Yam or Sweet Potato Pottage  
**Description:** A hearty Tiv meal made by cooking yam or sweet potato with palm oil and green vegetables. It is nourishing and commonly enjoyed as a family meal.  
**Key Ingredients:** Yam or sweet potato, palm oil, greens  
**Commonly Served As:** Main meal  
**Cultural Significance:** Represents the Tiv preference for earthy, filling traditional meals.

# Traditional Tiv Soups and Sauces

## 5. Pocho
**Category:** Soup / Sauce  
**English Name:** Watery Traditional Sauce  
**Description:** A light, watery sauce commonly prepared with smoked fish or meat, pepper, and locust beans. It is usually eaten with staple foods like pounded yam.  
**Key Ingredients:** Smoked fish or meat, pepper, locust beans  
**Commonly Served With:** Ruam Kumen and other staples  
**Cultural Significance:** A common traditional sauce in Tiv cuisine.

## 6. Genger
**Category:** Soup  
**English Name:** Genger Flower Bud Soup  
**Description:** A popular Tiv soup made from the flower buds of the Genger tree. It is valued both as food and for its medicinal reputation.  
**Key Ingredients:** Genger flower buds, local seasonings  
**Commonly Served With:** Pounded yam and other staples  
**Cultural Significance:** Known as both a traditional and medicinal soup.

## 7. Atuu
**Category:** Soup  
**English Name:** Okro Soup  
**Description:** A Tiv soup made primarily from okro. It is slimy in texture and is commonly prepared as a companion to staple foods.  
**Key Ingredients:** Okro, local seasonings  
**Commonly Served With:** Ruam Kumen and similar staples  
**Cultural Significance:** One of the commonly eaten soups in Tiv cuisine.

## 8. Atyever
**Category:** Soup  
**English Name:** Vegetable Soup  
**Description:** A Tiv vegetable-based soup often prepared with nune (locust beans) for a rich and distinctive flavor.  
**Key Ingredients:** Vegetables, locust beans (nune), local seasonings  
**Commonly Served With:** Pounded yam and other staples  
**Cultural Significance:** Reflects the Tiv use of vegetables and fermented seasoning in traditional cooking.



# Common Tiv Snacks and Beverages

## 11. Ibyer
**Category:** Snack / Dessert  
**English Name:** Fermented Maize or Millet Pudding  
**Description:** A fermented creamy pudding made from ground maize or millet. It is enjoyed as a light meal, snack, or refreshing traditional food.  
**Key Ingredients:** Maize or millet  
**Commonly Served As:** Snack or light meal  
**Cultural Significance:** A traditional Tiv fermented food.

## 12. Burukutu
**Category:** Beverage  
**English Name:** Traditional Grain Beer  
**Description:** A traditional alcoholic beverage brewed from guinea corn or millet. It is commonly consumed in social and communal settings.  
**Key Ingredients:** Guinea corn or millet  
**Commonly Served During:** Social gatherings and cultural occasions  
**Cultural Significance:** A traditional communal drink in many local settings.

## 13. Kuese
**Category:** Snack  
**English Name:** Bean Cakes  
**Description:** Deep-fried bean cakes similar to akara. They are eaten as a snack and may also be taken with pap or other light accompaniments.  
**Key Ingredients:** Beans, oil, seasoning  
**Commonly Served As:** Snack or breakfast item  
**Cultural Significance:** A familiar snack in Tiv food culture.

## 14. Igbough Ahi
**Category:** Snack  
**English Name:** Roasted or Boiled Bambara Nuts  
**Description:** A simple traditional snack made from bambara nuts, either roasted or boiled for eating.  
**Key Ingredients:** Bambara nuts  
**Commonly Served As:** Snack  
**Cultural Significance:** Reflects Tiv use of locally available legumes as food.

## 15. Asondo
**Category:** Snack  
**English Name:** Dried Sweet Potato Slices  
**Description:** Dried slices of sweet potato eaten as a snack or preserved food item.  
**Key Ingredients:** Sweet potato  
**Commonly Served As:** Snack  
**Cultural Significance:** Shows the Tiv tradition of preserving and consuming root crops in different forms.

# Culinary Characteristics of Tiv Food

## 16. Nune
**Category:** Seasoning  
**English Name:** Locust Beans / Iru  
**Description:** A strong, pungent traditional seasoning used in many Tiv soups and sauces to add depth of flavor.  
**Key Use:** Soup and sauce seasoning  
**Cultural Significance:** One of the most important traditional flavoring agents in Tiv cuisine.

## 17. Communal Dining
**Category:** Food Culture  
**English Name:** Shared Eating Tradition  
**Description:** Tiv food culture strongly values eating together and sharing meals. Hospitality is reflected in the common invitation "Va ya kwagh" meaning "come and eat."  
**Key Feature:** Shared meals and guest hospitality  
**Cultural Significance:** Reinforces kinship, generosity, and communal living.

## 18. Cultural Importance of Kyegh Sha Shwa
**Category:** Cultural Food Heritage  
**English Name:** Festival-Associated Dish  
**Description:** Kyegh sha shwa is not just a meal but a symbol of Tiv cultural pride, so significant that it is celebrated through a dedicated annual festival.  
**Key Feature:** Food-festival connection  
**Cultural Significance:** Demonstrates the close relationship between Tiv cuisine and cultural identity.
