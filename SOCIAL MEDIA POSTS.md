POST 2 — Proverbs (with live examples)
─────────────────────────────────────────
The Tiv people have a proverb for every situation in life. Here are some straight from the archive:

📜 "Mimi hemba tungu."
→ Honesty is better than collateral. (Integrity is more valuable than anything material.)

📜 "Atseree wua alom."
→ Trickery killed the hare. (Deception always leads to downfall.)

📜 "Mfega wa hemba ime."
→ Ignorance is darker than darkness. (Lack of knowledge is more dangerous than any physical threat.)

📜 "Ka akor a ou ka a mee iyese i ta u ye."
→ It is a person's strong appetite for meat that causes the sting of a scorpion.
(Greed leads people into trouble.)

📜 "Angbian doon ken ikyôngo."
→ A brother is good in the wilderness. (Family bonds hold even in the hardest times.)

These are centuries old — and they are all free to read at the Tiv Heritage Archive.

POST 4 — Traditional Foods (with live examples)
─────────────────────────────────────────
Tiv food is not just nutrition — it is culture on a plate.

Here are some traditional dishes documented in the archive:

🍽️ Ruam Kumen (Pounded Yam) — the cornerstone of Tiv cuisine. Present at every significant meal, from everyday family dining to major celebrations. Sharing Ruam Kumen reflects Tiv communal values and hospitality.

🍗 Kyegh Sha Shwa (Chicken with Sesame Seed Sauce) — so culturally important that it has an entire festival dedicated to it. Served to distinguished guests and at major communal events.

🌸 Genger (Genger Flower Bud Soup) — made from the buds of the Genger tree. Eaten both as food and used in traditional medicine.

🌾 Burukutu (Traditional Grain Beer) — brewed from guinea corn or millet. A communal drink that represents fellowship, hospitality, and bonding among the Tiv people.

🫘 Akpukpa (Bambara Nut Pudding) — a protein-rich traditional delicacy prized in Tiv households.

Explore Tiv foods → https://www.tivheritage.com/archive/foods

POST 5 — Festivals (with live examples)
─────────────────────────────────────────
The Tiv people celebrate life in ways rooted in the land, family, and community. Here are some of the festivals documented in our archive:

🎭 Kwagh-Hir (Puppet Theatre Festival)
One of Africa's most sophisticated indigenous theatre traditions. Communities use puppets, masquerades, music, and drama to teach moral lessons, retell history, and critique injustice — all in one performance.

🌾 Iniongo i iyou mbi her (New Yam Festival)
An annual harvest celebration — giving thanks for the season and praying for continued prosperity.

🏇 Nyinya (Festival of Affluence)
When a Tiv person acquires a horse — the highest symbol of status — they host a feast for their age group. Wealth is celebrated communally, not hoarded.

🐟 Akata (Fishing Festival, Katsina-Ala)
An inter-ethnic event bringing Tiv, Etulo, and Jukun communities together to celebrate their riverine heritage and cooperation.

🎶 Ivom Dance Festival
A gathering of communities around traditional Tiv dance, rhythm, and performance — preserving the arts for the next generation.

Explore all Tiv festivals → https://www.tivheritage.com/archive/festivals

POST 6 — Plants & Medicine (with live examples)
─────────────────────────────────────────
Long before modern pharmacies, the Tiv people understood the healing power of plants. The Tiv Heritage Archive is documenting this knowledge so it is never lost.

🌿 Ityuna (Bitter Leaf) — treats malaria, diabetes, and stomach ailments
🌳 Nune (Locust Bean Tree) — bark used for infections and wound healing
🌶️ Yiye (African Black Pepper) — used for digestion, inflammation, and respiratory health
🫐 Hulugh (Black Plum / Vitex doniana) — fever, pain, eye infections, digestive issues
🌾 Ihila (Spear Grass) — roots boiled traditionally for fever and body weakness
🪵 Tserama (African Teak) — bark and roots used for digestive issues, infections, and fever

This is not superstition — it is centuries of indigenous knowledge. Now documented and shared freely.

Explore Tiv plants → https://www.tivheritage.com/archive/plants

POST 7 — Animals in Tiv Culture (with live examples)
─────────────────────────────────────────
In Tiv culture, animals carry meaning — they appear in proverbs, folktales, rituals, and spiritual life.

🦅 Igyuve (Eagle) — symbolises vision, courage, and greatness
🐆 Anyam (Leopard) — one of the most spiritually significant animals in Tiv culture; invoked in Igbe anti-witchcraft rites for spiritual authority and protection
🐊 Ambe (Crocodile) — used in protective spiritual charms and symbolic objects
🦌 Ihyo (Antelope) — linked to invisibility, fortune, and divination practices
🐦 Kianpee (Hornbill) — associated with communication, alertness, and spiritual belief
🐇 Alom (Hare) — the trickster figure in Tiv oral tradition. "Atseree wua alom." — Trickery killed the hare.

Every animal has a story. Every story has a lesson.

Explore Tiv animals → https://www.tivheritage.com/archive/animals

POST 8 — Dictionary / Words (with live examples)
─────────────────────────────────────────
The Tiv language is rich, tonal, and expressive. Here are some words from our archive:

💬 Aondo — God
💬 Iveren — Blessing
💬 Ishima — Heart / Mind ("Ishima yam ngi wang." — My heart is troubled.)
💬 Tar — Land / Country ("Tar Tiv u doo." — The Tiv land is good.)
💬 Kwase — Woman ("Kwase wam doo." — My wife is good.)
💬 Doo — Good / Beautiful ("A doo kpishi." — It is very good.)
💬 Wua — Guinea corn ("Wua wua er ruam." — Too much guinea corn makes pounded yam.)
💬 Ungwa — To hear / understand ("M ungwa Tiv." — I understand Tiv.)
