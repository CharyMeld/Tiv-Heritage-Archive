# Research batch 075 — Bauchi: the languages

Researched 2026-10-01. Phase 3, the first Bauchi batch. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## Summary

- **47 languages** from Blench's Atlas, 46 with Glottocodes. They go under the existing West Chadic, Jarawan, Kainji, Plateau and Tarokoid branches.
- **Bauchi is one of Nigeria's densest language areas.** Most of these are small Chadic and Jarawan Bantu languages of Toro, Darazo, Bauchi and Ningi LGAs.
- **9 existing languages get Bauchi links:** Izere, Jere, Lemoro, Tunzu, Vaghat–Ya, Zari, Bole, Ngamo and Karekare.
- **Gombe** was created from Bauchi in 1996. Entries placed only in today's Gombe LGAs (Jara, Kutto, Kwaami) are left for a Gombe batch, and Dukku and Nafada parts are not linked.
- **Sensitive names:** the Atlas notes that the 'Saya' names for the Zaar are now considered derogatory, so they are **not recorded**.
- **130 links** and **88 other names**.
- **11 LGAs have no language placed in them by name.** Most were created later, and the northern LGAs are largely Hausa- and Fulfulde-speaking. This is listed as a gap.

## The languages

**Bure** (bure1242). Bure is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include BuBure. It is spoken in a single village south-east of Darazo town.

**Ɓeele** (beel1236). Ɓeele is a language spoken in Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Bele, Àɓéelé, Bellawa. The Atlas places it in Bauchi State without naming an LGA. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 120 (Temple 1922); a few villages.

**Ciwogai** (ciwo1236). Ciwogai is a language spoken in Ningi and Darazo LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Bade–Warji major group, Warji group). Other names include Tsagu. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,000 (Skinner 1977).

**Damlanci** (no Glottocode). Damlanci is a language spoken in Alkaleri LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Bantu: Jarawan). Other names include Damlawa. It is spoken at Maccido village. The Atlas notes that the ethnic population is 500 to 1,000 but that the language is now spoken by those over fifty, although it is not moribund. No Glottolog entry was found.

**Das** (dass1243). Das is a language spoken in Toro and Dass LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group). It is a cluster of Lukshi, Durr–Baraza, Zumbul, Wandi and Dot (Zoɗi); Dot, a single large village south of Bauchi on the Dass road, borrows heavily from Hausa but appears to be thriving. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 8,830 (1971); Durr–Baraza 4,700 (1971) or 30,000–40,000 (Caron 2005). Glottolog lists the language as Dass (dass1243).

**Daza** (daza1244). Daza is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group). It is spoken in a few villages. The Atlas has no further data. It is not to be confused with Daza (Dazaga), a Saharan name the Atlas gives for Teda.

**Deno** (deno1239). Deno is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). The Atlas places it 45 km north-east of Bauchi town. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 9,900 (1971).

**Diri** (diri1259). Diri is a language spoken in Ningi and Darazo LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Bade–Warji major group, Warji group). Other names include Diriya, Dirya, Sago. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,750 (1971).

**Dulbu** (dulb1238). Dulbu is a language spoken in Bauchi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Bantu: Jarawan, Lábír group). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 80 (1971).

**Fyandigeri** (gera1246). Fyandigeri is a language spoken in Bauchi and Darazo LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Fyandigere. It is spoken in at least thirty villages, though the Atlas notes that many Gera villages no longer speak it. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 13,300 (1971). Glottolog lists it as Gera (gera1246).

**Galambu** (gala1264). Galambu is a language spoken in Bauchi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Galembi, Galambe. It is spoken in at least fifteen villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 8,505 (Temple 1922), 2,020 (Meek 1925) and 1,000 (SIL).

**Gamo–Ningi** (gamo1241). Gamo–Ningi is a language spoken in Ningi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Kainji: Eastern Kainji, Northern Jos group). Other names include tì-Gamo. It is a cluster of Gamo and Ningi; the Atlas notes that most of its people speak Hausa. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 15,000.

**Geji** (geji1246). Geji is a language spoken in Toro and Bauchi LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group). Other names include Kayauri. It is a cluster of Mәgang (Bolu), Pyaalu (Pelu), Geji (Gyaazә) and Buu (Zaranda); the Geji member is spoken in about twenty villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 650 (1971) to 1,000 (Caron 2005) for Geji itself. A reading and writing book appeared in 2006 and New Testament extracts in 2007. Glottolog groups the cluster as Gejic (geji1246).

**Geruma** (geru1240). Geruma is a language spoken in Toro and Darazo LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Gerema, Germa, Geerum. It is spoken in at least ten villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 4,700 (1971).

**Giiwo** (giiw1236). Giiwo is a language spoken in Alkaleri, Bauchi and Darazo LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Kirifi, Bu Giiwo. It is spoken in 24 villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,620 (Temple 1922) and 14,000 (SIL).

**Guruntum–Mbaaru** (guru1271). Guruntum–Mbaaru is a language spoken in Bauchi and Alkaleri LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group, Guruntum subgroup). Other names include Gurutum, Gùrduŋ. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 10,000 (Jaggar 1988).

**Guus** (sigi1234). Guus is a language spoken in Tafawa Balewa LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group). Other names include Sigidi, Sugudi. It is spoken west of Tafawa Balewa town, in seventeen villages, close to Zaar. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 775 (1950). Glottolog lists it as Sigidi.

**Gwa** (gwaa1239). Gwa is a language spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Bantu: Jarawan). The Atlas cites speaker figures of fewer than 1,000 (1971).

**Gyem** (gyem1238). Gyem is a language spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Kainji: Eastern Kainji, Northern Jos group). Other names include Gema. It is spoken in Lame district. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,000 (estimate 2015).

**Jar** (jarr1236). Jar is a language spoken in Dass, Bauchi and Toro LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Bantu: Jarawan). Other names include Jarawa, Ɓankal. It is a cluster spread over Plateau, Bauchi and Adamawa states; its Zhar (Ɓankal) member is spoken from Dass town north to Bauchi town, west of the Gongola River, in Dass, Bauchi and Toro LGAs. The Atlas notes that 'Jarawa' is a Hausa name used for many language groups. The Atlas cites speaker figures of Zhar 20,000 (1971). A Zhar reading and writing book appeared in 2006 and New Testament extracts in 2007. It is not to be confused with Mbat, another Jarawan language for which 'Jar' is also recorded as a name.

**Jimi** (jimi1255). Jimi is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 250 (1971) and 400 (SIL 1973).

**Ju** (juuu1243). Ju is a language spoken in Bauchi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group, Guruntum subgroup). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 150 (1971).

**Kariya** (kari1316). Kariya is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Bade–Warji major group, Warji group). Other names include Kauyawa, Keriya, Vinahә. It is spoken at Kariya Wuro, 30 km south-east of Ningi. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,200 (1971) and 3,000 (Skinner 1977).

**Kir–Balar** (kirb1236). Kir–Balar is a language spoken in Bauchi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group, Boghom subgroup). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 360 for Kir (1971).

**Kubi** (kubi1239). Kubi is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Kuba. It is spoken 40 km north-east of Bauchi town. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,090 (Temple 1922) and 500 (SIL 1973).

**Kudu–Camo** (kudu1241). Kudu–Camo is a language spoken in Ningi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Kainji: Eastern Kainji, Northern Jos group, Ningi cluster). Other names include Kuda. The Atlas describes the language as moribund, perhaps extinct.

**Labɨr** (labi1245). Labɨr is a language spoken in Bauchi and Alkaleri LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Bantu: Jarawan). Other names include Lábɨ̀r. It is spoken south of the Bauchi–Gombe road, from the Gongola River at Kanyallo, in Bauchi LGA, to Gar in Alkaleri LGA, in around ten villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of perhaps 5,000 (2019 estimate).

**Lame** (lame1257). Lame is a language spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Bantu: Jarawan). Other names include Rufu. It is a cluster of Ruhu, Mbaru and Gura, spoken in Lame district. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,000 (SIL 1973).

**Lere** (lere1241). Lere is a language spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Kainji: Eastern Kainji, Northern Jos group). It is a cluster of Si, Gana and Takaya; the Atlas records Gana and Takaya as extinct and the cluster's languages as extinct. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 765 (1949) and 1,000 (SIL 1973).

**Mangas** (mang1416). Mangas is a language spoken in Bauchi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group, Boghom subgroup). Other names include Maás. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 180 (1971).

**Mburku** (mbur1239). Mburku is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Warji group). Other names include Barko, Barke, Vә Mvәran. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 210 (1949–50) and 4,000 (Skinner 1977).

**Miya** (miya1266). Miya is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Warji group). Other names include Muya. It is spoken at Miya town and its hamlets, in Ganjuwa district (the Atlas gives Darazo LGA; Ganjuwa is now an LGA of its own). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 5,200 (1971).

**Pa'a** (paaa1242). Pa'a is a language spoken in Ningi and Darazo LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Bade–Warji major group, Warji group). Other names include Paha, Afa, FuCaka. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 8,500 (1971) and 20,000 (Skinner 1977).

**Polci** (polc1245). Polci is a language spoken in Bauchi and Toro LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group). It is a cluster of Zul, Mbaram, Dir, Buli, Langas, Luri and Polci; Zul, spoken in about fifteen villages, is mutually intelligible with Mbaram. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 6,150 or more (1971). Glottolog groups the cluster as Polcic (polc1245).

**Sanga** (sang1329). Sanga is a language spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Kainji: Eastern Kainji, Northern Jos group). Other names include Aŋma Asanga. It is spoken in Lame district. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,700 (1950) and 5,000 (SIL 1973). Glottolog lists it as Sanga (Nigeria).

**Shall–Zwall** (shal1242). Shall–Zwall is a language spoken in Dass LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Plateau: Beromic). It is a cluster of Shall and Zwall.

**Shau** (shau1238). Shau is a language spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Kainji: Eastern Kainji, Northern Jos group). Other names include Sho, Lìsháù. It is spoken in the villages of Shau and Mana, and the Atlas describes it as almost extinct.

**Shɨkɨ** (shik1242). Shɨkɨ is a language spoken in Bauchi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Bantu: Jarawan). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 300 (1971). Glottolog lists it as Shiki.

**Siri** (siri1278). Siri is a language spoken in Darazo and Ningi LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Bade–Warji major group, Warji group). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,000 (1971) and 3,000 (Skinner 1977).

**Sur** (surr1238). Sur is a language spoken in Dass LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Tarokoid). Other names include Suru, Tapshin. It is spoken in the villages of Tapshin and Myet.

**Tala** (tala1295). Tala is a language spoken in Bauchi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group, Guruntum subgroup). It is spoken in Zungur district.

**Tangale** (nucl1696). Tangale is a language spoken in Alkaleri LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole–Tangale group). Other names include Tangle, Táŋlɛ̀. The Atlas places it mainly in Gombe State (Kaltungo and Akko LGAs), and also in Alkaleri LGA of Bauchi State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 36,000 (1952) and 100,000 (SIL 1973).

**Warji** (warj1253). Warji is a language spoken in Darazo and Ningi LGAs of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Bade–Warji major group, Warji group). Other names include Sәrzakwai, Sirzakwai. It is spoken in Ganjuwa district (the Atlas gives Darazo LGA) and in Warji district of Ningi LGA (Warji is now an LGA of its own), and also in Birnin Kudu LGA of Jigawa State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 28,000 (1971) and 50,000 (Skinner 1977). Glottolog lists it as Warji-Gala (warj1253).

**Zaar** (saya1246). Zaar is a language spoken in Tafawa Balewa LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group). Other names include Za'r, Zar, Vìk Zaar, Vigzar. It is spoken west of Tafawa Balewa town; its dialects are Kal, Gambar Leere and Lusa. The Atlas records other names for the people but notes that the 'Saya' names are now considered derogatory; they are not recorded here. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 50,000 (1971 and SIL 1973). A newsletter and a reading and writing book have been published since 2004–2006, and New Testament extracts in 2007. Glottolog treats Zaar as a dialect of a language it lists as saya1246.

**Zangwal** (zang1255). Zangwal is a language spoken in Bauchi LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group, Guruntum subgroup). The Atlas has no further data.

**Zeem–Caari–Danshe–Dyarim** (zeem1242). Zeem–Caari–Danshe–Dyarim is a language spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Zaar group). It is a cluster whose Zeem, Tule and Danshe members are extinct; Dyarim, whose main settlement is about 7 km south of Toro town, has about 2,000 ethnic Dyarim but only about 100 fluent speakers, and is threatened by a switch to Hausa. The Atlas cites speaker figures of Caari 'a few hundred' (Caron 2005).

**Zumbun** (zumb1240). Zumbun is a language spoken in Darazo LGA of Bauchi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West B: Warji group). Other names include Jimbin, Vina Zumbun. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,500 (1971).

## Research gaps

- **Bauchi: LGAs with no language placed by name.** Bogoro, Damban, Ganjuwa, Giade, Itas/Gadau, Jama'are, Katagum, Kirfi, Shira, Warji and Zaki have no Atlas language placed in them by name; most were created after the Atlas's sources, and the northern LGAs are largely Hausa- and Fulfulde-speaking, which the Atlas does not place by LGA.
- **Bauchi: Hausa and Fulfulde.** The Atlas names Bauchi among the states where Hausa is a first language, but Hausa is not yet a language record.
- **Bauchi: Sheni–Ziriya–Kere.** The Atlas places the Ziriya member in Toro LGA (moribund/extinct); not recorded.
- **Bauchi: Damlanci.** No Glottolog entry found.
- **Bauchi: homonyms.** Daza (Bauchi, Chadic) shares its name with Daza, an alias of Teda (Saharan); Jar (Jarawan cluster) shares its name with an alias of Mbat. Disambiguating notes added; the QC lists them as checks.
- **Bauchi: sensitive names.** The Atlas notes that the 'Saya' names (Saya, Sayawa, Seya, Seiyara) are now considered derogatory; they are not recorded. 'Ɓarawa' is a collective Hausa name for several Zaar-group peoples; not recorded as a language name.
