# Research batch 170 — Kaduna LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-07. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Kaduna. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **23 LGA descriptions** and **23 headquarters places** (Doka and Makera, parts of Kaduna city, as settlements). Slugs end in '-kaduna'.
- **Agreement on headquarters:**
  - Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC agree for 17 LGAs.
  - **Igabi** (Turunku), **Kudan** (Hunkuyi), **Sabon Gari** and **Zaria**: Statoids and INEC agree; Wikipedia names no headquarters.
  - **Kaduna North** (Doka) and **Kaduna South** (Makera): Statoids and Wikipedia agree; INEC's offices are elsewhere in Kaduna city.
  - **Sanga**: Gbantu (Wikipedia's form) is the record name, with Gwantu (Statoids, INEC) kept as the Hausa name. **Soba**: Maigana, with Statoids' Maigama kept.
- **Each description** names the peoples Wikipedia gives for the LGA (batch 166c) and any seat of a ruler or monument from batches 168–169.
- **Figures:** 2006 population and area from Statoids.
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 23 descriptions

**Birnin Gwari** (69 words). Birnin Gwari is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Birnin Gwari (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 252,363 at the 2006 census and an area of about 6,185 km² (Statoids). Birnin Gwari is the seat of the Birnin Gwari Emirate, raised to a full emirate in 1981, and the area has suffered repeated attacks by bandits (Wikipedia).

**Chikun** (72 words). Chikun is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Kujama (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 368,250 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,645 km² (Statoids). It takes its name from a Gbagyi village and was originally populated by the Gbagyi; Kajuru LGA was carved out of it in 1997 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Gbagyi among its people.

**Giwa** (48 words). Giwa is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Giwa (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 286,427 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,066 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives its creation as 15 September 1991.

**Igabi** (61 words). Igabi is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Turunku (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 430,229 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,727 km² (Statoids). Rigasa, one of the most populous wards in the state, is in the LGA (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Gbagyi among its people.

**Ikara** (48 words). Ikara is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Ikara (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 193,926 at the 2006 census and an area of about 853 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Hausa and Fulani among its peoples.

**Jaba** (86 words). Jaba is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Kwoi (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). INEC writes the town as Ngarsu-Kwoi. It had a population of 155,377 at the 2006 census and an area of about 368 km² (Statoids). It is named after the Hausa name for the Ham, who occupy most of it; Nok, which gave its name to the Nok culture, is a ward of the LGA (Wikipedia; INEC). Wikipedia names the Ham and Idun among its peoples.

**Jema'a** (83 words). Jema'a is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Kafanchan (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 278,735 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,661 km² (Statoids). Kafanchan is the seat of the Jema'a Emirate; the Fantswam, the people of Kafanchan, have had their own chiefdom since 2001 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Atyap, Bajju, Fantswam, Ham, Gwong, Nikyob, Nindem, Numana, Kanufi, Idun, Hausa, Fulani, Nyankpa and Berom among its peoples.

**Kachia** (65 words). Kachia is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Kachia (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 244,274 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,632 km² (Statoids). Zangon Kataf LGA was created from it in 1989 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Bajju, Fantswam, Ham, Bakulu, Hausa, Gbagyi, Adara and Koro among its peoples.

**Kaduna North** (59 words). Kaduna North is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Doka (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). INEC's LGA office is at Magajin Gari, Kaduna. It had a population of 357,694 at the 2006 census and an area of about 72 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia calls it the pioneer local government of Kaduna, the state capital.

**Kaduna South** (67 words). Kaduna South is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Makera (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). INEC's LGA office is at Barnawa, Kaduna. It had a population of 402,390 at the 2006 census and an area of about 59 km² (Statoids). It is part of the Kaduna metropolis (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Atyap, Bajju, Ham, Hausa, Gbagyi and Adara among its peoples.

**Kagarko** (61 words). Kagarko is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Kagarko (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 240,943 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,864 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Batinor (Koro) as its dominant group. Wikipedia names the Ham, Hausa, Gbagyi, Adara and Koro among its peoples.

**Kajuru** (60 words). Kajuru is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Kajuru (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 110,868 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,464 km² (Statoids). It was carved out of Chikun LGA in March 1997 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Gbagyi and Adara among its peoples.

**Kaura** (75 words). Kaura is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Kaura (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 222,579 at the 2006 census and an area of about 485 km² (Statoids). It was carved out of Jema'a LGA; Kagoro, seat of the Agworok chiefdom and of the Afan festival, is in the LGA (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Atyap, Agworok, Asholio, Takad, Atyecarak and Fantswam among its peoples.

**Kauru** (61 words). Kauru is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Kauru (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 170,008 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,810 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Atyap, Atsam, Abin, Abisi, Akurmi, Amala, Anu, Avori, Koonu, Ngmgbang, Dungi, Kaivi, Hausa and Irigwe among its peoples.

**Kubau** (49 words). Kubau is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Anchau (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 282,045 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,505 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Hausa and Fulani among its peoples.

**Kudan** (38 words). Kudan is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Hunkuyi (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 138,992 at the 2006 census and an area of about 400 km² (Statoids).

**Lere** (84 words). Lere is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Saminaka (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 331,161 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,158 km² (Statoids). Saminaka was the headquarters of the former Saminaka LGA, which Roger Blench's Atlas uses for many of the area's languages (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Akurmi, Amala, Avori, Koonu, Dungi, Agbiri, Aniragu, Arumaruma, Avono, Anaseni, Tumi, Kuzamani, Hausa, Fulani, Amo and Zaar among its peoples.

**Makarfi** (48 words). Makarfi is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Makarfi (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 146,259 at the 2006 census and an area of about 541 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Hausa and Fulani among its peoples.

**Sabon Gari** (68 words). Sabon Gari is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Sabon Gari (according to Statoids and INEC). INEC writes the town as Sabon Gari-Zaria. It had a population of 286,871 at the 2006 census and an area of about 263 km² (Statoids). It is one of the LGAs that make up the city of Zaria, and was created on 27 August 1991 (Wikipedia).

**Sanga** (65 words). Sanga is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Gbantu (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 149,333 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,256 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives Gbantu, with the Hausa form Gwantu, which Statoids and INEC use. Wikipedia names the Ayu, Numana, Ningeshe, Mada and Ninzam among its peoples.

**Soba** (54 words). Soba is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Maigana (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 293,270 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,234 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes Maigama. The Habe mosque at Maigana is a declared national monument (NCMM).

**Zangon Kataf** (81 words). Zangon Kataf is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Zonkwa (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 316,370 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,668 km² (Statoids). It was created from Kachia LGA in 1989; the palace of the Agwatyap, head of the Atyap Chiefdom, is at Atak Njei in the LGA (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Atyap, Bajju, Atyecarak, Fantswam, Anghan, Bakulu and Hausa among its peoples.

**Zaria** (72 words). Zaria is a local government area of Kaduna State with its headquarters at Zaria (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 408,198 at the 2006 census and an area of about 300 km² (Statoids). Zaria, formerly Zazzau, one of the original seven Hausa city-states, is the seat of the Zazzau Emirate; its city walls are a declared national monument (Wikipedia; NCMM). Wikipedia names the Hausa among its people.

## Research gaps

- **Kaduna: headquarters of Igabi, Kudan, Sabon Gari and Zaria.** Wikipedia names no headquarters for these LGAs; Statoids and INEC agree (Turunku, Hunkuyi, Sabon Gari, Zaria).
- **Kaduna: Kaduna North and Kaduna South.** Statoids and Wikipedia give Doka and Makera; INEC's offices are at Magajin Gari and Barnawa. All are parts of Kaduna city; the city itself (place #54) is not set as either LGA's headquarters.
- **Kaduna: peoples of the northern LGAs.** No source read names the peoples of Birnin Gwari, Giwa, Kudan, Sabon Gari, Soba or Kaduna North; their descriptions do not name peoples.
