# Research batch 069 — Yobe: the languages

Researched 2026-10-01. Phase 3, the first Yobe batch. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## Summary

- **10 languages**, all from Blench's Atlas, each with a Glottocode:
  - the **Bade group**: Bade, Ɗuwai and Ngizim
  - the **Bole group**: Bole (Fika), Ngamo, Karekare, Kutto and Maaka
  - **two Arabic varieties**: Uled Suliman (Libyan) and Baggara (Sudanese)
  They go under the existing West Chadic and Semitic branches.
- **Method:** 28 Atlas entries mentioned Yobe or its LGAs; all were read by hand.
  - Removed as false matches: classification labels, Bauchi-only entries, and three extinct Jigawa languages.
  - **The Atlas writes 'Borno State'** for Bade, Fika and Damaturu (Yobe has been a separate state since 1991). Those entries are linked to Yobe, quoting the Atlas.
- **30 links** and **23 other names**.
- **Existing languages:** Kanuri gets six Yobe LGAs, and Shuwa Arabic gets a state-level link.
- **Nine LGAs have no language placed in them by name.** Most were created after the Atlas's sources; this is listed as a gap.

## The languages

**Bade** (bade1248). Bade is a language spoken in Bade LGA of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West branch B: Bade/Warji major group, Bade group). Other names include Bedde, Gidgid. It is also spoken in Hadejia LGA of Jigawa State. Its dialects are Western Bade (Magwaram), Southern Bade (Bade k-Aɗo) and Gashua Bade (Mazgarwa). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 31,933 including Ɗuwai and Ngizim (1952) and 100,000 (SIL 1973). Folktales were published in 1975.

**Ɗuwai** (duwa1244). Ɗuwai is a language spoken in Bade LGA of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West branch B: Bade/Warji major group, Bade group). Other names include Duwai, Eastern Bade.

**Ngizim** (ngiz1242). Ngizim is a language spoken in Damaturu LGA of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West branch B: Bade/Warji major group, Bade group). Other names include Ngezzim. The Atlas describes it as vigorous, with Hausa as a second language. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 39,200 including Bade and Ɗuwai (1952) and 25,000 (Schuh 1972).

**Bole** (nucl1695). Bole is a language spoken in Fika LGA of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West branch A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Bòò Pìkkà, Bopika, Fika, Piika, Bolanci. It is also spoken in Bauchi State (Dukku, Alkaleri and Darazo LGAs, as the Atlas gives them). Its dialects are Bara and Fika. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 32,000 (1952) and more than 100,000 (1990). Glottolog lists it as Bole (nucl1695).

**Ngamo** (ngam1282). Ngamo is a language spoken in Fika LGA of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West branch A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Gamo. It is also spoken in Darazo and Dukku LGAs, which the Atlas gives under Bauchi State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 17,800 (1952).

**Karekare** (kare1348). Karekare is a language spoken in Fika LGA of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West branch A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Kerekere, Karaikarai, Kerikeri. It is also spoken in Gamawa and Misau LGAs of Bauchi State. Its dialects are Western Jalalum, northern Pakaro and eastern Ngwajum. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 39,000 (1952).

**Kutto** (kutt1236). Kutto is a language spoken in Gujba LGA of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West branch A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Kupto, Kúttò. It is spoken in two villages, and also in Bajoga LGA, which the Atlas gives under Bauchi State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,000 (1990 estimate).

**Maaka** (maak1236). Maaka is a language spoken in Gujba LGA of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Chadic (West branch A: Bole–Ngas major group, Bole group). Other names include Magha, Maga, Maha. The Atlas places it at Gulani and Bara towns and their hamlets, north-east of the Dadin Kowa Reservoir, and names two dialects, Maaka (at Gulani) and Maha (at Vara). The Atlas cites speaker figures of more than 4,000 (1990).

**Uled Suliman Arabic** (liby1240). Uled Suliman Arabic is a language spoken in Geidam and Yunusari LGAs of Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Afroasiatic (Semitic), the Uled Suliman member of its Arabic cluster. Other names include Libyan Arabic, Arabiyye. The Atlas also names Mober (Mobbar LGA, Borno). The Uled Suliman, formerly seasonal migrants, are now based in north-eastern Borno, and their migrations extend south into Yobe and Jigawa as far as the Hadejia–Nguru wetlands; they are also in Chad and Niger. The Atlas cites speaker figures of perhaps 20,000 regularly moving through Nigeria. The Atlas has no data on the Nigerian variety. Glottolog lists Libyan Arabic as liby1240.

**Baggara Arabic** (suda1236). Baggara Arabic is a language spoken in Yobe State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Afroasiatic (Semitic), the Baggara member of its Arabic cluster. Other names include Sudanese Arabic, Arabiyye. The Atlas places it in Yobe State without naming an LGA; it is also spoken in Chad and Sudan. The Atlas has no data on the Nigerian variety and notes that the impact of the Boko Haram insurgency is unknown. Glottolog's Sudanese Arabic (suda1236) is linked, as the Atlas gives that name.

## Research gaps

- **Yobe: LGAs with no language placed by name.** Bursari, Gulani, Jakusko, Karasuwa, Machina, Nangere, Potiskum, Tarmua and Yusufari have no Atlas language placed in them by name; most were created after the Atlas's sources (Gulani appears as a town for Maaka). A current source is needed.
- **Yobe: Maaka and Gulani.** The Atlas places Maaka in 'Gujba LGA. Gulani and Bara towns'; Gulani is now a separate LGA. Linked to Gujba as written.
- **Yobe: Hausa and Fulfulde.** Widely spoken but not placed in Yobe by name in the Atlas; Hausa is not yet a language record.
- **Yobe: Baggara Arabic.** Placed in 'Yobe State' only. Glottolog's Sudanese Arabic code is used because the Atlas gives that name; the Nigerian variety is undocumented.
