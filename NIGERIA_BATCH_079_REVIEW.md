# Research batch 079 — Bauchi LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-02. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Bauchi. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **20 LGA descriptions**, filling fields that were empty.
- **19 headquarters towns**, created as places. Bauchi reuses the existing place.
- **Figures:** the 2006 population and area of each LGA, from Statoids.
- **Sources for the headquarters:** Statoids, INEC's LGA offices that sit by the LGA secretariat, and Wikipedia. INEC's office list is saved in the repo.
- **Tafawa Balewa → Bununu (*reported*):** Wikipedia says the headquarters moved there in 2011, and INEC's office is there. Statoids still gives Tafawa Balewa.
- **Shira → Yana:** Statoids and Wikipedia agree; INEC's office is at Shira town, which the text says.
- **Conflicting figures** (Jama'are, Toro, Bogoro, Ganjuwa) are noted in the text or statistics notes; Statoids' figures are used.
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 20 descriptions

**Alkaleri** (101 words). Alkaleri is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Alkaleri (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 329,424 at the 2006 census and an area of about 5,918 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives its districts as Pali, Duguri and Gwana, with the headquarters in Pali district, and names the Fulani as the main group, with Kanuri, Dugurawa, Guruntawa and Labur (Jaku) people. The Duguri Emirate, created in 2025 (Premium Times), has its seat at Yuli in the LGA. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bolewa, Duguri and Jaku.

**Bauchi** (121 words). Bauchi is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Bauchi (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 493,810 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,687 km² (Statoids). Bauchi is the capital of Bauchi State and the seat of the Bauchi Emirate; Wikipedia says Yakubu founded the town in 1809 and built walls about 10.5 km round. The National Museum Bauchi and the Mausoleum of Abubakar Tafawa Balewa, a proposed national monument, are in the city, and Wikipedia's copy of the monuments list places the Shadawanka rock paintings at Bauchi. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bankal, Duguri, Gera, Jaku, Mbat, Pa'a and Zul.

**Bogoro** (72 words). Bogoro is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Bogoro (according to Statoids, INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 84,215 at the 2006 census and an area of about 894 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says the Zaar, also called Sayawa, are the majority of its people, and gives an area of 834.5 km². The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Zaar.

**Dambam** (98 words). Dambam is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Dambam (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 150,922 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,077 km² (Statoids). Statoids and the archive spell the LGA Damban; Wikipedia, INEC and the 2025 emirate list write Dambam. Wikipedia gives it two districts, Dagauda and Jalam, and says it borders Darazo, Misau, Katagum and Gamawa. Dambam is the seat of the Dambam Emirate, created in 2025 (Premium Times). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Karekare.

**Darazo** (94 words). Darazo is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Darazo (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 251,597 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,015 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Fulani, Hausa and Karai-karai as its main peoples and the Zumbun language at Jimbim; Ganjuwa LGA was carved out of Darazo in September 1991 (Wikipedia). Darazo is the seat of the Darazo Emirate, created in 2025 (Premium Times). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bolewa and Ngamo.

**Dass** (90 words). Dass is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Dass (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 89,943 at the 2006 census and an area of about 535 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia records settlement around Mbula hill before the jihad and the arrival of the Jarawa there in the early 19th century; the third-class chiefdom the colonial government created in 1913 is now the first-class Dass Emirate. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bankal, Gwak, Zaar and Zul.

**Gamawa** (83 words). Gamawa is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Gamawa (according to Statoids, INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 286,388 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,925 km² (Statoids). It borders Yobe State; Wikipedia names the Fulani and, in the east, the Karai-Karai as its main peoples. Gamawa is the seat of the Gamawa Emirate, created in 2025 (Premium Times). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Karekare.

**Ganjuwa** (109 words). Ganjuwa is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Kafin Madaki (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 280,468 at the 2006 census and an area of about 5,059 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also at Kafin Madaki. Wikipedia says the LGA was carved out of Darazo in September 1991 and that the Madaki of Bauchi, a kingmaker of the Bauchi Emirate, is its District Head; it gives a 2006 population of 280,486. Gidan Madaki, a declared national monument, and Sumu Wildlife Park are in the LGA. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Gera.

**Giade** (85 words). Giade is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Giade (according to Statoids). It had a population of 156,969 at the 2006 census and an area of about 668 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also at Giade. Wikipedia says it became a local government in 1996 and that Hausa and Fulfulde are the most spoken languages. Giade is the seat of the Giade Emirate, created in 2025 (Premium Times). No people is recorded for the LGA in this archive yet.

**Itas/Gadau** (98 words). Itas/Gadau is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Itas (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 229,996 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,398 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also at Itas. The main campus of Bauchi State University is at Gadau, in the east of the LGA (Wikipedia). Wikipedia's table places Gadar Maiwa, seat of the Ari Emirate created in 2025 (Premium Times), in this LGA; its LGA is given differently elsewhere. No people is recorded for the LGA in this archive yet.

**Jama'are** (106 words). Jama'are is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Jama'are (according to Statoids). It had a population of 117,883 at the 2006 census and an area of about 493 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also at Jama'are. It is the seat of the Jama'are Emirate, founded in 1811 (Wikipedia), and had the smallest area of the state's LGAs in Statoids' figures; Wikipedia gives 341.6 km² and a 2006 population of 176,883. Wikipedia names the Fulani as its most prominent people and notes the Federal College of Education Jama'are. No people is recorded for the LGA in this archive yet.

**Katagum** (81 words). Katagum is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Azare (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 295,970 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,436 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also at Azare, which has been the seat of the Katagum Emirate since 1916; the town of Katagum itself is in Zaki LGA, not in Katagum LGA (Wikipedia). No people is recorded for the LGA in this archive yet.

**Kirfi** (94 words). Kirfi is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Kirfi (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 147,618 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,371 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia calls the town Kirfin Kasa, names the Hausa as the main group, and notes the Kirfanci and Bure languages. The Kirfin Sama hill site ruins, a proposed national monument, bear the name of Kirfi, though no source read places them in the LGA. No people is recorded for the LGA in this archive yet.

**Misau** (82 words). Misau is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Misau (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 263,487 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,226 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also at Misau. It is the seat of the Misau Emirate (Wikipedia), and Wikipedia says its people are mostly Fulani and Kanuri. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani, Hausa, Kanuri, Karekare and Shuwa Arabs.

**Ningi** (115 words). Ningi is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Ningi (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 387,192 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,625 km² (Statoids). Ningi is the seat of the Ningi Emirate; Wikipedia gives the LGA three districts, Ningi, Ari and Burra, and says the Gamo-Ningi language lost its last speaker in the 1980s. Burra is the seat of the Burra Emirate, created in 2025 (Premium Times). In 2022 the state revoked land allocations made by the LGA chairman in the Lame-Burra forest reserve (ICIR). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Butawa, Pa'a and Warji.

**Shira** (73 words). Shira is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Yana (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 234,014 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,321 km² (Statoids). INEC's office, however, is at Shira town, on the Giade–Misau road. The Shira rock paintings, a declared national monument, are at Shira. No people is recorded for the LGA in this archive yet.

**Tafawa Balewa** (120 words). Tafawa Balewa is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Bununu (according to Wikipedia). It had a population of 219,988 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,515 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says the headquarters moved from Tafawa Balewa town to Bununu in 2011 because of repeated unrest, and INEC's office is at Bununu; Statoids still gives Tafawa Balewa. Abubakar Tafawa Balewa, Nigeria's first prime minister, was born at Tafawa Balewa town (Wikipedia). Bununu and Lere are seats of emirates created in 2025 (Premium Times), and the Zaar Chiefdom has its seat at Mhrim in the LGA. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bankal, Gwak, Ngas and Zaar.

**Toro** (115 words). Toro is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Toro (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 350,404 at the 2006 census and an area of about 6,932 km² (Statoids). It had the largest area of the state's LGAs in Statoids' figures; Wikipedia gives 6,705 km². Wikipedia lists three districts, Toro, Jama'a and Lame, which are also the names of three emirates created in 2025 (Premium Times), all seated in the LGA. The Dutsen Zane Geji rock paintings and the Kwandonkaya cairn, both declared national monuments, are in Toro. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bankal, Ngas, Zaar and Zul.

**Warji** (82 words). Warji is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Warji (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 114,720 at the 2006 census and an area of about 625 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also at Warji. The Warji Emirate, created in 2025 (Premium Times), has its seat at Katangar Warji; Wikipedia's article on Ningi counts Warji LGA as part of the Ningi Emirate. No people is recorded for the LGA in this archive yet.

**Zaki** (90 words). Zaki is a local government area of Bauchi State with its headquarters at Katagum (according to Statoids, INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 191,457 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,476 km² (Statoids). Katagum town gives its name to the Katagum Emirate, whose seat moved to Azare in 1916 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Fulani as the main group, with Hausa, Kanuri and Karai-Karai in the east, and notes the Bade language. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bade.

## Research gaps

- **Bauchi: LGA headquarters.** Giade and Jama'are rest on Statoids alone (INEC offices in the same towns, not by the secretariat). Shira: Yana (Statoids, Wikipedia) but INEC's office is at Shira town. Tafawa Balewa: Bununu since 2011 (Wikipedia; INEC office there), Statoids still Tafawa Balewa. An official Bauchi State list would settle them.
- **Bauchi: figures.** Jama'are 2006: Statoids 117,883 / 493 km², Wikipedia 176,883 / 341.6 km²; Toro area 6,932 vs 6,705 km²; Bogoro 894 vs 834.5 km²; Ganjuwa 280,468 vs 280,486; Wikipedia's Zaki area copies Katagum's. Statoids recorded.
- **Bauchi: peoples per LGA.** No people is linked yet to Giade, Itas/Gadau, Jama'are, Katagum, Kirfi, Shira or Warji; the Warji people are linked to Ningi but not to Warji LGA. Needs a people–LGA source (075b).
- **Bauchi: Damban/Dambam.** The archive's LGA is 'Damban' (Statoids); Wikipedia, INEC and the 2025 emirate law write Dambam. Not renamed.
- **Bauchi: LGA creation dates.** Only Ganjuwa (September 1991, from Darazo) and Giade (1996) are dated by Wikipedia.
