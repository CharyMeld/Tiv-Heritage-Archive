# Research batch 044 — Plateau: the languages

Researched 2026-09-30. Phase 3, the first Plateau batch. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## Summary

- **43 languages** and **1 new branch**, Tarokoid (Glottolog `taro1265`), all from Blench's Atlas.
  - All 491 Atlas entries were searched, not only those containing 'Plateau State'.
  - 46 entries name Plateau State or a Plateau LGA. Every one was read by hand.
- **41 Glottocodes.** Each was matched by name or by one of the Atlas's other names, and its map point checked to lie in Plateau.
- **99 links**, to the state and to LGAs.
- **136 other names**, typed by the Atlas's key.
- **Existing records get Plateau links:**
  - **Goemai**, in Shendam. The Atlas writes 'Nasarawa State' here, but Shendam is in Plateau.
  - **Hone**, in Wase.
  - **Wapan**, in Shendam.
  - The Nasarawa **Kulere** people are now linked as speaking the new Kulere language.
- **Boundaries:** the Atlas's data predate 1996, so 'Plateau State, Lafia/Awe' is Nasarawa today and is not linked. 'Jos' and 'Langtang' are now divided LGAs, so those entries are linked to the state only.

## The languages

**Akpondu** (akpo1243). Akpondu is a language spoken in Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Alumic). The Atlas gives no LGA and names Ninzo as the second language. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1 (2005). In 2005 the last speaker was a 'rememberer' who could recall only fragments of vocabulary; the Atlas lists it as moribund or extinct.

**Aten** (eten1239). Aten is a language spoken in Barkin Ladi LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Beromic). Other names include Ten, Etien, Ganawuri, Jal. It is also spoken in Jema'a LGA of Kaduna State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 6,710 (1963 census); about 40,000 (Kjenstad 1988; Blench 2003). A literacy programme is in progress; Mark appeared in 1940, and the New Testament has been published. Glottolog lists it as Eten.

**Berom** (bero1242). Berom is a language spoken in Barkin Ladi LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Beromic). Other names include Birom, Berum, Cèn Bèrom, Afango, Akuut, Baho. The Atlas also names Jos LGA, which has since been divided into Jos North, Jos South and Jos East. It is also spoken in Jema'a LGA of Kaduna State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 54,500 (HDG); 200,000 (SIL 1985). Its dialect groups are Gyel–Kuru–Vwang, Fan–Foron–Heikpang, Bachit–Gashish, Du–Ropp–Rim–Riyom and perhaps Hoss; Nincut is treated as a separate language. An official orthography was published in 1985; Scripture portions appeared from 1916, the New Testament in 1984 and the complete Bible in 2009.

**Bo-Rukul** (boru1244). Bo-Rukul is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Southeastern group). Other names include Mabo–Barkul, Mabol, Barukul. It is spoken in Richa district. The Atlas also gives 'Kulere' as another name for it, although Kulere is a separate, Chadic language of Bokkos LGA.

**Boghom** (bogh1241). Boghom is a language spoken in Kanam LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch B: Zaar group, Boghom subgroup). Other names include Burom, Burrum, Burma, Borrom, Boghorom, Bogghom. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 9,500 (1952); 50,000 (SIL 1973). Scripture portions have appeared since 1955, and a reading and writing book in 2018.

**Cakfem–Mushere** (cakf1236). Cakfem–Mushere is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3). Other names include Chakfem, Chokfem. Mushere is spoken in about thirteen villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of Cakfem 5,000 (SIL). It has two members: Cakfem, with the dialect Jajura, and Mushere, which is sharply divided into two dialects, plus Kadim, spoken in a single village. Some literacy work is under way.

**Cara** (cara1270). Cara is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Beromic). Other names include Chara, Nfachara, Fakara, Pakara, Fachara, Terea. It is spoken in nine villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 735 (1936); 5,000 (Blench estimate, 2012).

**Che** (chee1238). Che is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Ninzic). Other names include Ce, Kuche, Rukuba, Sale, Inchazi. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 15,600 (1936); 50,000 (SIL 1973). Mark appeared in 1924 and John in 1931.

**Fɨran** (fira1238). Fɨran is a language spoken in Barkin Ladi LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Central group, South–Central subgroup: Izeric). Other names include Faran, Forom, Kwakwi. It is spoken at Kwakwi station, south of Jos. The Atlas cites speaker figures of under 1,500 (1991).

**Fyer** (fyer1241). Fyer is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A: Ron group). Other names include Fier. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,500 (1970); 10,000 (Blench 1999).

**Horom** (horo1245). Horom is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Southeastern group). Other names include Barom. It is spoken in one village and one hamlet. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 500 (SIL 1973); 1,000 (Blench 1998).

**Iguta** (igut1238). Iguta is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group). Other names include Naraguta. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,580 (HDG); 3,000 (SIL 1973).

**Izere** (izer1241). Izere is a language spoken in Jos South and Barkin Ladi LGAs of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Central). Other names include Izarek, Zarek, Jarawa. It is also spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State, and by migrants in Jema'a LGA of Kaduna State. The Atlas places the Fobur and Northeastern dialects in 'Jos LGA', since divided. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 22,000 (1971); 30,000 (1977). It is a cluster whose members include Fobur (with Shere and Jos Zarazon), Northeastern (Federe, Zendi, Fursum, Jarawan Kogi), Southern (at Forom and Gashish in Barkin Ladi LGA), Ichèn, Faishang and Ganang. Mark appeared in 1940, and a New Testament translation is under way.

**Janji** (janj1240). Janji is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group). Other names include Jenji, Tìjánjí. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 360 (1950).

**Doori** (dugu1249). Doori is a language spoken in Kanam LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Bantu (Jarawan; Jar cluster). Other names include Dõõri, Duguranci. It is spoken mainly in Alkaleri and Tafawa Balewa LGAs of Bauchi State. Earlier sources divided it into regional dialects, but the Atlas finds that all Doori speak mutually intelligible varieties. It is gradually yielding to Hausa: middle-aged people still use it, but young people no longer actively do. Glottolog lists it as Duguri.

**Mbat** (bada1258). Mbat is a language spoken in Kanam LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Bantu (Jarawan; Jar cluster). Other names include Mbada, Bat, Bada, Baɗa, Kanna, Jar. It is spoken in the north-central part of Kanam LGA, centred at Gagdi-Gum; the related Mbat-Galamkya is spoken in north-western Kanam, including Gyangyang 2 and Gidgid. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 10,000 (SIL), and 10,000 for Mbat-Galamkya. Hausa and Fulfulde are used as second languages. Glottolog lists it as Bada (Nigeria), with Mbat and Galamkya as dialects.

**Jere** (jere1244). Jere is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group). Other names include Jera, Jeere, Buji, Jengre. It is also spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State and Saminaka LGA of Kaduna State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 23,000 (SIL 1972). It is a cluster. Its Plateau members are Boze (Buji), spoken on both sides of the Jos–Zaria road directly north of Jos in three dialects (εGorong, εKɔkɔŋ and εFiru), Gusu (Sanga) and Jere proper (Ezelle); Ibunu-Lɔrɔ and Panawa are spoken in Bauchi State. Glottolog treats Boze (Buji) and Gusu as dialects of Jere.

**Koenoem** (koen1239). Koenoem is a language spoken in Shendam LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3). Other names include Kanam. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,898 (1934); 3,000 (SIL). The Atlas groups it with Tal and Pyapung in the Talic cluster.

**Kulere** (kule1247). Kulere is a language spoken in Bokkos LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A: Ron group). Other names include Akande, Tof, Richa, Kamwai, Korom Ɓoye. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 6,500 (1925); 4,933 (1943); 8,000 (SIL 1973). Its dialects are Tof, Richa and Kamwai (which includes Marahai).

**Lemoro** (lemo1242). Lemoro is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, North–central cluster). Other names include Limorro, Emoro, Anowuru. It is also spoken in Toro LGA of Bauchi State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,950 (1936).

**Map** (amoo1242). Map is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Amic). Other names include Amon, Among, Timap, Ba. It is also spoken in Saminaka LGA of Kaduna State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,550 (1950). Three reading and writing books exist, and the Atlas describes it as vigorous (Jirgi 2016). Glottolog lists it as Amo.

**Miship** (mish1244). Miship is a language spoken in Mangu and Shendam LGAs of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3). Other names include Ship, Chip, Cip. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 10,127 (1934); 6,000 (SIL). Its dialects include Longmaar and Jiɓaam.

**Mundat** (mund1334). Mundat is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A: Ron group). Glottolog lists it as Mindat.

**Mwaghavul** (mwag1236). Mwaghavul is a language spoken in Barkin Ladi and Mangu LGAs of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3). Other names include Mwahavul, Sura, Mapan, Toos. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 20,000 (1952); 40,000 (SIL 1973); current informal estimates suggest around 200,000. It is a cluster of Mwaghavul, Mupun and Takas. Primers appeared from 1912, Scripture portions from 1915 and the New Testament in 1992; the Old Testament is in progress. A grammar and a dictionary were published in 2019.

**Ngas** (ngas1240). Ngas is a language spoken in Pankshin and Kanam LGAs of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3). Other names include Nngas Ngas. The Atlas also names Langtang LGA, since divided into Langtang North and Langtang South. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 55,250 (1952). Its dialects are Hill and Plain. Scripture portions appeared from 1916 and the New Testament in 1976; Hausa is the second language.

**Pan** (kofy1242). Pan is a language spoken in Shendam, Mangu and Qua'an Pan LGAs of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3). Its members are Mernyang, Doemak and Kwagallak (Shendam LGA), Tèŋ and Shindai (Qua'an Pan LGA; Shindai in Namu District) and Jipal (Mangu LGA); two more, Bwol and Gworam, are in Lafia LGA, now in Nasarawa State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 72,946 (1963), of whom Mernyang 16,739 and Kwagallak 25,403. The people call themselves Kofyar. Glottolog lists the language as Pan (ISO kwl), with Mernyang, Doemak, Kwagallak and Jipal as dialects.

**Pe** (peee1238). Pe is a language spoken in Pankshin LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Tarokoid). Other names include Pai, Dalong. It is spoken in seven villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,511 (1934); 2,000 (SIL 1973); 5,000 (1996). Glottolog lists it as Pye.

**Pyam** (fyam1238). Pyam is a language spoken in Barkin Ladi and Mangu LGAs of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Southeastern). Other names include Fyem, Pyem, Paiem, Fem, Pem. The Atlas also names Jos LGA, which has since been divided into Jos North, Jos South and Jos East. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 7,700 (1952); 14,000 (SIL 1973). The Atlas describes it as endangered. A reading and writing book and an Android dictionary appeared in 2018. Glottolog lists it as Fyam.

**Pyapung** (pyap1239). Pyapung is a language spoken in Shendam LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3: Talic). Other names include Piapun, Pyapun. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 5,167, including a few hundred Tal speakers (1934); 10,000 (Blench estimate, 2016).

**Rigwe** (irig1241). Rigwe is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Plateau (Central, South–central subgroup). Other names include Aregwe, Irigwe, ɾȉgʷȅ, Miango, Nyango, Kwal. It is also spoken in Kauru LGA of Kaduna State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 13,500 (HDG); 40,000 (UBS 1985). Its dialects are Northern (Kwan) and Southern (Miango). The New Testament translation is complete and the Old Testament is in progress; there are some radio broadcasts in Plateau State, and the orthography is used for texting and on Facebook. The Atlas records it as not currently endangered. Glottolog lists it as Irigwe.

**Run** (ronn1241). Run is a language spoken in Bokkos and Mangu LGAs of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A: Ron group). Other names include Ron, Lis ma Run, Bokos, Batura. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 13,120 (1934); 60,000 (UBS 1985). It is a cluster: Run Bokkos (with Bokkos and Baron) and Run Daffo–Butura in Bokkos LGA, and Manguna and Mangar in Mangu LGA. Bokkos and Daffo–Butura are more closely related to each other than to Sha. A Bible translation is in progress. Glottolog lists it as Ron.

**Sha** (shaa1247). Sha is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A: Ron group). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 500 (SIL); about 1,000 (1970). The Atlas lists it with the Run (Ron) cluster, but less closely related than its other members. Glottolog lists it as Sya.

**Shagawu** (no Glottocode). Shagawu is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A: Ron group). Other names include Shagau, Nafunfia, Maleni. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 20,000 (SIL).

**Tal** (tall1250). Tal is a language spoken in Pankshin LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3: Talic cluster, with Pyapung and Koenoem). Other names include Amtul, Kwabzak. It is spoken in 52 settlements. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 9,210 (1934); 10,000 (SIL 1973); 26,000 (2014 estimate). It has six mutually intelligible dialects. A dictionary appeared in 2018, and the language is used on social media.

**Tambas** (tamb1267). Tambas is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A: Ron group). Other names include Tembis. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,000 (SIL).

**Tarok** (taro1263). Tarok is a language spoken in Wase LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Tarokoid). Other names include iTarok, Appa, Yergam, Yergum. The Atlas also names Langtang LGA, since divided into Langtang North and Langtang South. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 68,000 (1971); 140,000 (UBS 1985). Its dialects are iTarok (Plain Tarok), iZini (Hill Tarok), Sәlyәr, iTarok Oga aSa and iGyang. A primer appeared in 1915, Scripture portions from 1917 and the New Testament in 1988.

**Tel** (mont1280). Tel is a language spoken in Shendam LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3). Other names include Teel, Tehl, Baltap, Montoil, Montol. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 13,386 (1934); 20,000 (SIL 1973). Glottolog lists it as Montol.

**Tunzu** (tunz1235). Tunzu is a language spoken in Jos East LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group). Other names include Dugusa, Duguza. It is spoken in five villages of Jos East LGA and in two villages of Toro LGA, Bauchi State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,500 (Blench estimate, 2003), with perhaps another 2,000 ethnic Tunzu who do not speak it. Its speakers also use Izere, Ibunu and Hausa, and the Atlas describes it as threatened by a switch to Hausa.

**Vaghat–Ya–Bijim–Legeri** (no Glottocode). Vaghat–Ya–Bijim–Legeri is a language spoken in Mangu LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Tarokoid). Other names include Kwanka, Kadun. Its Plateau members are Kwang (Vaghat) and Legeri, in Mangu LGA; Ya and Bijim are spoken in Tafawa Balewa LGA of Bauchi State.

**Yangkam** (yang1290). Yangkam is a language spoken in Wase LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Tarokoid). Other names include Bashiri. It is spoken at Bashar town. The Atlas also names Langtang LGA, since divided into Langtang North and Langtang South. The Atlas cites speaker figures of fewer than 400 in 1996, all over 40 years old (published figures such as 20,000 in 1977 refer to the ethnic population). The Atlas notes that the community has largely shifted to Hausa.

**Ywom** (yiwo1237). Ywom is a language spoken in Shendam LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch A3). Other names include Yiwom, Gerkanci, Gurka. The Atlas also names Langtang LGA, since divided into Langtang North and Langtang South. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,520 (1934); 8,000 (SIL 1973). Reading and writing books appeared in 2011 and 2018. Glottolog lists it as Yiwom.

**Zari** (zari1242). Zari is a language spoken in Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (branch B: Zaar group). Other names include Kopti, Kwapm. It is spoken mainly in Toro and Tafawa Balewa LGAs of Bauchi State; the Atlas also names Jos LGA of Plateau State, since divided. The Atlas cites speaker figures of Zakshi 2,950 and Boto 1,000 (1950). It is a cluster of Zakshi, Boto and Zari.

**Zora** (izor1238). Zora is a language spoken in Bassa LGA of Plateau State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, North–central cluster). Other names include iZora. It is spoken in ten settlements. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 425 (1936); 19 speakers (March 2016), in an ethnic population of about 3,000–4,000. Hausa is the second language of the whole community and the first language of most of it; the Atlas describes Zora as highly endangered and not actively spoken by the younger generation. Glottolog lists it as Izora.

## Research gaps

- **Plateau: Jorto.** The Atlas lists Jorto (Shendam LGA, at Dokan Kasuwa; 4,876 in 1934; West Chadic A3) but doubts it really exists: no data have ever circulated. Not recorded.
- **Plateau: Jukun of Wase (Wase Tofa).** The Atlas's Jukun cluster has a member 'Wase Tofa' in Shendam and Langtang LGAs, with no other data. Not recorded until a source describes it.
- **Plateau: Kantana.** The Atlas lists Kantana (Jar cluster, Kanam LGA; yielding to Hausa) but also gives 'Kantana' as another name for Mama (Nasarawa). No source found separates the two; no Glottolog entry.
- **Plateau: current LGAs.** The Atlas names pre-1996 LGAs. Languages placed in 'Jos' (Berom, Pyam, Izere dialects, Zari) and 'Langtang' (Ngas, Tarok, Yangkam, Ywom, Wapan) are linked to the state only; which of Jos North/South/East and Langtang North/South needs a current source. Kanke, Mikang and Riyom have no language links yet for the same reason.
- **Plateau: Glottocodes not matched.** Shagawu (no Glottolog entry found), Vaghat–Ya–Bijim–Legeri (a family in Glottolog).
- **Plateau: Hausa and Fulfulde.** Named in the Atlas as second languages (Ngas, Mbat, Tunzu, Zora) but not yet language records.
