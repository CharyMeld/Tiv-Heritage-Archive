# Research batch 051 — Kogi: the languages

Researched 2026-09-30. Phase 3, the first Kogi batch. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **8 languages** and **3 new branches**, Yoruboid, Edoid and Ayere–Ahan, all from Blench's Atlas. Each has a Glottolog code.
- **39 links**, to states and LGAs, and **22 other names**.
- **Existing records get Kogi links:**
  - **Ebira**, in Okene, Okehi, Kogi and Bassa.
  - **Basa-Benue**, in Bassa and Ankpa.
  - The **Yoruba** people now speak the new Yoruba record.
  - **Nupe** is linked to Ibi (Taraba). This answers the Taraba gap 'Nupe at Ibi'.
- **Boundaries:** the Atlas mostly uses the pre-1991 states ('Kwara State, Kogi LGA'; 'Benue State, Ankpa LGA'). Only today's Kogi LGAs of the same name are linked. There are three *reported* links where a later LGA is named after the town the Atlas gives: Ogori/Magongo, Ibaji and Kabba/Bunu.
- **Kogi has fewer Atlas entries than Plateau or Taraba,** because it is dominated by a few large languages: Igala, Ebira, Yoruba (Okun) and Nupe.
- **Excluded:** the Arigidi cluster, whose members are all in Ondo State.

## The languages

**Igala** (igal1242). Igala is a language of Kogi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Yoruboid (East Benue–Congo). The Atlas places it in Ankpa, Dekina, Idah and Bassa LGAs (listing them under Benue State, before Kogi State was created), and also in Oshimili and Anambra areas to the south. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 295,000 (1952); 800,000 (UBS 1987). Its dialects are Ánkpa and Ògùgù (Ankpa LGA), Ìfè (Ankpa and Dekina), Ànyìgbá (Dekina), 'Idáh and Ìbàjì (Idah area) and Èbú (Oshimili). It has an official orthography; the New Testament appeared in 1935 and the Bible in 1970.

**Kakanda** (kaka1264). Kakanda is a language of Kogi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Nupoid (Nupe group). The Atlas places it in Kogi LGA and in Agaie and Lapai LGAs of Niger State, in communities along the Niger centred on Budon. Other names include Akanda, Hyabe, Adyaktye. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 4,500 (1931); 20,000 (Blench 1989). It is a cluster of Kakanda–Budon and Kakanda–Gbanmi/Sokun.

**Kupa** (kupa1238). Kupa is a language of Kogi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Nupoid (Nupe group). It is spoken in 52 villages around Abugi, in Kogi LGA.

**Nupe** (nupe1254). Nupe is a language of Kogi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Nupoid. The Atlas places Nupe mainly in Niger State (Lavun, Mariga, Gbako, Agaie and Lapai LGAs), in Edu and Kogi LGAs, in the Federal Capital Territory and, as Nupe Tako, in Bassa LGA of Kogi State; there are small but well established Nupe communities in Ibi (Taraba) and in Nasarawa State. Other names include Nife, Nyffe, Anupe, Tapa, Takpa, Nupenci. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 360,000 (1952); 1,000,000 (UBS 1987), a figure that may include closely related languages. It is a cluster of Nupe (Central), the accepted literary form, and Nupe Tako (Basa Nge). Scripture portions appeared from 1860 and the Bible in 1953. Nupe was still spoken in Brazil at the end of the nineteenth century, and was recorded in Cuba as Lucumu Tacua.

**Ọkọ–Eni–Ọsayẹn** (okoe1238). Ọkọ–Eni–Ọsayẹn is a language of Kogi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as an isolated branch of Benue–Congo (the Ọkọ–Eni–Ọsayẹn cluster). It is spoken at Ogori (Ọkọ) and Magongo (Ọsayẹn), which the Atlas places in Okene LGA, now Ogori/Magongo LGA. Other names include Oko, Uku, Ogori, Osanyin, Magongo. The Atlas cites speaker figures of about 4,000 (Ọkọ), 3,000 (Eni) and 3,000 (Ọsayẹn) (1970, uncertain). It is a cluster of three members, Ọkọ, Eni and Ọsayẹn, forming their own branch of Benue–Congo.

**Ọkpamheri** (okpa1238). Ọkpamheri is a language of Kogi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Edoid (North-Western Edoid, Southern). The Atlas places it in Akoko-Edo LGA of Edo State and in Oyi LGA of the former Kwara State, now in Kogi State. Other names include Opameri. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 18,136 (1957); 30,000 (SIL 1973). Its name means 'we are one'; its dialects are Okulosho, Western Okpamheri and Emhalhe, each with many sub-dialects named after towns.

**Uwu** (ayer1245). Uwu is a language of Kogi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Benue–Congo (Uwu–Ahan). The Atlas places it in Kabba District, in Oyi LGA of the former Kwara State. Other names include Ayere. Glottolog lists it as Ayere (ayer1245), related to Ahan in the Ayere–Ahan group.

**Yoruba** (yoru1245). Yoruba is a language of Kogi State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Yoruboid (Volta–Niger). The Atlas places Yoruba in most of Kwara, Lagos, Osun, Oyo, Ogun and Ondo states, in the western LGAs of Kogi State, and in Benin Republic and Togo; it is also a ritual language in Cuba and Brazil. Other names include Yorùbá, Yorouba, Yariba, Aku, Nago. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 5,100,000 (1952); 15,000,000 (UBS 1984). It has many dialects. The Atlas's preliminary grouping includes an Okun group of Kogi State (Yagba, Gbede and Ijumu), and names Owe and Bunu among the dialects. It has an official orthography and a written literature of more than a century; Scripture portions appeared from 1850.

## Research gaps

- **Kogi: Arigidi cluster.** The Atlas's head line says 'Ondo State, Akoko North LGA; Kwara State, Kogi LGA', but all ten members are placed in Akoko North, Ondo State. Not linked to Kogi.
- **Kogi: current LGAs.** The Atlas uses pre-1991 LGAs. Igala's presence in Olamaboro, Omala, Ofu and Igalamela-Odolu, Ebira's in Adavi and Ajaokuta, and Yoruba's in the western LGAs (Yagba East and West, Ijumu, Kabba/Bunu, Mopa-Muro, Lokoja) need a current source.
- **Kogi: Ọkpamheri and Uwu.** The Atlas names 'Oyi LGA' of the former Kwara State (Ọkpamheri) and its Kabba District (Uwu); their present LGAs are not stated.
- **Kogi: other languages.** Only the eleven Atlas entries that name Kogi or its LGAs (or their pre-1991 equivalents) were used; other languages spoken in Kogi, if any, need other sources.
