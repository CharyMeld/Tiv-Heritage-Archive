# Research batch 085 — Gombe LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-02. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Gombe. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **11 LGA descriptions**, filling fields that were empty.
- **10 headquarters towns**, created as places. Gombe reuses the existing place.
- **Figures:** the 2006 population and area of each LGA, from Statoids.
- **Sources for the headquarters:** Statoids and Wikipedia. INEC's Gombe office list was never archived, so it is not used.
- **Billiri and Dukku are *reported*:** their Wikipedia articles do not name the headquarters.
- **Conflicting figures:** Wikipedia's populations for Gombe and Balanga differ from Statoids. They are noted; Statoids' figures are used.
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 11 descriptions

**Akko** (84 words). Akko is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Kumo (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 337,853 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,627 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia calls Kumo the second-largest centre in the state after Gombe, and says the LGA has three emirates, Akko, Gona and Pindiga; Pindiga is the capital of the Pindiga Emirate. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani, Jukun and Tangale.

**Balanga** (105 words). Balanga is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Talasse (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 212,549 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,626 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes the headquarters Tallase. Wikipedia gives a population of 176,944 and names the Hausa, Fulani and Waja as its main peoples; the Dadiya and Waja chiefdoms are linked to the LGA, and Blench's Atlas places Cen Tuum, a language isolate now probably extinct, at Cham town. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bangunji, Dadiya, Lunguda, Tangale, Tsobo and Waja.

**Billiri** (86 words). Billiri is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Billiri (according to Statoids). It had a population of 202,144 at the 2006 census and an area of about 737 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says Billiri, also known as Tangle, is historically and today a major settlement of the Tangale, whose ruler, the Mai Tangle, reigns there; it describes the protests that followed the appointment of a new Mai in 2021. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Tangale.

**Dukku** (92 words). Dukku is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Dukku (according to Statoids). It had a population of 207,190 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,815 km² (Statoids). Dukku is the seat of the Dukku Emirate, which Wikipedia says was created out of the Gombe Emirate in 2001; the Gongola River flows through the west and north of the LGA, and Wikipedia names the Fulani as the main group. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bolewa and Fulani.

**Funakaye** (92 words). Funakaye is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Bajoga (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 236,087 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,415 km² (Statoids). Blench's Atlas and the National Commission for Museums and Monuments still use the older name 'Bajoga LGA'; the commission places there the Mbormi (Burmi) battle ground of 1903, a proposed national monument. Wikipedia notes limestone mining by Lafarge since the 1990s. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani.

**Gombe** (104 words). Gombe is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Gombe (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 268,000 at the 2006 census and an area of about 52 km² (Statoids). Gombe is the capital of Gombe State and the seat of the Gombe Emirate, whose capital the British moved there, then called Gombe Doma, in 1919 (Wikipedia). It is the smallest LGA in area; Wikipedia gives a 2006 population of 280,000. The National Museum Gombe and the Emir's Palace are in the city. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani.

**Kaltungo** (92 words). Kaltungo is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Kaltungo (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 149,805 at the 2006 census and an area of about 881 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia lists among its festivals the Kamo Cultural Festival and the Tangale (Dog) Festival; the Kaltungo and Tula chiefdoms are seated in the LGA, and Blench's Atlas places the Tula, Ma (Kamo) and Yebu (Awak) languages here. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Awak, Cham, Kamo, Tangale and Tula.

**Kwami** (84 words). Kwami is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Mallam Sidi (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 195,298 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,787 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia writes the headquarters both Mallam Sidi and Malam Sidi, and says the LGA is bordered on the east by Lake Dadin Kowa. Blench's Atlas places the Kwaami language here. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Kanuri.

**Nafada** (85 words). Nafada is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Nafada (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 138,185 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,586 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says Nafada lies in the traditional land of the Bole people and was the capital of the Gombe Emirate from 1913 to 1919; it is the seat of the Nafada Emirate. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bolewa and Fulani.

**Shongom** (87 words). Shongom is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Boh (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 151,520 at the 2006 census and an area of about 922 km² (Statoids). INEC, Statoids and the archive write the LGA Shomgom; Wikipedia writes Shongom and places Boh in the north of the LGA. Blench's Atlas places the Bangjinge, Burak, Goji and Pero languages here, Pero around Filiya. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Pero and Tangale.

**Yamaltu/Deba** (86 words). Yamaltu/Deba is a local government area of Gombe State with its headquarters at Deba (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 255,248 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,981 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says the LGA is inhabited mainly by Tera and Fulani people and dates Deba's history to 1375; the Deba and Yamaltu emirates and the Dadin Kowa Dam are in the LGA. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Tera.

## Research gaps

- **Gombe: LGA headquarters.** Billiri and Dukku rest on Statoids alone (their Wikipedia articles do not state the headquarters). INEC's Gombe LGA-office list, used for other states, was never archived; an official Gombe State list would settle them.
- **Gombe: figures.** Gombe LGA 2006: Statoids 268,000, Wikipedia 280,000; Balanga: Statoids 212,549, Wikipedia 176,944. Statoids recorded.
- **Gombe: Shomgom/Shongom.** INEC, Statoids and the archive write Shomgom; Wikipedia, the federal profile and Blench's Atlas write Shongom. Not renamed.
- **Gombe: LGA creation dates.** The federal profile says most LGAs were created in 1989 or 1992 and three more in 1996, without naming them.
