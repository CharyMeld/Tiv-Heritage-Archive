# Research batch 091 — Kano LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-02. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Kano. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **44 LGA descriptions**, filling fields that were empty.
- **44 headquarters towns**, created as places.
- **Figures:** the 2006 population and area of each LGA, from Statoids.
- **Sources for the headquarters:** Statoids, INEC's LGA offices in or by the secretariat, and Wikipedia. INEC's office list is saved in the repo.
- **Makoda is *reported*:** Statoids gives Makoda, but Wikipedia and INEC point to Koguna.
- **Kunchi:** Wikipedia calls the LGA 'Ghari'. This is noted; the record is not renamed.
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 44 descriptions

**Ajingi** (59 words). Ajingi is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Ajingi (according to Statoids). It had a population of 174,137 at the 2006 census and an area of about 714 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia dates the LGA to 1996. It is one of the three LGAs of the Gaya Emirate, a second-class emirate since 2024 (ICIR).

**Albasu** (62 words). Albasu is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Albasu (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 190,153 at the 2006 census and an area of about 398 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is opposite the LGA secretariat in Albasu town. It is one of the three LGAs of the Gaya Emirate since 2024 (ICIR).

**Bagwai** (64 words). Bagwai is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Bagwai (according to Statoids). It had a population of 162,847 at the 2006 census and an area of about 405 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places it in the north-west of the state, about 55 km from Kano, and notes the Watari dam and its irrigation scheme of about 1,000 hectares.

**Bebeji** (65 words). Bebeji is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Bebeji (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 188,859 at the 2006 census and an area of about 717 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Hausa and Fulani as the great majority of its people. The Habe Mosque at Bebeji is one of Kano's three declared national monuments (NCMM).

**Bichi** (67 words). Bichi is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Bichi (according to Statoids). It had a population of 277,099 at the 2006 census and an area of about 612 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says Bichi town was founded by the Danejawa, a Fulani clan of herders. Bichi was the seat of the Bichi Emirate from 2019 until it was abolished in 2024 (Wikipedia).

**Bunkure** (84 words). Bunkure is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Bunkure (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 170,891 at the 2006 census and an area of about 487 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is in the LGA secretariat. Wikipedia says the LGA was split from the former Rano LGA; it is one of the three LGAs of the Rano Emirate since 2024 (ICIR). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Hausa.

**Dala** (76 words). Dala is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Gwammaja (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 418,777 at the 2006 census and an area of about 19 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes Gwamaja. Dala lies in the north-west of the Kano metropolis; it was created in May 1989 from the old Kano Municipal LGA and takes its name from Dala Hill, after which Kano was first named (Wikipedia).

**Dambatta** (58 words). Dambatta is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Dambatta (according to Statoids). It had a population of 207,968 at the 2006 census and an area of about 732 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia also spells it Danbatta and Dambarta, and places it about 79 km north of Kano, on the border with Jigawa State.

**Dawakin Kudu** (68 words). Dawakin Kudu is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Dawakin Kudu (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 225,389 at the 2006 census and an area of about 384 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is beside the LGA secretariat. Wikipedia says 99 per cent of its people are Hausa and that it has the oldest dyeing pit in Kano State.

**Dawakin Tofa** (49 words). Dawakin Tofa is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Dawakin Tofa (according to Statoids). It had a population of 247,875 at the 2006 census and an area of about 479 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Hausa and Fulani as its two main peoples.

**Doguwa** (72 words). Doguwa is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Riruwai (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 151,181 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,473 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also in Riruwai town. It lies in the far south of the state; Wikipedia names the Falgore woodland among its features and says many of its Fulani are cattle herders.

**Fagge** (65 words). Fagge is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Waje (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 198,828 at the 2006 census and an area of about 21 km² (Statoids). Fagge lies within the Kano metropolis and was created from Nasarawa LGA on 4 December 1996; Wikipedia notes a significant Igbo population alongside the Hausa and Fulani.

**Gabasawa** (44 words). Gabasawa is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Zakirai (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 211,055 at the 2006 census and an area of about 605 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is also at Zakirai.

**Garko** (54 words). Garko is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Garko (according to Statoids). It had a population of 162,500 at the 2006 census and an area of about 450 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places it on the A237 highway and names the Fulani and Hausa as most of its people.

**Garum Mallam** (80 words). Garum Mallam is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Garum Mallam (according to Statoids). It had a population of 116,494 at the 2006 census and an area of about 214 km² (Statoids). INEC and Wikipedia write Garun Malam or Garun Mallam. Wikipedia places it on the A2 highway and names the Kano seed processing centre at Kadawa as a landmark. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Hausa.

**Gaya** (77 words). Gaya is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Gaya (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 201,016 at the 2006 census and an area of about 613 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is opposite the LGA secretariat. Wikipedia calls Gaya, a town, emirate and LGA, the oldest and most significant site in Kano's history, older than Kano itself; the Gaya Emirate has been a second-class emirate since 2024 (ICIR).

**Gezawa** (47 words). Gezawa is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Gezawa (according to Statoids). It had a population of 282,069 at the 2006 census and an area of about 340 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Hausa and Fulani as most of its people.

**Gwale** (60 words). Gwale is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Gwale (according to Statoids). It had a population of 362,059 at the 2006 census and an area of about 18 km² (Statoids). Gwale lies within greater Kano; Wikipedia notes its many Islamic scholars and that the politician Malam Aminu Kano was born in its Sudawa quarter.

**Gwarzo** (47 words). Gwarzo is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Gwarzo (according to Statoids). It had a population of 183,987 at the 2006 census and an area of about 393 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Hausa and Fulani as most of its people.

**Kabo** (68 words). Kabo is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Kabo (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 153,828 at the 2006 census and an area of about 341 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is beside the LGA secretariat. Wikipedia names the Kabo forest reserves among its sights. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Hausa.

**Kano Municipal** (73 words). Kano Municipal is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Kofar Kudu (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 365,525 at the 2006 census and an area of about 17 km² (Statoids). Its secretariat is at Kofar Kudu, the gate of the Emir's palace, Gidan Rumfa, in the south of the old city of Kano (Wikipedia); it had the smallest area of the state's LGAs.

**Karaye** (90 words). Karaye is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Karaye (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 141,407 at the 2006 census and an area of about 479 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is opposite the LGA secretariat. Karaye is the headquarters of the Karaye Emirate, a second-class emirate since 2024, and Wikipedia names the Challawa Gorge Dam there; it records the Maguzawa as the area's early inhabitants. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani, Hausa and Kanuri.

**Kibiya** (67 words). Kibiya is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Kibiya (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 136,736 at the 2006 census and an area of about 404 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is beside the LGA secretariat. Wikipedia gives a 2006 population of 189,870. It is one of the three LGAs of the Rano Emirate since 2024 (ICIR).

**Kiru** (49 words). Kiru is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Kiru (according to Statoids). It had a population of 264,781 at the 2006 census and an area of about 927 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia describes it as an agrarian area of farming, trade and rural settlements.

**Kumbotso** (45 words). Kumbotso is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Kumbotso (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 295,979 at the 2006 census and an area of about 158 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is behind the LGA secretariat.

**Kunchi** (57 words). Kunchi is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Kunchi (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 111,018 at the 2006 census and an area of about 671 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives the LGA under the name Ghari, with its headquarters at Kunchi; the archive keeps the name Kunchi.

**Kura** (48 words). Kura is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Kura (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 144,601 at the 2006 census and an area of about 206 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is inside the LGA secretariat in Kura town.

**Madobi** (36 words). Madobi is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Madobi (according to Statoids). It had a population of 136,623 at the 2006 census and an area of about 273 km² (Statoids).

**Makoda** (72 words). Makoda is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Koguna (according to INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 222,399 at the 2006 census and an area of about 441 km² (Statoids). Statoids gives Makoda as the headquarters, but Wikipedia names Koguna and INEC's office is beside the LGA secretariat at Koguna, so the headquarters is uncertain. Wikipedia says the LGA was carved out of Dambatta.

**Minjibir** (69 words). Minjibir is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Minjibir (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 213,794 at the 2006 census and an area of about 416 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is in the old LGA secretariat. Wikipedia says Minjibir, about 20 km north-east of Kano, was founded in the early 18th century by the Fulani of the Yerimawa clan.

**Nasarawa** (68 words). Nasarawa is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Bompai (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 596,669 at the 2006 census and an area of about 34 km² (Statoids). INEC and Statoids write Nassarawa. Its headquarters is the Bompai area of Kano city; Wikipedia gives a 2006 population of 678,669, the largest of the state's LGAs either way.

**Rano** (62 words). Rano is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Rano (according to Statoids). It had a population of 145,439 at the 2006 census and an area of about 520 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia counts Rano among the seven Hausa states of tradition (Hausa Bakwai); it is the seat of the Rano Emirate, a second-class emirate since 2024 (ICIR).

**Rimin Gado** (54 words). Rimin Gado is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Rimin Gado (according to Statoids). It had a population of 104,790 at the 2006 census and an area of about 225 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia also gives the name Rafin Gado and places it about 20 km west of Kano.

**Rogo** (66 words). Rogo is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Rogo (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 227,742 at the 2006 census and an area of about 802 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is beside the LGA secretariat. Rogo borders Kaduna and Katsina states (Wikipedia) and is one of the two LGAs of the Karaye Emirate since 2024 (ICIR).

**Shanono** (51 words). Shanono is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Shanono (according to Statoids). It had a population of 140,607 at the 2006 census and an area of about 697 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia traces the town to two herding brothers, Jaulere and Shanu, who came from Hadejia.

**Sumaila** (58 words). Sumaila is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Sumaila (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 253,661 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,250 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says Sumaila was founded in the 1740s as a settlement of the Jobawa Fulani, first called Garun Sam'ila.

**Takai** (52 words). Takai is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Takai (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 202,743 at the 2006 census and an area of about 598 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is near the LGA secretariat. Wikipedia places it on the A237 highway.

**Tarauni** (55 words). Tarauni is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Unguwa Uku (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 221,367 at the 2006 census and an area of about 28 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes Ungwa Uku. Tarauni lies within the city of Kano and has ten wards (Wikipedia).

**Tofa** (59 words). Tofa is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Tofa (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 97,734 at the 2006 census and an area of about 202 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is on the LGA secretariat road. It had the smallest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census.

**Tsanyawa** (45 words). Tsanyawa is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Tsanyawa (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 157,680 at the 2006 census and an area of about 492 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is beside the LGA secretariat.

**Tudun Wada** (58 words). Tudun Wada is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Tudun Wada (according to Statoids). It had a population of 231,742 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,204 km² (Statoids). Blench's Atlas places the Kurama language here, the only Kano LGA it names for a language other than Hausa.

**Ungogo** (48 words). Ungogo is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Ungogo (according to Statoids). It had a population of 369,657 at the 2006 census and an area of about 204 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places its secretariat in the north of the city of Kano.

**Warawa** (63 words). Warawa is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Warawa (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 128,787 at the 2006 census and an area of about 360 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is beside the LGA secretariat. Wikipedia says it was created out of Dawakin Kudu LGA in the early 1990s and has 15 wards.

**Wudil** (47 words). Wudil is a local government area of Kano State with its headquarters at Wudil (according to Statoids). It had a population of 185,189 at the 2006 census and an area of about 362 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places it on the A237 highway near the Hadejia River.

## Research gaps

- **Kano: LGA headquarters.** Twenty LGAs rest on Statoids alone (their INEC office is not by the secretariat and their Wikipedia article does not name the headquarters). Makoda: Statoids gives Makoda; Wikipedia and INEC point to Koguna.
- **Kano: Kunchi or Ghari.** Wikipedia gives the LGA as 'Ghari' with headquarters at Kunchi; INEC, Statoids and the archive use Kunchi. Whether the LGA has been renamed needs an official source.
- **Kano: figures.** Nasarawa 2006: Statoids 596,669, Wikipedia 678,669; Kibiya: Statoids 136,736, Wikipedia 189,870. Statoids recorded.
- **Kano: Tudun Wada article.** Wikipedia's 'Tudun Wada' is a disambiguation page; no article on the Kano LGA was found.
