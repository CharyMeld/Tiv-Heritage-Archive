# Research batch 166 — Kaduna: Kainji languages

Researched 2026-10-07. First of three Kaduna language and people batches (166 Kainji languages, 166b Plateau and other languages, 166c peoples). Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **17 new languages**, all Kainji, mostly of the Kauru subgroup north of the Jos Plateau: Atsam, Bishi, Ngmgbang, Bin, Dungu, Gbiri–Niragu, Kaivi, Kono, Mala, Nu, Ruma, Shuwa–Zamani, Tumi, Vono, Vori, Sheni, Kere.
- **Glottolog codes** for 14; none for Ngmgbang, Shuwa–Zamani and Kere (see gaps).
- **Existing records linked to Kaduna:** Kurama (Ikara; Kauru reported), Map and Jere (state), Rigwe (Kauru), Kamuku and Cicipu (Birnin Gwari).
- **LGAs linked:** birnin-gwari, ikara, kachia, kauru, lere.

## A method choice for you to check

The Atlas places most of these languages in **Saminaka LGA**, which no longer exists (Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA). Linking only by the Atlas would leave them at state level. Where INEC's 2015 polling-unit directory has a unit or ward bearing the language's own name or a name the Atlas gives for it, I linked that LGA as **reported** and wrote the match in the note (11 links). Ambiguous name matches were not used. If you prefer Atlas-only links, these 11 links can be dropped before deployment.

## The records

**Atsam.** Atsam is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: group A). The Atlas places it in Kachia LGA, as the LGA was before the 1989 splits. Other names include Tsam, Chawai, Chawe, Chawi. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 10,200 (1931); 30,000 (1972). The Atlas gives Atsam (singular Tsam) as the people's own name, and records Scripture portions from 1923 and 1932.

**Bishi.** Bishi is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: group A). The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). Its speakers live in at least twenty-six villages (2013). Other names include Abisi, Bisi, Pitti. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 1,600 (1950). The Atlas notes that Ngmgbang, formerly listed as a dialect of Bishi, is a distinct language. Glottolog lists it as Piti.

**Ngmgbang.** Ngmgbang is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: group A). The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). It is spoken in a few villages (2013). Other names include Ribam, Rigmgbang. The Atlas notes that it was formerly listed as a dialect of Bishi but is clearly a distinct language.

**Bin.** Bin is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, Kauru subgroup). The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). The Atlas places it about 15 km west of Mariri, along the Geshere road, in four villages (2016). Other names include Bina, tìBin, Bogana, Binawa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 220 (1949); 2,000 (1973); about 3,000–4,000 (2016 estimate).

**Dungu.** Dungu is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, Kauru subgroup). The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). Other names include Dungi, Dingi, Dwingi, Dunjawa. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 310 (1949).

**Gbiri–Niragu.** Gbiri–Niragu is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, Kauru subgroup). The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). Wikipedia and INEC place Gure and Kahugu in Lere LGA. Its members are Gbiri and Niragu. Other names include Gure, Gura, Kahugu, Kagu, Kafugu. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 5,000 (1952). The Atlas records a literacy programme under way in Gbiri.

**Kaivi.** Kaivi is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, Kauru subgroup). The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). Other names include Kaibi. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 650 (1949).

**Nu.** Nu is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, Kauru subgroup). The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). It is spoken in about seven villages. Other names include Tinu, Kinugu, Kinuka, Kinuku. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 460 (1949); 500 (1973); about 3,000 (2016 estimate). The Atlas describes it as vigorous.

The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). Other names include Rurama, Turuma, Bagwama. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 2,200 (1948). The Atlas notes that the name Bagwama also refers to Kurama.

**Vori.** Vori is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, Kauru subgroup). The Atlas places it in Saminaka LGA, a former LGA; Saminaka town is now the headquarters of Lere LGA (Wikipedia). Other names include TiVori, Fiti, Surubu, Zurubu, Skrubu. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 1,950 (1948).

**Sheni.** Sheni is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, group c). The Atlas places it in Lere LGA, in two settlements, Sheni and Gurjiya. Other names include Shani, Shaini, tiSeni. Speaker figures in the Atlas: 6 fluent speakers in an ethnic community of about 1,500 (2003). The Atlas's figures show the language close to extinction.

**Kere.** Kere is a language of Kaduna State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Kainji (Eastern Kainji: Northern Jos group, group c). The Atlas places it at Kere, in Lere LGA. The Atlas records it as extinct (2003).

## Research gaps

- **Kaduna: Saminaka LGA.** The Atlas places most Kauru-subgroup languages (Bishi, Ngmgbang, Bin, Dungu, Gbiri–Niragu, Kaivi, Kono, Mala, Nu, Ruma, Shuwa–Zamani, Tumi, Vono, Vori) in Saminaka LGA, which no longer exists; its area now lies at least partly in Lere and Kauru LGAs. Where an INEC polling unit carries the language's name, the LGA is linked as reported; Bishi, Ngmgbang, Dungu, Ruma, Shuwa–Zamani, Vono and Vori are linked at state level only. A current survey (e.g. Ajaegbu et al. 2013) would settle the LGAs.
- **Kaduna: Atsam and Kachia.** The Atlas places Atsam (Chawai) in Kachia LGA as it was before 1989; INEC's Fadan Chawai is in Kauru LGA. Both links are kept until a current source settles it.
- **Kaduna: Sheni's second settlement.** The Atlas places Gurjiya, Sheni's second settlement, in Lere LGA; INEC lists a polling unit Gurjiya in Kargi ward of Kubau LGA. Kubau is not linked.
- **Kaduna: glottocodes.** Ngmgbang, Shuwa–Zamani and Kere have no clearly matching Glottolog languoid; none is set. Glottolog's Kizamani (izm) may correspond to Shuwa–Zamani.
- **Kaduna: extinct and moribund languages.** The Atlas records Kere as extinct and Sheni with six fluent speakers (2003), and Kwacika (a Kamuku variety in Birnin Gwari) with one old speaker in the 1980s; their current status needs a recent source.
