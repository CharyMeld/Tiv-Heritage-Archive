# Research batch 055 — Kogi LGA profiles

Researched 2026-09-30. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Kogi. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **21 LGA descriptions**, filling fields that were empty.
- **20 headquarters towns**, created as places. Lokoja reuses the existing place.
- **Figures:** the 2006 population and the area of each of the 21 LGAs, from Statoids.
- **New official source:** INEC's list of its Kogi LGA offices. For 8 LGAs it names the LGA secretariat.
- **Headquarters decided on the evidence:**
  - **Okehi → Obangede** (Wikipedia and INEC), not 'Okehi' as Statoids has it.
  - **Ofu → Ugwolawo**, INEC's spelling; Ogwoawo and Ugwalawo are kept as variants.
- **No official Kogi table of headquarters was found**, so five LGAs rest on Statoids alone. This is listed as a gap.
- **New link:** the Etsu Bassa-Nge's seat, Gboloko, is in Bassa LGA, per Wikipedia.
- **Not used:** Wikipedia's Mopa-Muro figure 'at the 2016 census' (there was no census in 2016).
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 21 descriptions

**Adavi** (59 words). Adavi is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Ogaminana (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 202,194 at the 2006 census and an area of about 718 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia's article on the Ebira places the Ebira Tao in the LGA; INEC's office for the LGA is at Oniyeka Okunchi.

**Ajaokuta** (89 words). Ajaokuta is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Egayan (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 122,321 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,362 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia spells the headquarters Egayin; INEC's office for the LGA is at Adogo. The Federal Government's profile of the state names Ajaokuta Steel as the largest iron and steel industry in Nigeria. Wikipedia's articles on the Ebira and the Igala place Ebira (including the Eganyi) and Igala in the LGA.

**Ankpa** (79 words). Ankpa is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Ankpa (according to Statoids). It had a population of 267,353 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,200 km² (Statoids). Blench's Atlas places Igala and Basa-Benue here, and names Ánkpa and Ògùgù among the dialects of Igala; the federal profile names the Ogugu as a subgroup of the Igala. Ankpa is also one of the traditional councils of the Igala Kingdom (Wikipedia).

**Bassa** (86 words). Bassa is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Oguma (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 139,993 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,925 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives a 2006 population of 139,687. It is the home of the Bassa-Nge, whose ruler, the Etsu Bassa-Nge, has his seat at Gboloko in the LGA, and of the Bassa (Basa) and Igala; Blench's Atlas places Basa-Benue, Igala, Ebira (Koto) and Nupe Tako here.

**Dekina** (68 words). Dekina is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Dekina (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 260,312 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,461 km² (Statoids). Blench's Atlas places Igala here and names Ànyìgbá and Ìfè among its dialects in the LGA; Dekina is also one of the traditional councils of the Igala Kingdom (Wikipedia).

**Ibaji** (65 words). Ibaji is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Onyedega (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 128,129 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,377 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places the headquarters on the Niger. The LGA bears the name of the Ìbàjì dialect of Igala, which Blench's Atlas records in the Idah area.

**Idah** (78 words). Idah is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Idah (according to Statoids). It had a population of 79,815 at the 2006 census and an area of about 36 km² (Statoids). It is the smallest LGA of the state by area. Idah is the capital of the Igala Kingdom and the seat of the Attah Igala, and the Ojogwu Atogwu Tumulus near the Attah's palace is Kogi's only declared national monument (No. 38).

**Igalamela-Odolu** (53 words). Igalamela-Odolu is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Ajaka (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 148,020 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,175 km² (Statoids). Ajaka is also one of the traditional councils of the Igala Kingdom (Wikipedia).

**Ijumu** (64 words). Ijumu is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Iyara (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 119,929 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,306 km² (Statoids). It is one of the Okun LGAs; Ijumu is among the Okun dialects of Yoruba, and the Olujumu among the Okun stools (Wikipedia; Blench's Atlas).

**Kabba/Bunu** (70 words). Kabba/Bunu is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Kabba (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 145,446 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,706 km² (Statoids). Kabba, the largest Okun town, is the seat of the Obaro of Kabba, chairman of the Okun Area Traditional Council; Bunu is another of the Okun peoples (Wikipedia; Daily Trust).

**Kogi** (73 words). Kogi is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Koton Karfe (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 115,900 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,498 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives Koton Karifi as another form. It is the seat of the Ohimege-Igu of Koton Karfe, a first-class stool, and Blench's Atlas places Kakanda, Kupa, Nupe and Ebira (Koto) in the LGA.

**Lokoja** (107 words). Lokoja is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Lokoja (according to Statoids). It had a population of 195,261 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,180 km² (Statoids). Lokoja, at the confluence of the Niger and Benue, is the state capital; Wikipedia dates its foundation to a trading post established in 1857 by William Balfour Baikie. It is the largest LGA of the state by area. The National Museum of Colonial History, Mount Patti and three of the NCMM's proposed national monuments are in Lokoja, and Wikipedia names the Bassa-Nge, Oworo and Nupe as indigenous to the area.

**Mopa-Muro** (60 words). Mopa-Muro is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Mopa (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 44,037 at the 2006 census and an area of about 901 km² (Statoids). It had the smallest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census. It is one of the Okun LGAs (Wikipedia).

**Ofu** (73 words). Ofu is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Ugwolawo (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 192,169 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,680 km² (Statoids). Statoids and Wikipedia spell the headquarters Ogwoawo, Wikipedia also Ugwalawo or Gwalawo; INEC places the LGA secretariat at Ugwolawo, also the name of one of the traditional councils of the Igala Kingdom (Wikipedia).

**Ogori/Magongo** (89 words). Ogori/Magongo is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Akpafa (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 39,622 at the 2006 census and an area of about 79 km² (Statoids). According to Wikipedia it was created from the old Okene LGA for the Ogori and Magongo people; its main towns are Ogori and Magongo, where Ọkọ and Ọsayẹn are spoken, and it is the home of the Ovia-Osese and Owiya Osese festivals and the seat of the Olu of Magongo.

**Okehi** (58 words). Okehi is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Obangede (according to Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 199,999 at the 2006 census and an area of about 661 km² (Statoids). Statoids gives Okehi as the headquarters. Wikipedia's article on the Ebira and Blench's Atlas place the Ebira in the LGA.

**Okene** (76 words). Okene is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Okene (according to Statoids). It had a population of 320,260 at the 2006 census and an area of about 328 km² (Statoids). It was the most populous LGA of the state at the 2006 census. Okene is the seat of the Ohinoyi of Ebiraland, and until Kogi State was created it was seen as the administrative centre of the Ebira-speaking people (Wikipedia).

**Olamaboro** (50 words). Olamaboro is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Okpo (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 160,152 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,132 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia's article on the Igala places them in the LGA.

**Omala** (52 words). Omala is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Abejukolo (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 108,402 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,667 km² (Statoids). Omala is also one of the traditional councils of the Igala Kingdom (Wikipedia).

**Yagba East** (65 words). Yagba East is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Isanlu (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 140,150 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,396 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia describes it as populated mainly by the Yagba, one of the Okun peoples; the Agbana of Isanlu is among the Okun stools (Wikipedia).

**Yagba West** (58 words). Yagba West is a local government area of Kogi State with its headquarters at Odo Ere (according to Statoids). It had a population of 149,023 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,276 km² (Statoids). INEC's office for the LGA is also at Odo-Ere. It is one of the Okun LGAs (Wikipedia, Okun people).

## Research gaps

- **Kogi: no official table of LGA headquarters.** Ankpa, Idah, Lokoja, Okene and Yagba West rest on Statoids alone (INEC shows only its office in the town); an official Kogi State list would settle them.
- **Kogi: headquarters spellings.** Ofu: Ugwolawo (INEC), Ogwoawo (Statoids, Wikipedia), Ugwalawo; Ajaokuta: Egayan (Statoids), Egayin (Wikipedia).
- **Kogi: Bassa population.** Statoids gives 139,993 and Wikipedia 139,687 for 2006; Statoids' figure is recorded.
- **Kogi: LGA creation dates.** Not found except Ogori/Magongo's origin in the old Okene LGA (Wikipedia, no date).
