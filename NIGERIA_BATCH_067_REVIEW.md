# Research batch 067 — Borno LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-01. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Borno. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **27 LGA descriptions**, filling fields that were empty.
- **25 headquarters towns**, created as places. Maiduguri and Kukawa reuse existing places.
- **Figures:** the 2006 population and area of each LGA, from Statoids.
- **Sources for the headquarters:**
  - Statoids
  - the **Borno State Government's own table** (official, read via the Internet Archive)
  - INEC's LGA offices that sit by the LGA secretariat
  - Wikipedia
- **The state's table has five evident errors.** For example, it gives Guzamala 'Gajiram' (Nganzai's headquarters) and Nganzai 'Rann' (Kala/Balge's). These are overridden by the other sources and noted in each text.
- **Two LGAs are recorded as *reported*, because the sources conflict:**
  - **Bayo:** Briyel, Fikhayel or Bayo
  - **Ngala:** Gamboru Ngala or Ngala
- **Each description draws on the archive's own records:** the LGA's peoples, its emirate and its heritage (batches 063–066).
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 27 descriptions

**Abadam** (77 words). Abadam is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Malam Fatori (according to Statoids, the state government and Wikipedia). It had a population of 100,180 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,973 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places it on the western shore of Lake Chad, bordering Chad and Niger and close to Cameroon, and spells the headquarters Mallam Fatori; it is one of the LGAs of the Borno Emirate.

**Askira/Uba** (100 words). Askira/Uba is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Askira (according to Statoids, the state government and Wikipedia). It had a population of 138,091 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,362 km² (Statoids). It has two emirate councils, Askira and Uba, and Askira town was founded by Muhammadu Mai Maina in 1921 (Wikipedia). The village of Lassa in the LGA gave its name to the Lassa virus, first identified in returning missionaries (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Gude, Hausa, Kibaku, Kilba and Marghi.

**Bama** (87 words). Bama is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Bama (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 269,986 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,997 km² (Statoids). Bama, about 60 km from Maiduguri, is the seat of the Bama Emirate, of which it is the only LGA; it was captured by Boko Haram in 2014 (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri, Mandara, Marghi and Shuwa Arabs.

**Bayo** (90 words). Bayo is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Briyel (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 78,978 at the 2006 census and an area of about 956 km² (Statoids). The sources disagree on the headquarters: INEC places its office by the LGA secretariat at Briyel (Statoids: Biriyel), Wikipedia names Fikhayel and the state government's table gives Bayo. It is one of the four LGAs of the Biu Emirate (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Tera.

**Biu** (96 words). Biu is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Biu (according to Statoids, the state government and Wikipedia). It had a population of 176,072 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,315 km² (Statoids). Biu lies on the Biu Plateau at about 626 metres and is the capital of the Biu Emirate, the former Biu Kingdom (Wikipedia). Wikipedia names the Babur and Bura, Tera, Marghi, Mina and Fulani as its main peoples. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bura, Dera, Ga'anda and Tera.

**Chibok** (98 words). Chibok is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Chibok (according to Statoids, the state government and INEC). It had a population of 66,105 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,350 km² (Statoids). According to Wikipedia most of its people are Kibaku, and the federal profile says the Chibok (Kibaku) inhabit the LGA. The kidnapping of schoolgirls from Chibok in April 2014 led to the closure of most schools in the state (Wikipedia, Borno State). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kibaku and Marghi.

**Damboa** (91 words). Damboa is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Damboa (according to Statoids, the state government and INEC). It had a population of 231,573 at the 2006 census and an area of about 6,219 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives a 2006 population of 233,200, and describes the Marghi as the original settlers, joined by Kanuri drawn by the calabash trade. It is the seat of the Damboa Emirate Council. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri, Kibaku, Malgwa and Marghi.

**Dikwa** (87 words). Dikwa is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Dikwa (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 105,909 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,774 km² (Statoids). Dikwa was the fortified capital of Rabih az-Zubayr from 1893 to 1900 (Wikipedia), and Rabeh's Fort there is Borno's only declared national monument (No. 13). It is the seat of the Dikwa Emirate. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Shuwa Arabs.

**Gubio** (83 words). Gubio is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Gubio (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 152,778 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,464 km² (Statoids). Gubio, also written Gobiyo, lies about 97 km from both Damasak and Maiduguri on the road between them; it is one of the LGAs of the Borno Emirate, and 81 people were killed there in a massacre on 9 June 2020 (Wikipedia).

**Guzamala** (71 words). Guzamala is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Gudumbali (according to Statoids, INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 95,648 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,517 km² (Statoids). The state government's table gives Gajiram, which is Nganzai's headquarters. Gudumbali lies about 125 km north of Maiduguri (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri.

**Gwoza** (111 words). Gwoza is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Gwoza (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 276,312 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,883 km² (Statoids). Gwoza, about 135 km south-east of Maiduguri on the Cameroon border, lies below the Gwoza Hills of the Mandara Mountains, which reach about 1,300 metres (Wikipedia). It is the seat of the Gwoza Emirate, and Blench's Atlas places more languages in Gwoza than in any other Borno LGA (eleven). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Dghwede, Glavda, Guduf, Kanuri, Lamang, Mafa, Mandara and Sukur.

**Hawul** (72 words). Hawul is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Azare (according to Statoids, INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 120,314 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,098 km² (Statoids). The state government's table gives Hawul. It is one of the four LGAs of the Biu Emirate (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bura and Hwana.

**Jere** (74 words). Jere is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Khaddamari (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 211,204 at the 2006 census and an area of about 868 km² (Statoids). The state government's table gives Warabe, and INEC's office is at Ngudda Addamari. Wikipedia says most of its people are Baggara Arabs and Kanuri. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri.

**Kaga** (70 words). Kaga is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Benisheikh (according to Statoids, the state government, INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 90,015 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,700 km² (Statoids). INEC spells it Benesheikh. It is one of the LGAs of the Borno Emirate (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri.

**Kala/Balge** (74 words). Kala/Balge is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Rann (according to Statoids, the state government and Wikipedia). It had a population of 60,797 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,896 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia calls it the easternmost LGA of Nigeria. It is part of the Dikwa Emirate. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Afade, Kanuri and Shuwa Arabs.

**Konduga** (85 words). Konduga is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Konduga (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 156,564 at the 2006 census and an area of about 5,855 km² (Statoids). Konduga lies about 25 km south-east of Maiduguri on the north bank of the Ngadda River, and pottery from about 6300 BP has been found there (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri, Mandara, Marghi and Shuwa Arabs.

**Kukawa** (75 words). Kukawa is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Kukawa (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 203,864 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,901 km² (Statoids). Kukawa, founded as Kuka in 1814, was the capital of the al-Kanemi shehus from 1846 until Rabih destroyed it in 1893 (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri.

**Kwaya Kusar** (84 words). Kwaya Kusar is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Kwaya Kusar (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 56,500 at the 2006 census and an area of about 732 km² (Statoids). It had the smallest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census. Wikipedia describes its people as mainly Bura and places it in the Biu Emirate. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bura, Marghi and Tera.

**Mafa** (68 words). Mafa is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Mafa (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 103,518 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,869 km² (Statoids). Despite the name, Blench's Atlas places the Mafa language in Gwoza LGA, not here. It is one of the LGAs of the Borno Emirate (Wikipedia, Borno Emirate).

**Magumeri** (52 words). Magumeri is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Magumeri (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 140,231 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,856 km² (Statoids). It is one of the LGAs of the Borno Emirate (Wikipedia).

**Maiduguri** (108 words). Maiduguri is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Maiduguri (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 521,492 at the 2006 census and an area of about 132 km² (Statoids). Maiduguri, the state capital, was founded in 1907 and consists of Yerwa to the west and Old Maiduguri to the east (Wikipedia); it is the seat of the Shehu of Borno and has the National Museum Maiduguri. It had the largest population and the smallest area of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa and Kanuri.

**Marte** (72 words). Marte is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Marte (according to Statoids and the state government). It had a population of 129,370 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,154 km² (Statoids). Marte lies on the western shore of Lake Chad; its traditional head, the mai of Marte, is described by Wikipedia as one of the oldest continuous political offices in Borno State.

**Mobbar** (50 words). Mobbar is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Damasak (according to Statoids, the state government, INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 116,654 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,790 km² (Statoids). It borders the Republic of Niger (Wikipedia).

**Monguno** (66 words). Monguno is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Monguno (according to Statoids, the state government and Wikipedia). It had a population of 109,851 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,913 km² (Statoids). It is an administrative and commercial centre for the surrounding area (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri.

**Ngala** (88 words). Ngala is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Gamboru Ngala (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 237,071 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,465 km² (Statoids). The state government's table and INEC write the headquarters as Ngala. It borders Cameroon and is part of the Dikwa Emirate (Wikipedia); the El-Kanemi Prayer House at Ngala is a proposed national monument. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri and Shuwa Arabs.

**Nganzai** (71 words). Nganzai is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Gajiram (according to Statoids, INEC and Wikipedia). It had a population of 99,799 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,467 km² (Statoids). The state government's table gives Rann, which is Kala/Balge's headquarters. Wikipedia describes it as up to 90% Kanuri. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Kanuri.

**Shani** (64 words). Shani is a local government area of Borno State with its headquarters at Shani (according to Statoids, the state government and Wikipedia). It had a population of 102,317 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,262 km² (Statoids). It borders Bayo, Hawul and Kwaya Kusar LGAs (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Dera (Kanakuru).

## Research gaps

- **Borno: the state's table of LGA headquarters.** The Borno State Government's own table (2024–2025) gives wrong headquarters for Guzamala (Gajiram), Nganzai (Rann), Jere (Warabe), Hawul (Hawul) and Bayo (Bayo); the archive follows Statoids, INEC and Wikipedia there. A corrected official list would settle them.
- **Borno: Bayo and Ngala headquarters.** Bayo: Briyel (Statoids, INEC), Fikhayel (Wikipedia) or Bayo (state). Ngala: Gamboru Ngala (Wikipedia; Statoids 'Gambara Ngala') or Ngala (state, INEC). Both recorded at 'reported'.
- **Borno: figures.** Damboa's 2006 population: Statoids 231,573, Wikipedia 233,200 (Statoids recorded). Wikipedia's Konduga figure ('about 13,400') is not used.
- **Borno: LGA creation dates.** Not found in the sources read.
