## The 27 descriptions

**Auyo** (73 words). Auyo is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Auyo (according to Statoids). It had a population of 132,001 at the 2006 census and an area of about 512 km² (Statoids). Blench's Atlas records Auyokawa, an extinct Chadic language, at Auyo, and part of Baturiya Wetland, a Ramsar Site, lies in the LGA (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Hausa.

**Babura** (64 words). Babura is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Babura (according to Statoids). It had a population of 208,101 at the 2006 census and an area of about 992 km² (Statoids). It lies in the north of the state, bordering Katsina State and Danbatta LGA of Kano State, and is one of the LGAs of the Ringim Emirate (Wikipedia).

**Biriniwa** (63 words). Biriniwa is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Biriniwa (according to Statoids). It had a population of 142,329 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,567 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia writes Birniwa and describes the town as home to Kanuri (Mangawa), Hausa and Fulani; the Manga language is spoken in the LGA (Wikipedia, Jigawa State).

**Birnin Kudu** (89 words). Birnin Kudu is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Birnin Kudu (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 313,373 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,418 km² (Statoids). Birnin Kudu was a chiefdom from the 10th century; its rock paintings and rock gongs, recorded in 1955, include four declared national monuments, and Gwaram and Buji LGAs were carved from it in 1996 (Wikipedia; NCMM). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Warji.

**Buji** (44 words). Buji is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Gantsa (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 97,371 at the 2006 census and an area of about 548 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is in Gansa town.

**Dutse** (79 words). Dutse is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Dutse (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 246,143 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,099 km² (Statoids). Dutse is the capital of Jigawa State and the seat of the Dutse Emirate, and is home to the Federal University Dutse, founded in 2011 (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Hausa.

**Gagarawa** (45 words). Gagarawa is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Gagarawa (according to Statoids). It had a population of 80,394 at the 2006 census and an area of about 654 km² (Statoids). It lies in the north of the state (Wikipedia).

**Garki** (69 words). Garki is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Garki (according to Statoids). It had a population of 152,233 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,408 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia also calls the town Garkin Dirani and notes that it was the site of the Garki Project, a malaria research study; it is one of the LGAs of the Ringim Emirate.

**Gumel** (79 words). Gumel is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Gumel (according to Statoids). It had a population of 107,161 at the 2006 census and an area of about 223 km² (Statoids). Gumel is the seat of the Gumel Emirate, founded about 1750; its emir since 1981 died on 3 September 2026 and his son was appointed the 17th emir (Wikipedia; Daily Trust). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Manga.

**Guri** (61 words). Guri is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Guri (according to Statoids). It had a population of 115,018 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,060 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says the Bade language is spoken in the LGA, and places the Manga language there too; part of Baturiya Wetland lies in it.

**Gwaram** (50 words). Gwaram is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Gwaram (according to Statoids). It had a population of 272,582 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,912 km² (Statoids). It was carved out of Birnin Kudu LGA in 1996 (Wikipedia, Birnin Kudu).

**Gwiwa** (56 words). Gwiwa is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Gwiwa (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 124,517 at the 2006 census and an area of about 450 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says Gwiwa was a district headquarters early in British rule and became an LGA headquarters in 1992.

**Hadejia** (64 words). Hadejia is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Hadejia (according to Statoids). It had a population of 105,628 at the 2006 census and an area of about 32 km² (Statoids). Hadejia, once the Hausa state of Biram, is the seat of the Hadejia Emirate; the town lies north of the Hadejia River, upstream of the Hadejia-Nguru wetlands (Wikipedia).

**Jahun** (46 words). Jahun is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Jahun (according to Statoids). It had a population of 229,094 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,172 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says the LGA is inhabited mainly by Fulani.

**Kafin Hausa** (69 words). Kafin Hausa is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Kafin Hausa (according to Statoids). It had a population of 271,058 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,380 km² (Statoids). Blench's Atlas records two extinct Chadic languages, Shira and Teshena, at towns in the LGA, which it calls Keffin Hausa; Sule Lamido University is at Kafin Hausa (Wikipedia, Jigawa State).

**Kaugama** (52 words). Kaugama is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Kaugama (according to Statoids). It had a population of 127,956 at the 2006 census and an area of about 883 km² (Statoids). It lies in the north of the state; Wikipedia places the Manga language in the LGA.

**Kazaure** (62 words). Kazaure is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Kazaure (according to Statoids). It had a population of 161,494 at the 2006 census and an area of about 368 km² (Statoids). Kazaure has been the seat of the Kazaure Emirate since 1819 (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Hausa.

**Kiri Kasama** (58 words). Kiri Kasama is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Kiri Kasama (according to Statoids). It had a population of 191,523 at the 2006 census and an area of about 797 km² (Statoids). INEC writes Kirika Samma. Wikipedia places the Manga language in the LGA, and part of Baturiya Wetland lies in it.

**Kiyawa** (54 words). Kiyawa is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Kiyawa (according to Statoids). It had a population of 172,913 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,030 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places the town on the road between Kano and Azare, about 30 km east of Dutse.

**Maigatari** (73 words). Maigatari is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Maigatari (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 179,715 at the 2006 census and an area of about 870 km² (Statoids). Maigatari is a border town on the frontier with Niger, known for a large livestock market founded in 1870 (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani, Hausa and Kanuri.

**Malam Madori** (80 words). Malam Madori is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Malam Madori (according to Statoids). It had a population of 161,413 at the 2006 census and an area of about 766 km² (Statoids). Statoids and INEC's ward directory write Malam Maduri. Wikipedia says the town was founded by Malam Madu around 1930 and gives a 2006 population of 164,791. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani, Kanuri and Manga.

**Miga** (48 words). Miga is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Miga (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 128,424 at the 2006 census and an area of about 586 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is beside the LGA secretariat in Miga town.

**Ringim** (50 words). Ringim is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Ringim (according to Statoids). It had a population of 192,024 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,057 km² (Statoids). Ringim is the headquarters of the Ringim Emirate, created in November 1991 (Wikipedia).

**Roni** (49 words). Roni is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Roni (according to Statoids). It had a population of 77,819 at the 2006 census and an area of about 322 km² (Statoids). It had the smallest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census.

**Sule Tankarkar** (46 words). Sule Tankarkar is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Sule Tankarkar (according to Statoids). It had a population of 130,849 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,283 km² (Statoids). It borders the Republic of Niger (Wikipedia).

**Taura** (54 words). Taura is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Taura (according to Statoids). It had a population of 131,757 at the 2006 census and an area of about 653 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says most of its people are Hausa; it is one of the LGAs of the Ringim Emirate.

**Yankwashi** (38 words). Yankwashi is a local government area of Jigawa State with its headquarters at Karkarna (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 95,759 at the 2006 census and an area of about 371 km² (Statoids).
