# Research batch 146 — Ondo LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-07. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Ondo. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **18 LGA descriptions** and **17 new headquarters towns**. Akure South reuses the existing Akure place. Town slugs end in '-ondo'.
- **Agreement on headquarters:**
  - Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC agree for 14 LGAs.
  - **Akoko South-East (Isua) and Akoko South-West (Oka):** Wikipedia and INEC agree. Statoids has the two the other way round, which is noted in each description.
  - **Odigbo (Ore) and Okitipupa:** Statoids and Wikipedia agree; INEC's offices are elsewhere.
  - **Idanre:** Owena in all three sources, though Wikipedia's town page calls Idanre the headquarters.
- **Each description names** the LGA's people where a source gives them, its smaller languages, and any seat of a ruler, monument or festival recorded in batches 142–145.
- **Figures:** 2006 population and area from Statoids.
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 18 descriptions

**Akoko North-East** (73 words). Akoko North-East is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Ikare (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 175,409 at the 2006 census and an area of about 372 km² (Statoids). Ikare was the headquarters of the old Akoko Division (Wikipedia). The Akpes and Ukaan languages are spoken in the LGA, besides Yoruba (Blench's Atlas; Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba, of the Akoko sub-group.

**Akoko North-West** (71 words). Akoko North-West is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Okeagbe (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 213,792 at the 2006 census and an area of about 512 km² (Statoids). Three small languages of the Akoko hills are spoken in its towns besides Yoruba: Arigidi (North Akoko), Ahan and Akpes (Blench's Atlas; Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba, of the Akoko sub-group.

**Akoko South-East** (94 words). Akoko South-East is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Isua (according to Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 82,426 at the 2006 census and an area of about 530 km² (Statoids). Statoids gives Oka as the headquarters and about 530 km², while Wikipedia gives 225 km² of land; Statoids' rows for the two southern Akoko LGAs appear to be exchanged. Three Edoid languages are spoken here: Uhami at Isua, and Ehuẹun and Ukue at Epinmi (Blench's Atlas; INEC). Its people are Yoruba, of the Akoko sub-group.

**Akoko South-West** (84 words). Akoko South-West is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Oka (according to Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 229,486 at the 2006 census and an area of about 226 km² (Statoids). Statoids gives Isua as the headquarters and about 226 km², while Wikipedia gives 340 km²; Statoids' rows for the two southern Akoko LGAs appear to be exchanged. Oka-Akoko is the seat of the Olubaka of Oka (Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba, of the Akoko sub-group.

**Akure North** (62 words). Akure North is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Iju/Itaogbolu (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 131,587 at the 2006 census and an area of about 660 km² (Statoids). INEC's LGA office is at Iju. Its people are recorded as Yoruba; their sub-group is not named in a source read.

**Akure South** (101 words). Akure South is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Akure (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 353,211 at the 2006 census and an area of about 331 km² (Statoids). Akure is the state capital and the seat of the Deji of Akure, whose old palace is a declared national monument; the National Museum Akure is also in the city (NCMM; Wikipedia). It had the largest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census (Statoids). Its people are recorded as Yoruba; their sub-group is not named in a source read.

**Ese Odo** (75 words). Ese Odo is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Igbekebo (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 154,978 at the 2006 census and an area of about 762 km² (Statoids). It is the Ijaw LGA of Ondo State, home of the Western Apoi and the Arogbo, who speak Ịzọn (Wikipedia; Blench's Atlas). Wikipedia's Ondo State page also lists the 'Ese Odo' among the Yoruba sub-groups.

**Idanre** (85 words). Idanre is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Owena (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 129,024 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,914 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia's page on Idanre town calls the town the LGA's headquarters. Idanre Hill, whose cultural landscape is a proposed national monument, rises above the town (NCMM; Wikipedia). It had the largest area of the state's LGAs (Statoids). Its people are Yoruba, of the Idanre sub-group.

**Ifedore** (74 words). Ifedore is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Igbara-Oke (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 176,327 at the 2006 census and an area of about 295 km² (Statoids). Two declared national monuments are in the LGA: the Iho Eleru cave near Isarun and the Igbara-Oke petroglyphs (NCMM; Wikipedia). Its people are recorded as Yoruba; their sub-group is not named in a source read.

**Ilaje** (77 words). Ilaje is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Igbokoda (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 290,615 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,318 km² (Statoids). The LGA covers Ondo State's Atlantic coast, and it is the home of the Ilaje, whose Ugbo Kingdom, ruled by the Olugbo, has its capital at Ode Ugbo (Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba, of the Ilaje sub-group.

**Ile-Oluji-Okeigbo** (53 words). Ile-Oluji-Okeigbo is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Ile-Oluji (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 172,870 at the 2006 census and an area of about 698 km² (Statoids). Its people are Yoruba; Oke-Igbo speaks a variety similar to the Ife dialect (Wikipedia).

**Irele** (50 words). Irele is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Irele (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 145,166 at the 2006 census and an area of about 963 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia writes Ode-Irele. Its people are Yoruba, of the Ikale sub-group.

**Odigbo** (91 words). Odigbo is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Ore (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). INEC's LGA office is at New Town, off Ore Road, Odigbo. It had a population of 230,351 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,818 km² (Statoids). The Ashuba marble stone at Araromi Obu, a proposed national monument, is in the LGA (NCMM; INEC). Furupagha Ijaw communities live in its Ebijan ward (Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba of the Ondo lineage, with Ikale in parts, and Ijaw (Wikipedia).

**Okitipupa** (65 words). Okitipupa is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Okitipupa (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). INEC's LGA office is at Bolorunduro junction, Akure Road. It had a population of 233,565 at the 2006 census and an area of about 803 km² (Statoids). Okitipupa is the central town of the Ikale area (Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba, of the Ikale sub-group.

**Ondo East** (77 words). Ondo East is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Bolorunduro (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 74,758 at the 2006 census and an area of about 354 km² (Statoids). It had the smallest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census (Statoids). Wikipedia gives an area of about 896 km². Its people are recorded as Yoruba; their sub-group is not named in a source read.

**Ondo West** (68 words). Ondo West is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Ondo (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 283,672 at the 2006 census and an area of about 970 km² (Statoids). Ondo is the seat of the Osemawe, ruler of the Ondo Kingdom (Wikipedia). Its people are recorded as Yoruba; their sub-group is not named in a source read.

**Ose** (75 words). Ose is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Ifon (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 144,901 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,465 km² (Statoids). Idoani, in the LGA, is the seat of the Alani and of the Idoani Confederacy, and one of its quarters speaks the Iyayu language (Wikipedia; Blench's Atlas). Its people are Yoruba, of the Ose sub-group.

**Owo** (73 words). Owo is a local government area of Ondo State with its headquarters at Owo (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 218,886 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,027 km² (Statoids). Owo is the seat of the Olowo, whose palace houses the National Museum Owo, and the town holds the Igogo festival every September (NCMM; Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba, of the Owo sub-group.

## Research gaps

- **Ondo: Statoids' southern Akoko rows.** Statoids gives Oka as the headquarters of Akoko South-East and Isua of Akoko South-West; Wikipedia, its LGA pages and INEC give the reverse, which is used. Statoids' areas for the two (530 and 226 km²) also look exchanged against Wikipedia (225 and 340 km²).
- **Ondo: headquarters of Idanre, Odigbo and Okitipupa.** Wikipedia's Idanre page calls Idanre town the headquarters (Owena in the other sources); INEC's Odigbo and Okitipupa offices are outside the headquarters towns. A state government source would settle these.
- **Ondo: differing LGA figures.** Wikipedia's LGA pages give some different figures from Statoids (for example Ondo East about 896 km², Owo 222,262 people); only Statoids' figures are recorded.
