# Research batch 152 — Ekiti LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-07. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Ekiti. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **16 LGA descriptions** and **15 new headquarters towns**. Ado Ekiti reuses the existing place. New town slugs end in '-ekiti'.
- **Headquarters:** Statoids and INEC's LGA office list agree for all 16. Wikipedia's state page lists the LGAs without headquarters.
- **Names:** the archive's **Aiyekire** is **Gbonyin**, and its **Idosi-Osi** is **Ido-Osi**, in INEC's lists and Wikipedia. The descriptions say 'Gbonyin, also called Aiyekire'.
  - Renaming the two LGA records, keeping the old names as other names, will be proposed in the QC fix (batch 153), as was done for Yewa North and South.
- **Each description** names any seat of a ruler, monument, festival or smaller language recorded in batches 148–151.
- **Figures:** 2006 population and area from Statoids.
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 16 descriptions

**Ado Ekiti** (90 words). Ado Ekiti is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Ado Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 308,621 at the 2006 census and an area of about 293 km² (Statoids). Ado-Ekiti is the state capital and the seat of the Ewi of Ado, whose kingdom holds the annual Udiroko festival (Wikipedia; Ekiti State Government). It had the largest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census (Statoids). Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Gbonyin** (64 words). Gbonyin, also called Aiyekire, is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Ode Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 148,193 at the 2006 census and an area of about 391 km² (Statoids). INEC and Wikipedia name the LGA Gbonyin; Statoids keeps Aiyekire. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Efon** (88 words). Efon is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Efon Alaaye (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 86,941 at the 2006 census and an area of about 232 km² (Statoids). Efon-Alaaye is the seat of the Alaaye of Efon, the LGA's one recognised paramount ruler; the Ekiti State Government notes that its people speak the Ekiti dialect with an intonation related to Ijesa (Ekiti State Government; Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ekiti East** (85 words). Ekiti East is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Omuo Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 137,955 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,072 km² (Statoids). The Ahan language is spoken at Ahan, one of Omuo's communities, and Omuo's Yoruba is close to Yagba-Ekiti speech (Blench's Atlas; Wikipedia). It had the largest area of the state's LGAs (Statoids). Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ekiti South-West** (52 words). Ekiti South-West is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Ilawe Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 165,277 at the 2006 census and an area of about 346 km² (Statoids). Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ekiti West** (64 words). Ekiti West is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Aramoko Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 179,892 at the 2006 census and an area of about 366 km² (Statoids). The Ikogosi Warm Springs are in the LGA (Wikipedia; INEC's Ikogosi ward). Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Emure** (54 words). Emure is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Emure Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 93,884 at the 2006 census and an area of about 301 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes Emure. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ido-Osi** (64 words). Ido-Osi, also written Idosi-Osi, is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Ido Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 159,114 at the 2006 census and an area of about 232 km² (Statoids). INEC writes Ido/Osi and Wikipedia Ido-Osi; Statoids keeps Idosi-Osi. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ijero** (54 words). Ijero is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Ijero Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 221,405 at the 2006 census and an area of about 391 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes Ijero. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ikere** (80 words). Ikere is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Ikere Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 147,355 at the 2006 census and an area of about 263 km² (Statoids). Ikere-Ekiti is the seat of the Ogoga, whose authority is challenged by the Olukere; the Olosunta and Orole hills rise in the LGA (Wikipedia; Peoples Gazette). Statoids writes Ikere. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ikole** (54 words). Ikole is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Ikole Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 168,436 at the 2006 census and an area of about 321 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes Ikole. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ilejemeje** (66 words). Ilejemeje is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Iye Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 43,530 at the 2006 census and an area of about 95 km² (Statoids). It had the smallest population and area of the state's LGAs (Statoids), which writes Iye. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Irepodun/Ifelodun** (55 words). Irepodun/Ifelodun is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Igede Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 129,149 at the 2006 census and an area of about 356 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes Igede. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Ise/Orun** (55 words). Ise/Orun is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Ise Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 113,754 at the 2006 census and an area of about 432 km² (Statoids). Statoids writes Ise. Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Moba** (81 words). Moba is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Otun Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 146,496 at the 2006 census and an area of about 199 km² (Statoids). Otun is the seat of the Oore, styled chairman of the traditional rulers of Moba land, and its Yoruba is close to Igbomina speech (Ekiti State Government; New Telegraph; Wikipedia). Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

**Oye** (72 words). Oye is a local government area of Ekiti State with its headquarters at Oye Ekiti (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 134,210 at the 2006 census and an area of about 507 km² (Statoids). Ire-Ekiti, in the LGA, holds the Ogun Onire festival, and its Ogun grove is a proposed national monument (NCMM; The Sun). Its people are Yoruba of the Ekiti sub-group (Wikipedia; the federal profile).

## Research gaps

- **Ekiti: Gbonyin and Ido-Osi.** INEC (2015 and 2024) and Wikipedia name these LGAs Gbonyin and Ido/Osi (Ido-Osi); Statoids and the archive have Aiyekire and Idosi-Osi. The renaming is proposed in batch 153; the date of the change to Gbonyin is not given in a source read.
- **Ekiti: Wikipedia's headquarters.** Wikipedia's state page does not list the headquarters; Statoids and INEC agree on all 16, but a state government list would add a third source.
