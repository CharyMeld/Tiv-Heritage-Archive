# Research batch 038 — Taraba: the languages

Researched 2026-09-26. Phase 3, the first Taraba batch. Created in review; published only after your approval.

- **68 languages** and **6 new branches** (Adamawa, Mambiloid, Dakoid, Beboid, Grassfields Bantu, Gbaya), all from Blench's Atlas. All 74 Taraba entries were read by hand; 9 were excluded after reading (see the top of the script).
- **61 Glottocodes**, matched by name with map points checked in or near Taraba.
- **148 links** (state and LGA), and **139 other names**, typed by the Atlas's key as in batch 036.
- The existing **Wannu** record is the Atlas's *Abinsi* and gets its Taraba links (Wukari). Tiv and Etulo already have theirs.

## The languages

**Abon** (abon1238). Abon is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Southern Bantoid (Tivoid). Other names include Abong, Abõ. It is spoken only in Abong town, east of Baissa.

**Akum** (akum1238). Akum is a language spoken in Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid. The Atlas locates it at about 6°50′N, 9°50′E, in three villages in Nigeria, and in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 600 in Cameroon (1976).

**Ambo** (ambo1249). Ambo is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as possibly Tivoid (Southern Bantoid; no data). It is spoken in a single village east of Baissa. The Atlas marks its classification as uncertain.

**Bali** (bali1245). Bali is a language spoken in Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group; Yendang subgroup). Other names include Ị̀báalí. It is spoken in a single village, Bali, south of Jalingo; the Atlas names 'Numan LGA', which is in Adamawa State, so no LGA is linked. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,000 (SIL).

**Batu** (batu1255). Batu is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Southern Bantoid (Tivoid). It is spoken in several villages east of Baissa, below the Mambila escarpment. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 25,000 (SIL). It is a cluster whose members include Amanda–Afi, Angwe and Kamino, each named after its Batu village.

**Bete** (bete1261). Bete is a language spoken in Wukari LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (no data). It is spoken in Bete town. The Atlas describes the language as dying out.

**Bitare** (bita1251). Bitare is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Southern Bantoid (Tivoid). Other names include Njwande, Yukutare. It is spoken near Baissa and in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,000 in Nigeria (SIL 1973); 3,700 in Cameroon (SIL 1987).

**Bukwen** (bukw1238). Bukwen is a language spoken in Takum LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as South Bantoid (Beboid). It is spoken near Takum.

**Buru** (no Glottocode). Buru is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as South Bantoid (unclassified). It is spoken in a village near Batu, east of Baissa. The Atlas leaves its classification open within South Bantoid.

**Como Karim** (como1258). Como Karim is a language spoken in Karim Lamido and Jalingo LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Central Jukunoid; Wurbo cluster). Other names include Shomoh, Shomong, Chomo, Shomo, Karim, Kirim.

**Dadiya** (dadi1249). Dadiya is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Waja group). Other names include Nda Dia, Dadia, Bwe Daddiya. It is also spoken in Balanga LGA of Gombe State and Lamurde LGA of Adamawa State, between Dadiya and Bambam. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,986 (1961); 20,000 (1992 estimate).

**Dirim** (no Glottocode). Dirim is a language spoken in Bali LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Dakoid). It is spoken in the Garba Chede area. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 9,000 (CAPRO 1992). The Atlas doubts whether it is really separate from Samba Daka.

**Dong** (dong1293). Dong is a language spoken in Zing LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Dakoid. It is spoken in at least six villages; the Atlas also names Mayo Belwa LGA (Adamawa State). The Atlas cites speaker figures of about 20,000.

**Dza** (dzaa1238). Dza is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Jen group). Other names include Ja, nnwa' Dzâ, Jenjo, Janjo, Jen. It is spoken along the Benue River, and also in Numan LGA of Adamawa State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 6,100 (1952), a figure that may include other Jen groups such as Joole and Tha.

**Dzodinka** (dzod1238). Dzodinka is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Southern Bantoid (Grasslands Bantu: East). Other names include Adiri, Adere. It is spoken in a single village on the border, and in Cameroon. Mark (1923) and John (1932) were published in the language.

**Etkywan** (etky1238). Etkywan is a language spoken in Takum and Sardauna LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Central Jukunoid; Kpan–Icen group). Other names include Icen, Ichen, Itchen, Kentu, Kyanton, Nyidu. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 6,330 in Donga district (1952); more than 7,000 (SIL 1973).

**Fam** (famm1241). Fam is a language spoken in Bali LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). It is spoken 17 km east of Kungana. The Atlas cites speaker figures of under 1,000 (1984); under 500 (2016).

**Gbaya** (no Glottocode). Gbaya is a language spoken in Bali LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Gbaya (Niger-Congo). Other names include Baya. It is spoken near the confluence of the Benue and Taraba rivers, but mainly in Cameroon and the Central African Republic. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 200 in Nigeria (1965).

**Jiru** (jiru1238). Jiru is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Central Jukunoid; Wurbo cluster). Other names include Zhiru, Atak, Wiyap, Kir.

**Joole** (no Glottocode). Joole is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Jen group). Other names include èèʒìì. It is spoken along the Benue River, and also in Numan LGA of Adamawa State.

**Jibu** (jibu1239). Jibu is a language spoken in Gashaka LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Central Jukunoid; Jukun cluster). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 25,000 (SIL 1987). It belongs to the Jukun cluster; its dialects include Gayam and Garbabi. Parts of the Bible have been translated.

**Jukun Takum** (juku1254). Jukun Takum is a language spoken in Takum, Sardauna and Bali LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Central Jukunoid; Jukun cluster). Other names include Takum-Donga. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 40,000 second-language speakers only (UBS 1979). It belongs to the Jukun cluster; its dialects are Takum and Donga. The Atlas also gives Jibu as another name for it, although Jibu is a separate member of the cluster (Gashaka LGA). A Donga primer appeared in 1915.

**Kam** (kamm1249). Kam is a language spoken in Bali LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Kam group). It is spoken only in Mayo Kam and Kamijim villages. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 583 (1922); more than 1,000 (1987 estimate).

**Kapya** (kapy1238). Kapya is a language spoken in Takum LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Yukuben–Kutep). It is spoken at Kapya.

**Kholok** (khol1240). Kholok is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (Bole–Ngas group; Bole group). Other names include Kode, Koode, Kwoode, Widala, Pitiko. It is spoken near Didango. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,500 (1977).

**Hone** (hone1235). Hone is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Kororofa cluster). Other names include Kona. It is spoken in villages north and west of Jalingo, and also in Wase LGA of Plateau State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,000 (1977). Mark was published in 1927.

**Wapan** (wapa1235). Wapan is a language spoken in Wukari LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Kororofa cluster). Other names include Wukari. It is also spoken in Awe, Lafia, Shendam and Langtang LGAs (Nasarawa and Plateau states; precise areas uncertain). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 60,000 (SIL 1973). It is the Jukun of Wukari. A primer appeared in 1915 and Scripture portions since 1914; a Bible translation is in progress.

**Dampar** (no Glottocode). Dampar is a language spoken in Wukari LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Kororofa cluster). It is spoken at Dampar.

**Kpan** (kpan1246). Kpan is a language spoken in Wukari, Takum and Sardauna LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Central Jukunoid; Kpan–Icen group). Other names include Kpanten, Ikpan, Akpanzhi, Kpanzon, Abakan, Kpwate. Its dialects fall into a western group (Kumbo, Takum, Donga and the extinct Bissaula) and an eastern group.

**Kulung** (kulu1255). Kulung is a language spoken in Karim Lamido and Wukari LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Bantu (Jarawan). Other names include Kúkùlúŋ, Bambur, Wurkum. It is spoken at Balasa, Bambur and Kirim (Karim Lamido LGA) and at Gada Mayo (Wukari LGA). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 15,000 (SIL). It is still passed on to children and learned by neighbours. Hausa is the main second language. Scripture portions appeared between 1926 and 1950.

**Kulung (Chadic)** (no Glottocode). Kulung (Chadic) is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (Bole–Ngas group; Bole group). Other names include Wurkum. The Atlas cites speaker figures of perhaps 2,000. Its speakers consider themselves Kulung (whose language is Jarawan Bantu), although their own language is Chadic and related to Piya.

**Kuteb** (kute1248). Kuteb is a language spoken in Takum LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Yukuben–Kutep). Other names include Kutev, Kutep, Ati, Mbarike, Zumper. It is also spoken in Furu-Awa subdivision, Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 15,592 (1952); 30,000 (UBS 1986); 1,400 in Cameroon (1976). Its dialects are Lissam, Fikyu, Jenuwa, Kunabe and Kentin. The New Testament appeared in 1990.

**Kyak** (kyak1243). Kyak is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Bikwin group). Other names include Kyãk, Bambuka. It is spoken at Bambuka. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 10,000 (SIL).

**Laka** (laka1252). Laka is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mbum group). Other names include Lau, Lao Habe. It is spoken at Lau (now in Lau LGA), in Yola LGA of Adamawa State, and mainly in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 460 (1952); 500 (SIL 1973).

**Lamja-Deŋsa-Tola** (lamj1245). Lamja-Deŋsa-Tola is a language spoken in Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Dakoid). The Atlas names Mayo Belwa LGA (Adamawa State); the Lamja and Deŋsa live in 13 villages, with Ganglamja the central Lamja town. Its dialects are mutually intelligible and may not be distinct enough from Samba Daka to count as a separate language.

**Lamnso** (lamn1239). Lamnso is a language spoken in Sardauna and Takum LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Southern Bantoid (Grasslands Bantu). Other names include Lam-Nsaw, Lam-Nsọ, Lam-Nsọ'. It is spoken at Gembu and nearby towns (Sardauna LGA) and at Manya (Takum LGA), but mainly in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 125,000 in Cameroon (SIL 1987). The New Testament appeared in 1989.

**Leelạu** (leel1242). Leelạu is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Bikwin group). Other names include Lelo, Munga. It is spoken in one village and a hamlet 15 km east of Karim Lamido town.

**Limbum** (limb1268). Limbum is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Southern Bantoid (Grasslands Bantu). It is spoken on the Mambila uplands, but mainly in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of few in Nigeria; 73,000 in Cameroon (SIL 1982).

**Loo** (looo1238). Loo is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Bikwin group). Other names include Shú̹ŋó̹. It is spoken at Lo village and its hamlets, 30 km north of Karim Lamido town, and in Kaltungo LGA of Gombe State. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 8,000 (1992 estimate).

**Maghdi** (magh1238). Maghdi is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Bikwin group). Other names include Mághdì, Widala. Its speakers are a section of the Widala (a name that also applies to Kholok). The Atlas cites speaker figures of under 2,000 (1992).

**Mak** (makn1235). Mak is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Bikwin group). Other names include Panya, Panyam, Zoo. It is spoken 15 km north of Karim Lamido town. The name Panya comes from Poonya, a founding hero. Its dialects are Panya and Zo.

**Mambila** (no Glottocode). Mambila is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). Other names include Ju Nɔri, Mambilla, Mambere. It is spoken on the Mambila Plateau, and in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 18,000 (1952); 60,000 (SIL 1973); 10,000 in Cameroon. Almost every village has its own dialect, forming a chain, with centres at Bang, Dorofi, Gembu, Hainari, Kabri, Mayo Ndaga, Mbamnga, Tamien and Warwar. The New Testament appeared in the Gembu dialect in 1975.

**Mashi** (mash1269). Mashi is a language spoken in Takum LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as South Bantoid (Beboid). It is spoken in one village near Takum.

**Mbembe Tigong** (tigo1236). Mbembe Tigong is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Central Jukunoid; Jukun–Mbembe–Wurbo group). Other names include Tigong, Tigun, Tugun, Tukun, Tigum, Akonto. It is spoken mainly in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,900 in Nigeria (SIL 1973). It is a cluster including Ashuku and Nama.

**Mbɔŋnɔ** (mbon1253). Mbɔŋnɔ is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). Other names include Bungnu, Mbọngnọ, Kamkam, Kakaba, Bunu. It is spoken at Kakara town. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 800 (1952); about 3,000 (Blench and Connell 1999).

**Mingang Doso** (ming1254). Mingang Doso is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Jen group). Other names include Munga, ŋwai Mәngàn, Dosọ. It is spoken in one village and its hamlets 15 km east of Karim Lamido town.

**Mɔɔ** (mooo1239). Mɔɔ is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Bikwin group). Other names include ŋwaa Mɔ́ɔ̀, Gwomo, Gwom, Gwomu, Gomu.

**Mumuye** (nucl1240). Mumuye is a language spoken in Jalingo, Zing and Yorro LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group; Mumuye subgroup). The Atlas also names Mayo Belwa LGA (Adamawa State). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 103,000 (1952); 400,000 (UBS 1980). It is a cluster with a north-eastern group (the Zing group: Bajama, Jeng, Zing, Mang, Kwaji, Meeka, Yaa) in Zing, Yorro and Mayo Belwa, and a south-western group (Monkin and Kpugbong groups, including Lankoviri and Jalingo) in Jalingo LGA. Mark appeared in Zinna in 1938.

**Mvanɨp** (mvan1238). Mvanɨp is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). Other names include Magu. It is spoken in a single quarter of Zongo Ajiya town in the north-west of the Mambila Plateau. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 100 (Blench 1999).

**Naki** (naki1238). Naki is a language spoken in Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as South Bantoid (Beboid). The Atlas places one village (Belogo, also called Tosso 2) in Nigeria at about 6°57′N, 10°13′E; it is spoken mainly in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 3,000 in Cameroon (1976).

**Ndoola** (ndoo1241). Ndoola is a language spoken in Sardauna and Gashaka LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). Other names include Ndoro, Njoyamɛ. It is also spoken in one village in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,169 (1952); more than 15,000 (1999 estimate). It has at least two dialects.

**Ndunda** (ndun1251). Ndunda is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). It is spoken in the north-west of the Mambila Plateau. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 400 (Blench 1999).

**Nyam** (nyam1285). Nyam is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (Bole–Ngas group; Bole–Tangale group). It is spoken in a single village, Andami.

**Pangseng** (pang1286). Pangseng is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group; Mumuye subgroup). Its varieties are Pangseng, Komo and Jega.

**Piya–Kwonci** (piya1245). Piya–Kwonci is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as West Chadic (Bole–Ngas group; Bole group). Other names include Pia, Wurkum, Pitiko. It is spoken near Didango. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 2,500 (1977); Kwonci more than 4,000 (1990). It is a cluster of Piya and Kwonci.

**Rang** (rang1269). Rang is a language spoken in Zing LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Mumuye–Yendang group; Mumuye subgroup).

**Samba Daka** (samb1311). Samba Daka is a language spoken in Jalingo, Bali and Zing LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Dakoid). Other names include Chamba Daka, Chamba, Tchamba, Tsamba, Jama, Daka. The Atlas also names Ganye and Mayo Belwa LGAs (Adamawa State). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 66,000 (1952); more than 100,000 (1990). Its dialects include Samba Daka, Samba Jangani, Samba Nnakenyare and Samba of Mapeo, and may form a cluster with Lamja and Taram. Mark was published in 1933.

**Samba Leko** (samb1305). Samba Leko is a language spoken in Wukari and Takum LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Leko group). Other names include Chamba Leko, Samba Leeko, Sama, Leko, Suntai. The Atlas also names Ganye and Fufore LGAs (Adamawa State); it is spoken mainly in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 42,000 in all (SIL 1972); 50,000 (1971).

**Shoo–Minda–Nye** (shoo1247). Shoo–Minda–Nye is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Central Jukunoid; Wurbo cluster). Other names include Jinleri. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 10,000 (SIL). It is a cluster of Shoo, Minda and Nye, and may be related to Jessi, spoken between Lau and Lankoviri.

**Somyɛv** (somy1238). Somyɛv is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). Other names include Kila, Zuzun. It is the blacksmiths' dialect of Kila Yang village, 10 km west of Mayo Ndaga, and was formerly spoken in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 4 speakers (2006). It is on the point of extinction.

**Tep** (tepp1235). Tep is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). It is spoken in a single village and its hamlets on the Mambila Plateau. The Atlas cites speaker figures of under 4,000.

**Tha** (thaa1239). Tha is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Bikwin–Jen group). It is spoken at Joole Manga Dìdí village, and in Numan LGA of Adamawa State.

**Ugarә** (mesa1245). Ugarә is a language spoken in Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Southern Bantoid (Tivoid). Other names include Binangeli, Messaka. Quoting Cassetta and Cassetta (1994), the Atlas says that most speakers live in Cameroon and that those in Nigeria live mainly in Benue and Taraba states. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 5,000 (1994 estimate).

**Vute** (vute1244). Vute is a language spoken in Sardauna LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Northern Bantoid (Mambiloid). Other names include Bute, Mbute, Wute, Voute. It is spoken on the north-east of the Mambila Plateau, but mainly in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 1,000 or fewer in Nigeria; 30,000 in Cameroon (1985). It has at least six dialects.

**Wiyaa** (waja1259). Wiyaa is a language spoken in Bali LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Adamawa (Waja group). Other names include Wagga, Nyan Wịyáù, Waja. It is spoken mainly in Balanga and Kaltungo LGAs of Gombe State (Waja district). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 19,700 (1952); 50,000 (1992 estimate). Its dialects are Plain and Hills.

**Yamba** (yamb1251). Yamba is a language spoken in Sardauna and Gashaka LGAs of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Southern Bantoid (Grassfields; Nkambe cluster). Other names include Mbem. It is spoken in Antere and other border villages, but mainly in Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of few in Nigeria; 25,000 in Cameroon (SIL 1982).

**Yukuben** (yuku1243). Yukuben is a language spoken in Takum LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Jukunoid (Yukuben–Kutep). Other names include Nyikuben, Nyikobe, Ayikiben, Yikuben, Boritsu, Balaabe. It is also spoken in Furu-Awa subdivision, Cameroon. The Atlas cites speaker figures of 10,000 (1971); 1,000 in Cameroon (1976).

**Ligri** (ligr1238). Ligri is a language spoken in Karim Lamido LGA of Taraba State, according to Roger Blench's Atlas of Nigerian Languages (2020), which classifies it as Bantu (Jarawan; Jar cluster). The Atlas cites speaker figures of 800 (Ayuba estimate, 2008). It is the Taraba member of the Jar cluster, which is otherwise spoken in Plateau and Bauchi states.

## Research gaps

- **Nupe at Ibi.** The Atlas mentions 'small but well established Nupe communities in Ibi (Taraba State)'. Nupe is not yet a language record.
- **Glottocodes not matched.** Buru (Glottolog 'Buru-Angwe' merges it with Batu Angwe), Dirim ('Jirim'?), Joole, Gbaya, Mambila (a family in Glottolog), Kulung (Chadic), Dampar.
- **Current LGAs.** The Atlas names pre-1996 LGAs. Which present LGA (Lau, Ussa, Donga, Kurmi, Ardo Kola, Yorro, Gassol, Ibi) each language is in needs checking.
- **Adamawa LGAs named under Taraba.** Mayo Belwa, Ganye, Fufore, Yola and Numan are Adamawa LGAs; the Atlas lists them for Dong, Mumuye, Samba Daka, Samba Leko, Lamja and others.
- **Bali village.** The Atlas places the Bali language at 'Bali, a single village south of Jalingo' in 'Numan LGA'. The present LGA is not known.
- **Wannu and Wapan.** The archive's Wannu record (Glottolog wann1241, map point at Abinsi) is the Atlas's Abinsi; Wapan (Jukun of Wukari) is a separate record. The Atlas gives 'Wapan' as the Abinsi people's own name too.
