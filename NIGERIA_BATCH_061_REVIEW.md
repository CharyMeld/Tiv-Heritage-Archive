# Research batch 061 — Adamawa LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-01. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Adamawa. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **21 LGA descriptions**, filling fields that were empty.
- **20 headquarters towns**, created as places. Yola South reuses the existing Yola place.
- **Figures:** the 2006 population and area of each LGA, from Statoids.
- **New official source:** INEC's list of its Adamawa LGA offices, saved in the repo. It fixes Maiha's headquarters, and it supports Gulak (Madagali) and Gela (Mubi South).
- **Headquarters strength:** four are well documented (Demsa, Hong, Michika and Maiha). Sixteen rest on Statoids alone, because no official Adamawa table was found (listed as a gap).
- **Each description draws on records already in the archive** from batches 057b, 059 and 060: the LGA's peoples, its traditional state, and its heritage sites and festivals.
- **Not used from Wikipedia:**
  - implausible 2006 figures for Lamurde and Shelleng
  - '2016' population estimates
  - the mountain ranges placed in Yola North and Yola South
  - the language survey of displaced persons, which is not a census
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 21 descriptions

**Demsa** (91 words). Demsa is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Demsa (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 180,251 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,825 km² (Statoids). Demsa lies on the Benue (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bachama (Bwatiye), Bata, Bwazza, Ɓile, Mbula and Waka (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas). The state's festival table places Vunon (with prayers to the deity Nzeanzo), Mba-Pur, Jabba, Mba-Tambal and Mba-Zonhwo here.

**Fufore** (113 words). Fufore is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Fufore (according to Statoids). It had a population of 207,287 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,972 km² (Statoids). It had the largest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census. Fufore is the seat of the Fufore Emirate, a second-class traditional state created in December 2024 (Adamawa State Government; Daily Trust). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bachama, Bata, Fulani, Mumuye and Verre (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas). The state lists the Yadim and Sella Negis waterfalls, at Yadim village, among its tourist sites.

**Ganye** (99 words). Ganye is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Ganye (according to Statoids). It had a population of 164,087 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,888 km² (Statoids). According to Wikipedia, Jada and Toungo LGAs were carved out of Ganye, which it calls the headquarters of the Sama (Chamba) people. Ganye is the seat of the Gangwari Ganye, the first-class ruler of the Ganye Chiefdom. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Chamba, Fulani, Mumuye and Pere (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas).

**Girei** (99 words). Girei is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Girei (according to Statoids). It had a population of 129,995 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,848 km² (Statoids). Statoids spells it Girie. Girei lies on the Benue; according to Wikipedia the Fulani (Fulɓe) are the main people, with Bwatiye villages such as Greng and Labondo. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bata, Fulani, Mbula and Yungur (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas). INEC's office for the LGA is opposite the LGA secretariat.

**Gombi** (96 words). Gombi is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Gombi (according to Statoids). It had a population of 146,429 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,101 km² (Statoids). Gombi is the seat of the Gombi Chiefdom, a third-class traditional state created in December 2024 (Adamawa State Government; Daily Trust). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Ga'anda, Hwana, Lala and Ngwaba (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas). The state's festival table places the Kwayfa, Janda and Khombalta festivals at Ga'anda.

**Guyuk** (97 words). Guyuk is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Guyuk (according to Statoids). It had a population of 177,785 at the 2006 census and an area of about 757 km² (Statoids). Guyuk lies on the Numan–Biu road (Wikipedia), and the state lists limestone and gypsum among the LGA's minerals. It is the home of the Lunguda, whose Simalama festival marks the end of the rainy season; the Elephant House, of great significance to the Lunguda, is No. 18 on the National Commission for Museums and Monuments' list of proposed national monuments.

**Hong** (114 words). Hong is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Hong (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 169,126 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,626 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia calls Hong the capital of the Kilba (Holba), with Marghi, Kamwe and Fulani also present, and lists 12 wards. Hong is the seat of the Huba Chiefdom, a second-class traditional state created in December 2024 for the Huba (Kilba) people (Daily Trust; Adamawa State Government). The National Museum Hong is in the LGA secretariat complex (NCMM). The state's tables place the Kilba funeral festival Tiwa at Hong, and the Tapichima cold-water spring at Pella.

**Jada** (100 words). Jada is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Jada (according to Statoids). It had a population of 168,473 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,794 km² (Statoids). According to Wikipedia it was created from the old Ganye LGA. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Chamba, Fulani, Koma and Mumuye (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas). The state lists the Koma Hills, at Koma village, as the home of the historic Koma people, and the Klashe Shatin festival of the Chamba and Mumuye.

**Lamurde** (109 words). Lamurde is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Lamurde (according to Statoids). It had a population of 112,803 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,171 km² (Statoids). According to Wikipedia it was created from Numan LGA on 14 December 1990 and is inhabited mainly by the Bwatiye (Bachama) and Tsobo; Wikipedia's article on Numan calls Lamurde the spiritual or ancestral home of the Bwatiye, where the Hama Bachama has a second palace. Wikipedia gives an area of 2,098 km². The state's tables place the Wurokakai and Kwatte festivals of the Bachama here, and the Ruwan Zafi hot spring.

**Madagali** (128 words). Madagali is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Gulak (according to Statoids). It had a population of 134,827 at the 2006 census and an area of about 821 km² (Statoids). INEC's office for the LGA is also at Gulak. According to Wikipedia the LGA was created in 1991 and borders Cameroon. Gulak is the seat of the Madagali Chiefdom, a second-class traditional state created in December 2024 (Daily Trust; Adamawa State Government). The LGA holds the Sukur Cultural Landscape, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and Adamawa's only declared national monument (No. 5), with the NCMM's interpretation centre at Sukur. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Marghi and Sukur (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas).

**Maiha** (80 words). Maiha is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Maiha (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 111,215 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,273 km² (Statoids). Maiha borders Cameroon (Wikipedia) and is the seat of the Maiha Emirate, a third-class traditional state created in December 2024 (Daily Trust; Adamawa State Government). It is the home of the Nzanyi, whose initiation festivals Alalile and Wagurwa the state lists.

**Mayo Belwa** (129 words). Mayo Belwa is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Mayo Belwa (according to Statoids). It had a population of 153,129 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,768 km² (Statoids). According to a tradition recorded by Wikipedia, the town was founded by the Bata of the Mayo Ine valley as Gabalwa and took its present name after the forces of Lamido Lawal of Adamawa took it. A meteorite of about 5 kg fell in the town on 3 August 1974 (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani, Mumuye, Waka and Yandang (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas). The state lists the Gorobi rock formations and the Yandang festivals Phuki and Here-Yawetti.

**Michika** (95 words). Michika is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Michika (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 155,302 at the 2006 census and an area of about 967 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives the Kamwe form Mwe-cika, names the Kamwe as the main people and says the LGA was created in 1976; it borders Cameroon. Michika is the seat of the Michika Chiefdom, a second-class traditional state created in December 2024 (Daily Trust; Adamawa State Government). The state lists the Zhita and Yawle festivals of the Higgi (Kamwe).

**Mubi North** (102 words). Mubi North is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Mubi (according to Statoids). It had a population of 151,072 at the 2006 census and an area of about 903 km² (Statoids). INEC's office for the LGA is inside the LGA secretariat. Wikipedia places Adamawa State University and the Federal Polytechnic, Mubi, in the town. Mubi is the seat of the Mubi Emirate, which Wikipedia lists as a first-class emirate covering Mubi North and Mubi South. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fali (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas).

**Mubi South** (75 words). Mubi South is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Gella (according to Statoids). It had a population of 128,937 at the 2006 census and an area of about 414 km² (Statoids). INEC's office for the LGA is at Gela, opposite the district head's palace. It is part of the Mubi Emirate (Wikipedia). It is the home of the Gude, whose festivals Wangirwa and Vulma (at Gella) the state lists.

**Numan** (108 words). Numan is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Numan (according to Statoids). It had a population of 90,723 at the 2006 census and an area of about 905 km² (Statoids). Numan lies at the confluence of the Benue and Gongola rivers (Wikipedia). It is the seat of the Hama Bachama, the first-class ruler of the Bachama (Bwatiye), whose palace is at Numan; Lamurde LGA was created from Numan in 1990 (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Bachama and Kaan (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas). The state lists the Bachama festival Kadyaga here.

**Shelleng** (101 words). Shelleng is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Shelleng (according to Statoids). It had a population of 149,069 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,359 km² (Statoids). INEC's office for the LGA is on Old Local Government Secretariat Street in Shelleng town. The state lists Kiri Dam, at Kiri, among its tourist sites, and the Menjauli festival of the Kanakuru (Dera) and the Lamushi initiation of the Libbo. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Dera and Kaan (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas).

**Song** (110 words). Song is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Song (according to Statoids). It had a population of 192,697 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,256 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia lists among its districts Song, Dumne, Kilange, Gudu and Mboi. The Yungur Chiefdom, a third-class traditional state created in December 2024, has its headquarters at Dumne in the LGA (Adamawa State Government; Daily Trust). The Three Sisters Rock is No. 87 on the NCMM's list of proposed national monuments. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Mboi and Yungur (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas).

**Toungo** (105 words). Toungo is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Toungo (according to Statoids). It had a population of 52,040 at the 2006 census and an area of about 5,478 km² (Statoids). It is the largest LGA of the state by area and had the smallest population at the 2006 census (Statoids). Wikipedia's article on Ganye says Toungo was carved out of Ganye. The state lists the Makam Walls and the Sassa Waterfalls among its tourist sites. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Chamba and Mumuye (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas).

**Yola North** (82 words). Yola North is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Jimeta (according to Statoids). It had a population of 198,247 at the 2006 census and an area of about 718 km² (Statoids). Yola North includes Jimeta, part of the Yola metropolitan area (Wikipedia). The National Museum Yola and INEC's state office are in Jimeta. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Laka and Mumuye (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas).

**Yola South** (106 words). Yola South is a local government area of Adamawa State with its headquarters at Yola (according to Statoids). It had a population of 194,607 at the 2006 census and an area of about 113 km² (Statoids). Yola is the capital of Adamawa State and the seat of the Lamido of Adamawa, ruler of the Adamawa Emirate; the National Museum Fombina is in the Lamido's palace (NCMM; Adamawa State Planning Commission). It is the smallest LGA of the state by area (Statoids). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani, Mumuye and Verre (state festival and tourist tables, Wikipedia and Blench's Atlas).

## Research gaps

- **Adamawa: no official table of LGA headquarters.** Seventeen headquarters rest on Statoids alone (Wikipedia says only 'a town and LGA'); INEC names the secretariat town only for Maiha. An official Adamawa State list would settle them.
- **Adamawa: Lamurde area.** Statoids gives 1,171 km² and Wikipedia 2,098 km²; Statoids' figure is recorded.
- **Adamawa: LGA creation dates.** Only Michika (1976, Wikipedia), Lamurde (14 December 1990, from Numan) and Madagali (1991) are dated, and Jada and Toungo are said to come from Ganye without a date.
- **Adamawa: Shelleng festival name.** Wikipedia names a 'Mendamo festival' in Shelleng; the state's table has Menjauli (Kanakuru, Shelleng). Whether they are the same is not shown; only Menjauli is recorded.
