# Research batch 164 — Niger LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-07. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Niger. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **25 LGA descriptions** and **24 new headquarters towns**. Chanchaga uses the existing Minna place. New town slugs end in '-niger'.
- **Agreement on headquarters:**
  - Statoids, Wikipedia's LGA pages and INEC agree for 23 LGAs.
  - **Kontagora:** Statoids and INEC agree; Wikipedia's page is about the town and does not call it the LGA headquarters.
  - **Paikoro** (Paiko): Statoids and Wikipedia agree; INEC's address names no town.
  - INEC writes **Gawu Babangida** (Gurara) and **Sabon Wuse** (Tafa); both are kept as other names of the towns.
- **Muya / Munya:** the record keeps Muya (the Constitution and Statoids) and says that INEC and Wikipedia use Munya. Munya is already stored as a variant.
- **Each description** names the peoples linked to the LGA in batch 160b, and any ruler's seat, monument or festival from batches 162–163.
- **Figures:** 2006 population and area from Statoids.
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 25 descriptions

**Agaie** (82 words). Agaie is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Agaie (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 132,907 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,903 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says it is inhabited by the Nupe and that Agaie is an old town, founded about the fourteenth century. The archive links the Dibo, Gbagyi, Kakanda and Nupe peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Agwara** (72 words). Agwara is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Agwara (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 57,413 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,538 km² (Statoids). It had the smallest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census (Statoids). The archive links the Kambari people to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Bida** (103 words). Bida is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Bida (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 188,181 at the 2006 census and an area of about 51 km² (Statoids). Bida is the seat of the Etsu Nupe, ruler of the Bida Emirate, and the katamba of his palace is a declared national monument (Wikipedia; NCMM). Nupe Day is celebrated there each June. It had the smallest area of the state's LGAs (Statoids). The archive links the Gbagyi, Hausa and Nupe peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Borgu** (107 words). Borgu is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at New Bussa (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 171,965 at the 2006 census and an area of about 11,267 km² (Statoids). It was transferred from Kwara State to Niger State on 27 August 1991 and has the same extent as the Borgu Emirate (Wikipedia). Part of Kainji National Park, the Borgu Game Reserve, lies in it. It had the largest area of the state's LGAs (Statoids). The archive links the Busa, Kambari and Reshe peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Bosso** (69 words). Bosso is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Maikunkele (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 147,359 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,592 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names Gbagyi, Hausa and Nupe as its most common languages. The archive links the Gbagyi people to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Chanchaga** (82 words). Chanchaga is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Minna (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 201,429 at the 2006 census and an area of about 72 km² (Statoids). Minna, the state capital, occupies much of the LGA (Wikipedia), and the National Museum Minna is in the Federal Secretariat Complex there (NCMM). The archive links the Gbagyi and Kamuku peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Edati** (67 words). Edati is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Enagi (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 160,321 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,752 km² (Statoids). The Kaduna River divides it into two parts (Wikipedia). The archive links the Nupe people to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Gbako** (66 words). Gbako is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Lemu (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 127,466 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,753 km² (Statoids). The Kaduna River forms its western boundary (Wikipedia). The archive links the Nupe people to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Gurara** (91 words). Gurara is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Gawu (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). INEC writes the town as Gawu Babangida. It had a population of 90,974 at the 2006 census and an area of about 954 km² (Statoids). It adjoins the Federal Capital Territory; Wikipedia names the Gwari (Gbagyi) and Bassa as its major inhabitants, and the Gurara Waterfalls are in the LGA. The archive links the Gbagyi and Gwandara peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Katcha** (80 words). Katcha is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Katcha (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 122,176 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,681 km² (Statoids). It was carved out of the old Gbako LGA in 1981, and Badeggi is one of its major towns (Wikipedia). The archive links the Dibo and Nupe peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Kontagora** (87 words). Kontagora is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Kontagora (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 151,944 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,081 km² (Statoids). Kontagora is the seat of the Kontagora Emirate, whose ruler is styled the Sarkin Sudan (Voice of Nigeria); Wikipedia says Umaru Nagwamatse made it his capital in 1864. The archive links the Hausa and Kambari peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Lapai** (97 words). Lapai is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Lapai (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 110,127 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,051 km² (Statoids). It adjoins the Federal Capital Territory and is roughly coterminous with the Lapai Emirate; Wikipedia names Nupe Day and Gwari Day among its festivals. The Dabo Mosque at Gulu is a proposed national monument (NCMM). The archive links the Dibo, Gbagyi, Kakanda and Nupe peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Lavun** (66 words). Lavun is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Kutigi (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 209,917 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,835 km² (Statoids). The Kaduna River forms its eastern border (Wikipedia). The archive links the Nupe people to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Magama** (60 words). Magama is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Nasko (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 181,653 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,107 km² (Statoids). The archive links the Hun-Saare and Kambari peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Mariga** (82 words). Mariga is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Bangi (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 199,430 at the 2006 census and an area of about 5,552 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia names the Kamuku as its principal inhabitants and says several endangered Kamuku languages, Hausa and Kambari languages are spoken there. The archive links the Kambari, Kamuku and Nupe peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Mashegu** (77 words). Mashegu is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Mashegu (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 215,022 at the 2006 census and an area of about 9,182 km² (Statoids). It is bounded by the Niger in the west and the Kaduna River in the north-east (Wikipedia). The archive links the Kambari and Nupe peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Mokwa** (108 words). Mokwa is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Mokwa (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 244,937 at the 2006 census and an area of about 4,338 km² (Statoids). Its long southern border is the Niger, from Lake Jebba to beyond the Kaduna confluence, and Mokwa is a market town where traders from the south buy food from northern growers (Wikipedia). It had the largest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census (Statoids). The archive links the Gbagyi, Hausa, Nupe and Yoruba peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Muya** (95 words). Muya is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Sarkin Pawa (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 103,651 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,176 km² (Statoids). INEC and Wikipedia call it Munya; the archive keeps Muya, the name in the First Schedule to the 1999 Constitution and in Statoids, with Munya as a variant. Wikipedia names the Gbari as its main inhabitants. The archive links the Adara people to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Paikoro** (79 words). Paikoro is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Paiko (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). INEC lists its LGA office at an address naming no town. It had a population of 158,086 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,066 km² (Statoids). Paiko lies about 25 km south-east of Minna (Wikipedia). The archive links the Adara and Gbagyi peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Rafi** (78 words). Rafi is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Kagara (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 181,929 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,680 km² (Statoids). Tegina and Pandogari are among its towns, and the Kaduna River forms its southern border (Wikipedia). The archive links the Gbagyi, Hùngwəryə, Kamuku and Pangu peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Rijau** (60 words). Rijau is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Rijau (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 176,053 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,196 km² (Statoids). The archive links the Hun-Saare and Kambari peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Shiroro** (65 words). Shiroro is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Kuta (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 235,404 at the 2006 census and an area of about 5,015 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia gives Gbagyi as its major language. The archive links the Gbagyi people to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Suleja** (109 words). Suleja is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Suleja (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 216,578 at the 2006 census and an area of about 119 km² (Statoids). Suleja, first named Abuja, was founded in the early 19th century by Hausa refugees from Zazzau and is the seat of the Suleja Emirate; the Ladi Kwali Pottery Centre was established there by Michael Cardew in 1950 (Wikipedia). Zuma Rock, at Madalla, is a proposed national monument (NCMM). The archive links the Gbagyi, Gwandara, Hausa and Koro peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Tafa** (71 words). Tafa is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Wuse (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). INEC writes the town as Sabon Wuse. It had a population of 83,544 at the 2006 census and an area of about 222 km² (Statoids). It adjoins the Federal Capital Territory (Wikipedia). The archive links the Gbagyi people to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

**Wushishi** (97 words). Wushishi is a local government area of Niger State with its headquarters at Wushishi (according to Statoids, Wikipedia and INEC). It had a population of 81,783 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,879 km² (Statoids). Zungeru, the capital of the Protectorate of Northern Nigeria from 1902 to 1916, is in the LGA; its colonial Government House ruins and the site of Mai Jimina's house at Wushishi are declared national monuments (Wikipedia; NCMM). The archive links the Gbagyi and Nupe peoples to it (batch 160b: Blench's Atlas, the federal state profile and Wikipedia).

## Research gaps

- **Niger: headquarters of Kontagora and Paikoro.** Wikipedia's Kontagora page is about the town and does not call it the LGA headquarters; INEC's Paikoro address names no town. Statoids gives Kontagora and Paiko; a state government list would settle both.
- **Niger: LGA descriptions.** Several Wikipedia LGA pages (Magama, Rijau, Agwara) give little beyond census figures and climate; the history and peoples of those LGAs need better sources.
