# Research batch 109 — Zamfara LGA profiles

Researched 2026-10-02. Completes the LGA layer of Phase 3 for Zamfara. Created in review; published only after your approval.

## What it adds

- **14 LGA descriptions** and **13 headquarters towns** (Gusau reuses the existing place).
- **Figures:** the 2006 population and area of each LGA, from Statoids.
- **Sources for the headquarters:** Statoids, INEC (Bakura) and Wikipedia (Birnin Magaji; Tsafe, which Wikipedia writes 'Chafe').
- The pages stay under 300 words, so they are noindex and the sitemap is unchanged.

## The 14 descriptions

**Anka** (75 words). Anka is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Anka (according to Statoids). It had a population of 142,280 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,746 km² (Statoids). Anka, in the west of the state, became the later capital of the Zamfara kingdom (Wikipedia, Zamfara State), and is the seat of the Anka Emirate. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Bakura** (60 words). Bakura is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Bakura (according to Statoids and INEC). It had a population of 186,905 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,366 km² (Statoids). INEC's office is behind the LGA secretariat in Bakura. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Birnin Magaji/Kiyaw** (83 words). Birnin Magaji/Kiyaw is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Birnin Magaji (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 178,619 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,188 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia says the headquarters, Birnin Magaji (or Magare), is in the north of the LGA, which also takes its name from the town of Kiyaw (Kiawa) to the south. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani.

**Bukkuyum** (57 words). Bukkuyum is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Bukkuyum (according to Statoids). It had a population of 211,633 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,214 km² (Statoids). INEC and Wikipedia also write Bukuyum and Bukwium. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Bungudu** (59 words). Bungudu is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Bungudu (according to Statoids). It had a population of 257,917 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,293 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places the town on the Sokoto River. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani and Hausa.

**Gummi** (49 words). Gummi is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Gummi (according to Statoids). It had a population of 204,539 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,610 km² (Statoids). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Gusau** (87 words). Gusau is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Gusau (according to Statoids). It had a population of 383,162 at the 2006 census and an area of about 3,364 km² (Statoids). Gusau is the capital of Zamfara State and the seat of the Gusau Emirate, whose new emir was appointed in July 2025 (Wikipedia; Channels TV); it had the largest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani.

**Kaura Namoda** (81 words). Kaura Namoda is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Kaura Namoda (according to Statoids). It had a population of 281,367 at the 2006 census and an area of about 868 km² (Statoids). Kaura Namoda was founded in 1807 by Muhammadu Namoda, whose tomb is a proposed national monument; it is home to the Federal Polytechnic, Kaura-Namoda, and was a railway terminus (Wikipedia; NCMM). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Maradun** (49 words). Maradun is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Maradun (according to Statoids). It had a population of 210,852 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,728 km² (Statoids). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Fulani.

**Maru** (58 words). Maru is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Maru (according to Statoids). It had a population of 291,900 at the 2006 census and an area of about 6,654 km² (Statoids). It had the largest area of the state's LGAs. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Shinkafi** (82 words). Shinkafi is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Shinkafi (according to Statoids). It had a population of 135,649 at the 2006 census and an area of about 674 km² (Statoids). Shinkafi borders Sokoto State and the Republic of Niger, and is the seat of the Shinkafi Emirate, created in 2000; it had the smallest population of the state's LGAs at the 2006 census (Wikipedia). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Talata Mafara** (68 words). Talata Mafara is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Talata Mafara (according to Statoids). It had a population of 215,178 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,430 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia places the town on the southern edge of the irrigation project fed by the Bakolori Dam. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Tsafe** (57 words). Tsafe is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Tsafe (according to Statoids and Wikipedia). It had a population of 266,008 at the 2006 census and an area of about 1,698 km² (Statoids). Wikipedia also writes Chafe and Tsyahe. The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.

**Zurmi** (70 words). Zurmi is a local government area of Zamfara State with its headquarters at Zurmi (according to Statoids). It had a population of 293,837 at the 2006 census and an area of about 2,834 km² (Statoids). Zurmi borders Niger and Katsina State; the giant tombs of the Zamfara rulers there are a proposed national monument (Wikipedia; NCMM). The peoples recorded for the LGA in this archive include the Hausa.
