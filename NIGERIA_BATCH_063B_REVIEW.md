## The people records

**Shuwa Arabs.** The Shuwa Arabs are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by the federal government's state profile and by Wikipedia. Other names include Shuwa. Wikipedia describes them as living throughout the north and centre of the state, alongside the Kanuri, and as mainly descendants of Arab peoples. Their language is Shuwa Arabic; Blench's Atlas notes that they range widely across Borno and Yobe on transhumance, that the name Shuwa is regarded as pejorative, at least in Chad, and that the Boko Haram insurgency drove many to leave Nigeria. The sources place them in Bama, Dikwa, Konduga, Ngala and Kala/Balge LGAs.

**Bura.** The Bura are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by the federal government's state profile and by Wikipedia. Other names include Babur. Blench's Atlas describes the Bura–Pabir language as one language of two peoples, the Bura and the Pabir, with two dialects, Bura Pela (Hill Bura) and Bura Hyil Hawul (Plains Bura). The federal profile names both 'Babur' and 'Bura' among the peoples of the southern LGAs; how 'Babur' relates to the Pabir is not stated. The sources place them in Biu, Hawul and Kwaya Kusar LGAs.

**Kibaku.** The Kibaku are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by the federal government's state profile and by Wikipedia. Other names include Chibok, Kyibaku, Cíbɔ̀k, Kikuk. The federal profile calls them the Chibok and says they inhabit Chibok LGA. Their language, Cibak (Kibaku), is placed by Blench's Atlas south of Damboa town. The sources place them in Chibok, Damboa and Askira/Uba LGAs.

**Mandara.** The Mandara are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by the federal government's state profile and by Wikipedia. Other names include Wandala. Wikipedia names them among the peoples of the centre of the state, and the federal profile among its smaller groups. Their language, Wandala, is a vehicular language in this part of Nigeria and Cameroon, according to Blench's Atlas. The sources place them in Bama, Gwoza and Konduga LGAs.

**Malgwa.** The Malgwa are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by the federal government's state profile. Other names include Gamergu, Məlgwa. The federal profile names them as the Gamergu. Blench's Atlas gives Məlgwa as their own name and Gamergu (Gamargu, Malgo) as other names, and places Malgwa, a member of the Wandala cluster, in Damboa, Gwoza and Konduga LGAs. The sources place them in Damboa LGA.

**Buduma.** The Buduma are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by Wikipedia. Other names include Yedina. Wikipedia places them in the extreme north-east of the state. Blench's Atlas places their language, Yedina, on the islands of Lake Chad, spoken mostly in Chad.

**Kanembu.** The Kanembu are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them in the extreme north-east of the state. Blench's Atlas describes them as a separate people speaking Kanuri, placed in the Borno LGAs on the edge of Lake Chad, and also in Niger, Cameroon and Chad.

**Afade.** The Afade are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by Wikipedia. Wikipedia places them in the extreme north-east of the state. Blench's Atlas places their language, Afaɗə (also called Kotoko), in Ngala LGA and in Cameroon. The sources place them in Kala/Balge LGA.

**Dghwede.** The Dghwede are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by Wikipedia. Wikipedia names them among the peoples of the centre of the state. The sources place them in Gwoza LGA.

**Glavda.** The Glavda are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by Wikipedia. Wikipedia names them among the peoples of the centre of the state; their language is also spoken in Cameroon. The sources place them in Gwoza LGA.

**Guduf.** The Guduf are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by the federal government's state profile and by Wikipedia. Other names include Kədupaxa. Wikipedia names them among the peoples of the centre of the state, and the federal profile among its smaller groups. Their language belongs to the Guduf–Cikide cluster of the mountains east of Gwoza town. The sources place them in Gwoza LGA.

**Lamang.** The Lamang are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by Wikipedia. Other names include Laamang. Wikipedia names them among the peoples of the centre of the state. The sources place them in Gwoza LGA.

**Mafa.** The Mafa are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by Wikipedia. Other names include Matakam. Wikipedia names them among the peoples of the centre and south of the state. Blench's Atlas places the Mafa language in Gwoza LGA and mainly in Cameroon; despite the name, the Mafa are not placed in Mafa LGA by the Atlas. The sources place them in Gwoza LGA.

**Tera.** The Tera are a people of Borno State, named among the state's peoples by the federal government's state profile. The federal profile names them among the smaller peoples of the southern LGAs (Biu, Hawul, Kwaya Kusar, Bayo and Shani). Blench's Atlas places the Tera cluster in Biu and Bayo LGAs, and also in Gombe State. The sources place them in Biu, Bayo and Kwaya Kusar LGAs.

## Research gaps

- **Borno: LGAs with no people linked.** Abadam, Gubio, Mafa, Magumeri, Marte and Mobbar: no source read names their peoples. The federal profile says the Kanuri live in 'quite a number of LGAs', and Wikipedia places the Kanembu in the extreme north-east, but neither names these LGAs. A current source (e.g. the state government's LGA pages) is needed.
- **Borno: Mafa outside Gwoza.** Wikipedia's language table lists Mafa in Bama, Konduga, Maiduguri and Monguno; the Atlas places the Mafa language only in Gwoza (mainly Cameroon). The entries may confuse the language with Mafa LGA; only Gwoza is linked.
- **Borno: Pabir and Babur.** The Atlas names two peoples, the Bura and the Pabir, speaking Bura–Pabir; Wikipedia writes 'Babur/Bura'; the federal profile lists 'Babur' and 'Bura' separately. A Pabir record and the exact relation of Babur to Pabir need a source.
- **Borno: groups not recorded.** Waja (Wikipedia: extreme south; the Atlas places Waja in Gombe only), Ngoshe (federal profile; a place name used for Glavda and Gvoko varieties), Lapang (Wikipedia, History; probably the Laamang), Baggara Arabs (Wikipedia, Jere; the Atlas places Baggara Arabic in Yobe) and the speakers of Putai, Hdi, Gvoko, Cinene, Jilbe and Teda.
- **Borno: Hausa in 'Askira'.** The federal profile says 'Askira', linked to Askira/Uba LGA.
